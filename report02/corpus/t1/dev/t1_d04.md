## SECTION 1. THE PARTNERSHIP

### 1.1 Organization

Varrick Onslade Long/Short Equity Fund, L.P. (the "Partnership") is a limited partnership organized on October 14, 2025 under the Delaware Revised Uniform Limited Partnership Act (the "Delaware Act"). The Partnership commenced investment operations on January 2, 2026. Its sole general partner is Varrick Onslade GP, LLC, a Delaware limited liability company (the "General Partner"), which has delegated investment discretion to Varrick Onslade Capital Management, LLC, a Delaware limited liability company (the "Investment Manager").

### 1.2 Structure

The Partnership is an open-end, stand-alone fund: it does not invest through a master fund, no feeder fund invests in it, and Limited Partners may redeem Interests periodically as described in Section 6. The Investment Manager also manages Varrick Onslade Long/Short Equity Offshore Fund, Ltd., a Cayman Islands exempted company (the "Offshore Fund"), which follows the same strategy and trades alongside the Partnership on a pari passu basis under the allocation policy in Section 11.1. The Offshore Fund has separate assets, liabilities and investors and holds no interest in the Partnership.

### 1.3 Term

The Partnership has no fixed term. It will continue until dissolved upon (a) a determination by the General Partner, on not less than 90 days' written notice to the Limited Partners; (b) the withdrawal, bankruptcy or dissolution of the General Partner, unless a majority in interest of the Limited Partners elects within 90 days to continue the Partnership with a successor general partner; or (c) a decree of judicial dissolution under the Delaware Act.

### 1.4 Classes of Interests

The Partnership offers Class A Interests and Class B Interests, which participate pro rata in a single portfolio and differ only as to the Management Fee, the Incentive Allocation, the minimum initial investment, the lock-up and the exchange right in Section 5.4. Interests are offered as of the first Business Day of each calendar month (each, a "Subscription Date"). "Business Day" means a day on which banks in New York City and the New York Stock Exchange are open.

## SECTION 2. INVESTMENT PROGRAM

### 2.1 Objective and Strategy

The Partnership seeks to compound capital over a full market cycle with lower volatility than the U.S. equity market by taking long and short positions in U.S.-listed companies with market capitalizations between $750 million and $15 billion at the time of investment. Long positions are concentrated in companies with durable competitive positions and improving returns on invested capital. Short positions target deteriorating fundamentals, aggressive revenue recognition, unsustainable leverage or valuations that discount improbable outcomes, supplemented by sector exchange-traded funds and index futures used to adjust market exposure.

### 2.2 Exposure, Leverage and Derivatives

The Partnership generally holds 35 to 55 long and 50 to 90 short positions, with gross exposure of 150% to 250% and net exposure of 15% to 60% of net asset value. At the time of investment no long position may exceed 8%, and no short position 3%, of net asset value. The Partnership borrows on margin from its Prime Brokers and may use options, total return swaps and index futures, counted toward exposure on a delta-adjusted basis. These guidelines may be changed without notice.

## SECTION 4. FEES AND EXPENSES

### 4.1 Management Fee

The Partnership pays the Investment Manager a management fee (the "Management Fee") quarterly in advance, as of the first Business Day of each calendar quarter, based on the net asset value of each Limited Partner's capital account as of that day, before accrual of the Incentive Allocation. The Management Fee is charged at an annual rate of 1.75% (0.4375% per quarter) for Class A Interests and 1.25% (0.3125% per quarter) for Class B Interests. Contributions accepted during a quarter are charged pro rata, and the unearned portion is credited on any redemption other than at a quarter end. The Management Fee is not reduced by fees the Investment Manager earns from the Offshore Fund or its managed accounts.

### 4.2 Incentive Allocation

As of the end of each Performance Period, the General Partner is allocated from each Limited Partner's capital account an amount (the "Incentive Allocation") equal to 20% of the Net Profits allocated to that capital account for Class A Interests and 17.5% of such Net Profits for Class B Interests, in each case only to the extent those Net Profits exceed the Loss Carryforward Amount. "Performance Period" means each fiscal year and, for any amount redeemed other than at year end, the period ending on its Redemption Date. Net Profits are determined after the Management Fee and all Partnership expenses and include unrealized gains. The Incentive Allocation is not subject to any hurdle rate or preferred return, and it is not measured against any market index. It is calculated separately for each capital account.

### 4.3 Loss Carryforward

If Net Losses are allocated to a capital account for any Performance Period, no Incentive Allocation will be made from that capital account until the Net Profits subsequently allocated to it exceed those unrecovered Net Losses (the "Loss Carryforward Amount"). The Loss Carryforward Amount operates as a high-water mark for each capital account and is not reset at the end of any Performance Period or after any period of time; it is reduced only by subsequent Net Profits and, proportionately, upon a partial redemption.

### 4.4 Fee Waivers

Interests held by the General Partner, the Investment Manager and their principals, employees and immediate family members bear no Management Fee or Incentive Allocation. The General Partner and the Investment Manager may waive, reduce or rebate either for any Limited Partner, including by side letter under Section 9.1, without notice to or consent of other Limited Partners. A reduction affects only the Limited Partner to which it is granted.

### 4.5 Expenses

The Partnership bears its investment expenses (commissions, stock borrow charges, margin interest and custody fees) and its operating expenses (administration, audit, tax, legal and regulatory filing costs). Operating expenses, other than investment and extraordinary expenses, are capped at 0.25% of average net asset value per fiscal year, with any excess borne by the Investment Manager. Organizational expenses of approximately $340,000 are amortized over 60 months for purposes of net asset value, a departure from U.S. generally accepted accounting principles ("GAAP"). The Investment Manager bears its own overhead.

Class B Interests may not be exchanged for Class A Interests.

## SECTION 6. REDEMPTIONS

### 6.1 Redemption Dates

Subject to Section 6.3 and the rest of this Section 6, a Limited Partner may redeem all or part of its capital account as of the last Business Day of each calendar quarter (each, a "Redemption Date"). Partial redemptions must be at least $250,000, and a request that would reduce a capital account below $500,000 may be treated as a request for full redemption.

### 6.2 Notice of Redemption

A Limited Partner must deliver an irrevocable written redemption request to the Administrator, with a copy to the General Partner, at least 60 days before the applicable Redemption Date. A late request is applied to the next Redemption Date. The General Partner may waive or shorten the notice requirement in its sole discretion, generally or in particular cases.

### 6.3 Lock-Up

Class A Interests are subject to a soft lock-up of 12 months and Class B Interests are subject to a hard lock-up of 24 months, in each case measured separately for each Capital Contribution from the Subscription Date on which it was accepted.

A Class A Limited Partner may redeem a Capital Contribution, and the appreciation attributable to it, as of a Redemption Date falling before the first anniversary of its Subscription Date, subject to a charge of 3% of the amount redeemed (the "Early Redemption Charge"). The Early Redemption Charge is retained by the Partnership for the benefit of the remaining Limited Partners.

No Class B Capital Contribution, or appreciation attributable to it, may be redeemed as of a Redemption Date falling before the second anniversary of its Subscription Date, and no charge or payment permits earlier redemption, except where the General Partner determines that continued ownership would be unlawful for the Limited Partner. Redemptions are applied first to amounts no longer subject to a lock-up.

### 6.4 Payment of Redemption Proceeds

Proceeds are generally paid in cash within 30 days after the Redemption Date. On a redemption of 90% or more of a capital account, the Partnership may retain up to 10% of the proceeds until completion of the audit for the fiscal year that includes the Redemption Date and will pay the balance, as adjusted and without interest, within 30 days after the audit is completed.

### 6.5 Fund-Level Gate

If aggregate redemption requests for any Redemption Date, including requests carried forward from earlier Redemption Dates, exceed 20% of the net asset value of the Partnership as of that Redemption Date, determined before giving effect to redemptions, the General Partner may reduce each request pro rata so that the aggregate amount redeemed equals 20% of net asset value. The unsatisfied portion of each request is carried forward to the next Redemption Date, where it is redeemed in priority to requests first submitted for that date and remains subject to this Section 6.5. The gate applies to the Partnership as a whole and not separately to any class or Limited Partner. Amounts carried forward continue to bear profits, losses and the Management Fee until redeemed.

### 6.6 Suspension and Compulsory Redemption

The General Partner may suspend the calculation of net asset value, redemptions or the payment of proceeds while a principal exchange is closed, while an emergency makes the disposal or valuation of investments impracticable, or after it has determined to dissolve the Partnership. It may also compel the redemption of any Interest on 10 days' written notice, without any Early Redemption Charge, where continued participation could cause adverse legal, tax or regulatory consequences.

## SECTION 10. CERTAIN RISK FACTORS

An investment in the Partnership involves significant risks, including those summarized below. This summary is not complete.

### 10.1 Market, Short Selling and Leverage Risk

Long and short positions may lose value simultaneously, and hedges may fail when correlations rise. Losses on short positions are theoretically unlimited, and borrowed securities may be recalled, forcing the Partnership to cover at unfavorable prices. Leverage magnifies losses, and a Prime Broker may raise margin requirements or withdraw financing on short notice. Rehypothecated assets may be unrecoverable if a Prime Broker becomes insolvent.

### 10.2 Dependence on the Chief Investment Officer; No Key Person Provision

The success of the Partnership depends substantially on Ms. Oakhurst, who has sole portfolio management authority. The Partnership Agreement does not contain a key person provision. The death, disability, departure or reduced involvement of Ms. Oakhurst would not by itself suspend the Partnership's investment activities, give rise to any special redemption right or require notice to Limited Partners, and a Limited Partner wishing to redeem in that event would be limited to the ordinary terms of Section 6. A Limited Partner may have negotiated notification or redemption rights on such an event by side letter; those rights are not available to other Limited Partners.

### 10.3 Limited Liquidity

Interests cannot be transferred without the General Partner's consent, and redemptions are available only quarterly, subject to the lock-up, the notice period, the Fund-Level Gate, the holdback and suspension. A gated request remains exposed to the Partnership's performance until paid.

### 10.4 Preferential Terms for Certain Investors

Limited Partners with side letters may pay lower fees, redeem on shorter notice or without a lock-up, or receive additional information. A Limited Partner with shorter notice may redeem ahead of others in a period of poor performance, leaving the remaining Limited Partners to bear the costs of the resulting liquidations and a larger share of any gate.

### 10.5 Incentive Allocation

The Incentive Allocation may encourage riskier investments than would otherwise be made. It is calculated on unrealized as well as realized gains and, because there is no hurdle, applies from the first dollar of Net Profits once any Loss Carryforward Amount is recovered.
