## SECTION 1. THE FUND

### 1.1 Organization

Skelmorlie Ridge Multi-Strategy Fund, L.P. (the "Fund") is a limited partnership organized under the Delaware Revised Uniform Limited Partnership Act (the "Delaware Act") on November 18, 2024. The Fund commenced investment operations on April 1, 2025. Its general partner is Skelmorlie Ridge GP, LLC, a Delaware limited liability company (the "General Partner"). The General Partner has delegated investment discretion to Skelmorlie Ridge Capital Management, LP (the "Investment Manager"), a Delaware limited partnership registered with the SEC as an investment adviser under the Investment Advisers Act of 1940 (the "Advisers Act") and with the CFTC as a commodity pool operator, under an investment management agreement dated as of April 1, 2025. The General Partner and the Investment Manager are controlled by Orla Penhallow and have their principal offices in Stamford, Connecticut.

The Fund offers limited partnership interests ("Interests") in three classes: Class A Interests, Class B Interests and Founders Class Interests (each, a "Class"). Investors admitted to the Fund are "Limited Partners". The Fund maintains a separate capital account (a "Capital Account") for each Class held by each Limited Partner and tracks each capital contribution separately for purposes of the lock-up.

### 1.2 Structure and Term

The Fund is an open-end fund that offers Interests monthly and permits Limited Partners to redeem quarterly, as described in Sections 5 and 6. The Fund has no fixed term. It will continue until dissolved under the Partnership Agreement, which provides for dissolution upon a determination of the General Partner, the withdrawal or bankruptcy of the General Partner (unless the Limited Partners elect to continue the Fund with a successor) or a decree of judicial dissolution under Section 17-802 of the Delaware Act.

The Fund is a stand-alone fund. It is not a feeder fund and does not invest through a master fund. The Investment Manager also manages Skelmorlie Ridge Multi-Strategy Offshore Fund, Ltd., a Cayman Islands exempted company offered to non-U.S. persons and U.S. tax-exempt investors (the "Parallel Fund"). The Parallel Fund invests side by side with the Fund, not through it, and neither vehicle holds any interest in the other. Shares of the Parallel Fund are offered by a separate memorandum.

### 1.3 Classes of Interests

Class A Interests and Class B Interests are offered continuously. Founders Class Interests are offered only until the Founders Class Termination Date (Section 5.4). The Classes differ in their Management Fee rates, Incentive Allocation rates, lock-up periods, early redemption charges, redemption notice periods and minimum initial investments; Founders Class Interests bear reduced fees and allocations in exchange for a longer lock-up. The Management Fee rate, Incentive Allocation rate, lock-up period and redemption notice period for each Class are as set forth in the table in Section 2.3. In all other respects the Classes participate pro rata in the Fund's investments, gains, losses, Pass-Through Expenses and Fund Expenses. Interests of one Class may be exchanged for another Class only with the consent of the General Partner, and no exchange into Founders Class Interests is permitted after the Founders Class Termination Date.

## SECTION 3. INVESTMENT PROGRAM

### 3.1 Objective and Platform

The Fund seeks consistent absolute returns with low correlation to equity and credit markets. The Investment Manager allocates capital among portfolio management teams employed by it, each running a discrete sub-portfolio (a "Sub-Portfolio") within centrally set risk limits. As of June 1, 2026, it employed 27 teams across fundamental equity, event-driven, credit, fixed income relative value, quantitative, and macro and commodities strategies, any of which it may change without notice.

### 3.2 Risk Management

The Chief Risk Officer, Imogen Castellow, reports to the risk committee and not to any portfolio manager. Each Sub-Portfolio operates under exposure, concentration, liquidity and loss limits; a Sub-Portfolio whose loss from peak allocated capital reaches a threshold set by the risk committee has its capital reduced and, at a second threshold, is ordinarily closed. The Fund's gross notional exposure is expected ordinarily to range between four and eight times its NAV, principally because of fixed income relative value positions, and may exceed that range.

## SECTION 4. MANAGEMENT FEE, INCENTIVE ALLOCATION AND EXPENSES

### 4.1 Management Fee

The Fund pays the Investment Manager a management fee (the "Management Fee") in respect of each Capital Account at the annual rate for the relevant Class as set forth in the table in Section 2.3. The Management Fee is calculated on the NAV of each Capital Account as of the first Business Day of each calendar month, after giving effect to contributions made as of that day and before reduction for any accrued Incentive Allocation, and is payable monthly in advance. It is prorated for partial months and debited against the Capital Account on which it is calculated, so that each Class bears only its own rate. No Management Fee is charged on Capital Accounts of the General Partner, the Investment Manager or their principals and employees.

### 4.2 Incentive Allocation

As of the end of each fiscal year and as of any Redemption Date in respect of the amount redeemed (each, an "Allocation Date"), the Fund reallocates from each Capital Account to a special capital account of the General Partner (the "Special Account") an amount equal to the percentage for the relevant Class as set forth in the table in Section 2.3 of the Net Profits allocated to that Capital Account since the preceding Allocation Date (the "Incentive Allocation"), to the extent those Net Profits exceed the Loss Carryforward. "Net Profits" and "Net Losses" mean the net increase or decrease in the NAV of a Capital Account for a period, including unrealized gains and losses, after the Management Fee, Pass-Through Expenses and Fund Expenses and disregarding contributions and redemptions. The General Partner may waive or reduce the Incentive Allocation for principals, employees and affiliates.

### 4.3 Loss Carryforward

The Incentive Allocation is subject to a loss carryforward that functions as a high water mark (the "Loss Carryforward"). If a Capital Account is allocated Net Losses, no Incentive Allocation is made from it until those Net Losses have been fully recovered. The Loss Carryforward is maintained separately for each Capital Account, is cumulative, and does not reset or expire with the passage of time or at the end of any fiscal year. On a partial redemption, the Loss Carryforward is reduced in the same proportion as the NAV of the Capital Account. A permitted transferee succeeds to the Loss Carryforward attributable to the Interests transferred.

### 4.4 No Hurdle or Preferred Return

The Incentive Allocation is not subject to any hurdle rate or preferred return, and it is not measured against any index or reference rate. It applies to all Net Profits in excess of the Loss Carryforward, including Net Profits attributable to interest on cash balances and general market appreciation.

### 4.5 Pass-Through Expenses

In addition to the Management Fee and the Incentive Allocation, the Fund bears the costs the Investment Manager incurs in operating the investment program ("Pass-Through Expenses"), including the compensation of investment and risk personnel (including bonuses calculated by reference to the net trading profits of individual Sub-Portfolios), recruiting and sign-on payments, market data, technology and related occupancy costs. Pass-Through Expenses are allocated between the Fund and the Parallel Fund pro rata to average net assets and borne by all Classes pro rata. They are not capped, are not offset against the Management Fee, and are payable whether or not the Fund is profitable. For the period from April 1, 2025 through December 31, 2025, Pass-Through Expenses borne by the Fund were approximately 2.7% of average NAV on an annualized basis. Future Pass-Through Expenses may be materially higher or lower.

### 4.6 Organizational Expenses and Fund Expenses

The Fund's share of organizational and initial offering expenses was approximately $760,000, which the Fund amortizes over 60 months for purposes of calculating NAV, a departure from U.S. generally accepted accounting principles ("GAAP") that may result in a qualified audit opinion. The Fund also bears its operating expenses ("Fund Expenses"), including brokerage, financing, custody, administration, audit, legal and regulatory filing costs, taxes and indemnification obligations.

## SECTION 6. REDEMPTIONS AND TRANSFERS

### 6.1 Redemption Dates and Lock-Up

Subject to the lock-up, notice and gate provisions of this Section 6, a Limited Partner may redeem all or part of a Capital Account as of the last Business Day of each calendar quarter (each, a "Redemption Date"). Each capital contribution is subject to the lock-up period for its Class, measured from its Subscription Date, as set forth in the table in Section 2.3, and redemptions are applied to contributions in the order made. Where that table designates a lock-up as soft, a Limited Partner may redeem before expiry subject to the early redemption charge as set forth in the table in Section 2.3, which is retained by the Fund for the benefit of the remaining Limited Partners. Where it designates a lock-up as hard, no redemption is permitted before expiry except following a Key Person Event or with the consent of the General Partner in extraordinary circumstances. After expiry, a contribution may be redeemed as of any Redemption Date.

### 6.2 Redemption Notice

A Limited Partner must give written notice of redemption to the Administrator at least the number of days before the relevant Redemption Date as set forth in the table in Section 2.3. Notice periods are counted in calendar days, and a late notice is treated as a notice for the next Redemption Date. A redemption notice is irrevocable unless the General Partner consents to its withdrawal. The General Partner may waive or shorten the notice period in its discretion.

### 6.3 Investor-Level Gate

Redemptions are subject to the investor-level gate described in the table in Section 2.2 (the "Investor-Level Gate"). The Investor-Level Gate applies separately to each Capital Account and is not affected by the requests of other Limited Partners. Any portion of a request not satisfied because of the Investor-Level Gate is carried forward to the next Redemption Date without a new notice until satisfied in full, and amounts carried forward remain invested and continue to bear the Management Fee and expenses. The General Partner may waive the Investor-Level Gate only on a basis that applies pro rata to all Limited Partners of the affected Class.

### 6.4 Payment of Redemption Proceeds

The Fund will ordinarily pay at least 90% of estimated redemption proceeds within 30 days after the Redemption Date, without interest. On a full redemption, the balance, adjusted to the audited NAV, is paid within 30 days after completion of the audit for the fiscal year that includes the Redemption Date. The General Partner may pay proceeds in kind and may establish reserves for contingent liabilities.

### 6.5 Suspension, Compulsory Redemption and Transfers

The General Partner may suspend NAV determinations, redemptions and payment of proceeds while a principal market for the Fund's investments is closed, while the Fund's assets cannot reasonably be valued or liquidated, or where redemptions would be unlawful or materially prejudicial to remaining Limited Partners. The General Partner may compel the redemption of any Interest where continued participation would cause a regulatory, tax or ERISA violation. No Interest may be transferred without the prior written consent of the General Partner, which may be withheld in its sole discretion.

## SECTION 11. CERTAIN RISK FACTORS

An investment in the Fund is speculative and involves a high degree of risk, including the loss of the entire investment. The following is not a complete list of the risks involved.

### 11.1 Limited Operating History

The Fund commenced operations on April 1, 2025, and the prior performance of Ms. Penhallow or of any portfolio manager is not indicative of its future results.

### 11.2 Multi-Manager Platform Risk

The Fund depends on the Investment Manager's ability to recruit and retain capable portfolio managers, whose rising compensation the Fund bears as Pass-Through Expenses. Portfolio managers may leave on short notice, and crowding among multi-manager firms in similar trades may cause correlated losses across Sub-Portfolios in periods of market stress.

### 11.3 Pass-Through Expenses

Because Pass-Through Expenses are uncapped and depend on the performance of individual Sub-Portfolios, the Fund may bear substantial compensation expense in a period in which it is unprofitable, and Pass-Through Expenses may exceed the Management Fee. The ratio disclosed in Section 4.5 is not necessarily indicative of future periods.

### 11.4 Leverage, Short Selling and Derivatives

The Fund uses substantial leverage through margin borrowing, repurchase agreements and derivatives, which magnifies gains and losses. An increase in margin or haircut requirements could force the Fund to liquidate positions at disadvantageous prices. Short sales expose the Fund to theoretically unlimited loss, and fixed income relative value trades can incur large losses when spreads widen rather than converge.

### 11.5 Limited Liquidity of Interests

Interests are illiquid and no market for them is expected to develop. Redemptions are restricted by the lock-up, early redemption charge, notice and gate provisions described in Sections 2.2, 2.3 and 6, and by the General Partner's suspension rights. Because of the Investor-Level Gate, a Limited Partner seeking a full redemption will remain exposed to the Fund over several Redemption Dates. Founders Class Interests carry the longest lock-up and notice period of any Class.

### 11.6 Counterparty, Valuation and Operational Risk

Assets held by the Prime Brokers, the futures commission merchant and derivatives counterparties are subject to their credit risk, and rehypothecated assets may not be recoverable in full on an insolvency. Valuations of positions lacking quotations may differ from amounts ultimately realized, and systems failures or cyber intrusions could cause losses.

## SECTION 12. CONFLICTS OF INTEREST

### 12.1 Allocation Between the Fund and the Parallel Fund

The Investment Manager allocates investment opportunities between the Fund and the Parallel Fund pro rata to their net assets, except where legal, tax or regulatory considerations make a different allocation appropriate, and may effect cross trades between them at independent market prices.

### 12.2 Differing Class Economics

Because the Classes bear different Management Fee and Incentive Allocation rates, the Investment Manager and the General Partner earn more from Class A capital than from an equal amount of Class B or Founders Class capital, although all Classes participate pro rata in the same portfolio. Differences in lock-up and notice terms mean that Limited Partners of one Class may redeem earlier than others. Principals hold Founders Class Interests free of the Management Fee and the Incentive Allocation, which may affect the Investment Manager's liquidity decisions.

### 12.3 Pass-Through Expenses and Principal Transactions

The Investment Manager determines the compensation of investment personnel that the Fund bears as Pass-Through Expenses, and therefore has an incentive to hire and retain personnel at the Fund's cost. The Investment Manager will engage in principal transactions with the Fund only in compliance with Section 206(3) of the Advisers Act, with any required consent obtained from an independent representative.

## SECTION 13. CERTAIN TAX CONSIDERATIONS

### 13.1 Partnership Classification

The Fund expects to be classified as a partnership and not as a publicly traded partnership taxable as a corporation, relying on the private placement safe harbor of Treasury Regulations Section 1.7704-1(h). Each Limited Partner must include its distributive share of the Fund's income, gain, loss and deduction in its own return whether or not the Fund makes distributions, and the Fund does not intend to make distributions.

### 13.2 Character of Income and Deductions

The Fund's trading will produce predominantly short-term capital gain and ordinary income. The Incentive Allocation may be subject to Section 1061 of the Internal Revenue Code of 1986, as amended (the "Code"), which treats certain long-term capital gain allocated in respect of an applicable partnership interest as short-term capital gain. If the Fund is not engaged in a trade or business, a non-corporate Limited Partner's share of the Management Fee may be a miscellaneous itemized deduction that is currently not deductible.

### 13.3 Tax-Exempt Investors and Partnership Audits

Because the Fund uses leverage, a tax-exempt Limited Partner will likely realize unrelated business taxable income. Under the partnership audit regime of the Bipartisan Budget Act of 2015, an adjustment is generally assessed against the Fund unless it elects to push the adjustment out to the reviewed-year partners; the General Partner is the partnership representative.
