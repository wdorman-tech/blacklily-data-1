## SECTION 2. THE FUND AND ITS INVESTMENT PROGRAM

### 2.1 Organization and Structure

Brackenholt Senior Lending Fund, LP was formed as a limited partnership under the Delaware Revised Uniform Limited Partnership Act on January 22, 2024 and commenced operations on October 1, 2024 (the "Initial Closing Date"). The Fund is structured as an open-end, evergreen vehicle: it has no fixed term, it does not call capital from Limited Partners in drawdowns, and it admits Limited Partners and accepts additional subscriptions monthly at net asset value. It is not redeemable on demand. No Limited Partner may require the Fund to repurchase its Interests, and liquidity is provided only through the quarterly repurchase program described in Section 6. It is not a feeder fund, does not invest through a master fund and has no feeder funds of its own.

Brackenholt Senior Lending Fund (Cayman), Ltd., a Cayman Islands exempted company (the "Parallel Fund"), was organized for investors that are not U.S. persons and invests alongside the Fund in substantially all portfolio investments, in proportion to the capital each vehicle has available. The Parallel Fund is a separate entity with its own offering memorandum and repurchase program and is not offered by this Memorandum. Neither vehicle invests in, or is liable for the obligations of, the other.

### 2.2 Investment Objective, Strategy and Guidelines

The Fund seeks current income and, secondarily, preservation of capital by originating and acquiring senior secured, floating-rate loans to U.S. middle-market companies, which the Adviser defines as companies with annual EBITDA of $15 million to $100 million at the time of investment. Most borrowers are expected to be controlled by private equity sponsors. Loans generally bear interest at a spread over three-month Term SOFR subject to a floor and include at least one financial maintenance covenant.

At the time of each investment, measured against the Fund's total assets: (a) at least 80% of investments will be first lien or unitranche loans; (b) no more than 4.0% will be invested in any single borrower and its affiliates; (c) no more than 20% will be invested in borrowers in any single industry; and (d) no more than 10% will be invested in second lien loans, equity securities and warrants, taken together. These limits may be modified only with the consent of Limited Partners holding a majority of the Interests.

### 2.3 Leverage and the Credit Facility

The Fund, through a wholly owned financing subsidiary, is the borrower under a $300 million senior secured revolving credit facility with Sallowfield Bank, N.A., as administrative agent and lender (the "Credit Facility"). The Credit Facility is secured by the subsidiary's assets, is subject to a borrowing base, bears interest at one-month Term SOFR plus 2.10% per annum on drawn amounts, and matures on October 1, 2029. Total borrowings may not exceed a debt-to-equity ratio of 1.00 to 1.00 at the time of incurrence. The Fund may hedge interest rate exposure with the lender or its affiliates.

### 2.4 Valuation

The General Partner determines net asset value as of the last Business Day of each calendar month (each, a "Valuation Date"). Substantially all investments are Level 3 assets valued at fair value under ASC Topic 820. Ambleton Valuation Group, LLC, an independent valuation firm, provides a range of values for each Level 3 investment at least once each calendar quarter, and the Adviser's valuation committee, which excludes the responsible deal team, approves each final mark.

## SECTION 4. FEES AND EXPENSES

### 4.1 Management Fee

The Fund pays the Adviser a management fee (the "Management Fee") at an annual rate of 1.25% of the Fund's net asset value, accrued as of each Valuation Date at one-twelfth of that rate and payable monthly in arrears. Net asset value for this purpose is determined before deduction of the Management Fee and Incentive Fee for the month and before repurchases and distributions as of that Valuation Date. The Management Fee is charged on net assets. It is not charged on gross assets, on assets acquired with borrowings, or on undrawn capacity under the Credit Facility. The Management Fee is prorated for partial months, and Interests held by the Adviser and its personnel bear none. The General Partner may reduce the Management Fee for a particular Limited Partner by side letter (Section 7.1); any such reduction applies only to that Limited Partner and does not change the rate set forth in this Section 4.1.

### 4.2 Incentive Fee

The Fund pays the Adviser an incentive fee (the "Incentive Fee") in two parts, calculated independently of each other: an Income Incentive Fee equal to 12.5% of Pre-Incentive Fee Net Investment Income in excess of the Hurdle Rate, subject to the catch-up, as described in Section 4.3; and a Capital Gains Incentive Fee equal to 12.5% of cumulative net realized capital gains, as described in Section 4.4. A shortfall in one component does not reduce the other. No side letter in effect on the date of this Memorandum reduces or waives the Incentive Fee.

### 4.3 Income Incentive Fee

"Pre-Incentive Fee Net Investment Income" means interest, dividend and fee income (including origination and amendment fees) accrued during a calendar quarter, less the operating expenses accrued for the quarter, including the Management Fee and interest and fees under the Credit Facility but excluding the Incentive Fee. It includes accrued income not yet received in cash, such as original issue discount and payment-in-kind interest, and excludes realized and unrealized capital gains and losses.

Pre-Incentive Fee Net Investment Income for each quarter, expressed as a rate of return on the Fund's net asset value at the end of the immediately preceding quarter, is compared to a hurdle rate of 1.25% per quarter (5.0% annualized) (the "Hurdle Rate"). The Income Incentive Fee is paid quarterly in arrears as follows: (a) no Income Incentive Fee in any quarter in which Pre-Incentive Fee Net Investment Income does not exceed the Hurdle Rate; (b) 100% of Pre-Incentive Fee Net Investment Income in excess of the Hurdle Rate but not in excess of 1.4286% per quarter (5.7143% annualized) (the "catch-up"); and (c) 12.5% of Pre-Incentive Fee Net Investment Income in excess of 1.4286% per quarter.

The catch-up is a full catch-up: once Pre-Incentive Fee Net Investment Income reaches 1.4286% for a quarter, the Adviser has received 12.5% of all of it, and not only of the excess over the Hurdle Rate. The Hurdle Rate is not cumulative and does not compound, and a shortfall in one quarter is not carried forward. There is no high-water mark, look-back or loss carryforward with respect to the Income Incentive Fee, and it may be payable for a quarter in which net asset value declined because of capital losses.

For example, if Pre-Incentive Fee Net Investment Income for a quarter is 2.00%, the catch-up produces 0.1786% (1.4286% - 1.25%), the Adviser receives a further 0.0714% (12.5% x (2.00% - 1.4286%)), and the total Income Incentive Fee is 0.2500%, or 12.5% of 2.00%. At 1.35%, the Income Incentive Fee is 0.10% (1.35% - 1.25%); at 1.10%, none is payable.

### 4.4 Capital Gains Incentive Fee and Loss Carryforward

The Capital Gains Incentive Fee is determined and payable annually in arrears as of December 31, and upon any termination of the Advisory Agreement, and equals 12.5% of the Fund's cumulative realized capital gains from the Initial Closing Date, computed net of all cumulative realized capital losses and cumulative unrealized capital depreciation, less all Capital Gains Incentive Fees previously paid. Unrealized capital appreciation is excluded.

Because the computation is cumulative from the Initial Closing Date, realized capital losses and unrealized capital depreciation are carried forward and must be recovered by later net realized capital gains before any further Capital Gains Incentive Fee is payable (the "Loss Carryforward"). The Loss Carryforward is perpetual and is not reset at year end or upon the admission or repurchase of Limited Partners. The Loss Carryforward applies only to the Capital Gains Incentive Fee. The Capital Gains Incentive Fee has no hurdle or catch-up, and fees previously paid are not subject to clawback.

For example, if in Year 1 the Fund realizes a $4.0 million gain and a $1.0 million loss and holds $1.2 million of unrealized depreciation, the fee is 12.5% x $1.8 million = $225,000. If in Year 2 that depreciation grows to $3.5 million with no realizations, the cumulative result is negative $0.5 million, no fee is payable, and the deficit is carried forward. If in Year 3 the depreciated loan is sold at a $2.0 million realized loss and a further $5.5 million gain is realized, cumulative net realized gains are $6.5 million and the fee is 12.5% x $6.5 million = $812,500, less $225,000 previously paid, or $587,500.

## SECTION 6. REPURCHASES OF INTERESTS

### 6.1 Quarterly Repurchase Offers

Limited Partners have no right to redeem or withdraw from the Fund. The General Partner currently intends, but is not obligated, to cause the Fund to offer to repurchase Interests quarterly, as of the last Business Day of each calendar quarter (each, a "Repurchase Date"). The Fund sends each Limited Partner written notice of each offer (a "Repurchase Offer Notice") not less than 20 Business Days before the Notice Deadline. The General Partner may decline to make an offer for any quarter if it determines in good faith that doing so is in the best interests of the Fund.

### 6.2 Repurchase Requests and Notice Deadline

A Limited Partner wishing to tender all or part of its Interests must deliver an irrevocable written repurchase request to the Administrator (a "Repurchase Request") so that it is received not later than 60 days prior to the applicable Repurchase Date (the "Notice Deadline"). Requests received after the Notice Deadline are not accepted for that Repurchase Date and must be resubmitted for a later one. A partial request may not reduce a Limited Partner's Capital Account below $500,000.

### 6.3 Quarterly Repurchase Limit

The aggregate amount of Interests that the Fund will repurchase as of any Repurchase Date is limited to 5.0% of the Fund's net asset value as of the last Valuation Date of the immediately preceding calendar quarter (the "Quarterly Repurchase Limit"). The Quarterly Repurchase Limit is applied at the level of the Fund, across all Limited Partners, and not Limited Partner by Limited Partner. If timely Repurchase Requests exceed it, Interests are repurchased pro rata to the amounts requested, and unfilled portions do not carry over to the next quarter. The General Partner may, in its sole discretion, repurchase up to an additional 2.0% of net asset value as of any Repurchase Date but is under no obligation to do so. Repurchases by the Parallel Fund do not count against the Quarterly Repurchase Limit.

### 6.4 Early Repurchase Deduction

There is no hard lock-up. Interests repurchased as of a Repurchase Date that falls within one year after the Subscription Date on which they were issued are subject to a deduction of 2.0% of the net asset value of the Interests repurchased (the "Early Repurchase Deduction"), applied on a first-in, first-out basis. The Early Repurchase Deduction is retained by the Fund for the benefit of the remaining Limited Partners. It does not apply to reinvested distributions, and the General Partner may waive it upon the death or disability of a natural person Limited Partner or by side letter.

### 6.5 Payment and Suspension

The repurchase price is net asset value as of the Repurchase Date, less any Early Repurchase Deduction. The Fund pays at least 90% of the estimated price within 35 days after the Repurchase Date and, for a Limited Partner tendering its entire Interest, the balance after completion of the annual audit. The General Partner may suspend or postpone an offer if a material portion of the Fund's investments cannot reasonably be valued, if the repurchase would breach the Credit Facility or cause the Fund to be treated as a publicly traded partnership, or if it would be materially adverse to the remaining Limited Partners.

## SECTION 9. CERTAIN RISK FACTORS

An investment in the Fund is speculative and suitable only for investors who can bear the loss of their entire investment. The following does not purport to be a complete list of risks.

### 9.1 Limited Liquidity

Interests are illiquid, and the Fund is not required to make any repurchase offer. When timely Repurchase Requests exceed 5.0% of net asset value, a Limited Partner may have only part of its tender filled, and because unfilled requests do not carry over, a full exit may take many quarters. Repurchases may be suspended. A Limited Partner bears changes in net asset value between the Notice Deadline and the Repurchase Date.

### 9.2 Credit and Leverage Risk

Borrowers are typically highly leveraged and unrated or rated below investment grade. A downturn or higher interest rates may impair their ability to pay, and collateral may prove insufficient. Borrowings under the Credit Facility magnify losses, the lender's claim is senior to the Limited Partners', and a borrowing base shortfall may force asset sales or suspend distributions and repurchases.

### 9.3 Incentive Fee Considerations

The Income Incentive Fee is calculated on accrued income, including payment-in-kind interest that may never be collected, and is not reduced by capital losses; the Adviser may earn it in a quarter in which net asset value declines. The Loss Carryforward limits only the Capital Gains Incentive Fee. Leverage increases Pre-Incentive Fee Net Investment Income and may give the Adviser an incentive to borrow to exceed the Hurdle Rate.

### 9.4 Reliance on Key Personnel

The Partnership Agreement contains no key person provision, and the loss of Ms. Ferrante, Mr. Oyelaran or Ms. Kabberley would give Limited Partners no right to redeem, to suspend investment activity or to terminate the Advisory Agreement.

### 9.5 Side Letters

Limited Partners that hold side letters may pay a lower Management Fee and may not bear the Early Repurchase Deduction. A Limited Partner holding a personnel notification right may be permitted to submit a Repurchase Request after the Notice Deadline following a personnel event, and its request would share pro rata in the Quarterly Repurchase Limit with the requests of other Limited Partners.

## SECTION 10. CONFLICTS OF INTEREST

The Adviser also manages the Parallel Fund and two closed-end drawdown credit funds with overlapping strategies, and allocates opportunities among them in proportion to available capital, adjusted for each vehicle's guidelines, leverage and liquidity, under a written allocation policy. Vehicles holding different tranches of one borrower may have divergent interests in a workout. Because the Management Fee is based on net asset value and the Income Incentive Fee on accrued income, the Adviser has an incentive to value investments favorably and to prefer higher-yielding and payment-in-kind investments. The independent valuation process in Section 2.4 mitigates but cannot eliminate these conflicts.

## SECTION 11. CERTAIN TAX AND ERISA CONSIDERATIONS

### 11.1 U.S. Federal Income Tax

The Fund intends to be classified as a partnership that is not a publicly traded partnership within the meaning of Section 7704 of the Internal Revenue Code of 1986, as amended (the "Code"), relying on one or more of the safe harbors in Treasury Regulations Section 1.7704-1, and the General Partner may limit repurchases and transfers for that purpose. The Fund does not expect to rely on the qualifying income exception in Section 7704(c) of the Code, because interest earned in the conduct of a lending business may not be qualifying income. Each Limited Partner is taxed on its distributive share of Fund income whether or not distributed. U.S. tax-exempt Limited Partners should expect income attributable to Credit Facility borrowings to be unrelated business taxable income as debt-financed income under Section 514 of the Code.

### 11.2 ERISA Considerations

The General Partner intends to limit investment by "benefit plan investors," as defined in Section 3(42) of the Employee Retirement Income Security Act of 1974, as amended, to less than 25% of the value of each class of equity interests in the Fund, so that the Fund's assets are not "plan assets" under 29 C.F.R. Section 2510.3-101, and may reject subscriptions or require repurchases to maintain that limit. Endowments and governmental plans are not benefit plan investors for this purpose.
