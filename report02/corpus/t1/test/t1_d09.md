## SECTION 1. THE PARTNERSHIP

### 1.1 Organization

Castellow Wexcombe Multi-Manager Fund, L.P. is a limited partnership organized on September 17, 2019 under the Delaware Revised Uniform Limited Partnership Act (the "Delaware Act"). It commenced operations on January 1, 2020. Its general partner is Castellow Wexcombe Fund GP, LLC (the "General Partner"), which has delegated investment discretion to Castellow Wexcombe Advisors, LLC (the "Investment Manager"), an investment adviser registered with the Securities and Exchange Commission (the "SEC") under the Investment Advisers Act of 1940, as amended (the "Advisers Act"). Both are Delaware limited liability companies under common control with principal offices in Stamford, Connecticut. The General Partner and the limited partners (the "Limited Partners") are the "Partners." The fiscal year ends December 31, and all capital contributions and withdrawals are made in U.S. dollars.

As of June 30, 2026, the Partnership had a net asset value of approximately $384.6 million, invested in 23 private investment funds (each, a "Portfolio Fund") managed by 21 unaffiliated investment managers (each, a "Portfolio Manager").

### 1.2 Structure

The Partnership is an open-end fund of hedge funds. Interests are offered monthly and may be withdrawn quarterly as described in Section 5, and the Partnership does not call capital after admission. The Partnership is a stand-alone fund: it is not a feeder fund in a master-feeder structure, it does not invest through a master fund, and no parallel or feeder vehicle has been organized to invest in or alongside it. It holds its Portfolio Fund interests directly and neither controls nor is affiliated with any Portfolio Fund or Portfolio Manager. The Partnership offers a single class of Interests.

### 1.3 Term and Dissolution

The Partnership has no fixed term and will continue perpetually unless dissolved in accordance with the Partnership Agreement. It will be dissolved on an election by the General Partner on not less than 60 days' written notice, on the withdrawal or bankruptcy of the General Partner (unless a majority in interest of the Limited Partners elects a successor within 90 days), on the complete withdrawal of all Limited Partners, or on judicial dissolution. Because Portfolio Funds may be slow to pay withdrawal proceeds, winding up may take several years, and Portfolio Fund interests may be distributed in kind.

## SECTION 2. INVESTMENT PROGRAM

### 2.1 Investment Objective and Strategy

The Partnership seeks long-term capital appreciation with lower volatility than, and limited correlation to, global equity and fixed income markets over a full market cycle, by allocating among Portfolio Managers pursuing relative value, event driven, equity long/short, and global macro and systematic strategies. No strategy group is expected to represent more than 40% of net asset value. There can be no assurance that the objective will be achieved.

### 2.2 Manager Selection and Monitoring

Allocations require the approval of both voting members of the Investment Manager's investment committee (the "Investment Committee"), Anneliese Throckford and Desmond Varrell. Operational due diligence is conducted separately by staff reporting to Helena Marchbank, who may veto an allocation on operational grounds, and covers each Portfolio Fund's administrator, auditor, valuation policy, cash controls, counterparties and principals. The Investment Manager generally will not invest in a Portfolio Fund that lacks an independent administrator and an annual audit. After investment, it reviews monthly performance and exposure data, holds quarterly calls with each Portfolio Manager and conducts an on-site review at least once every 24 months.

### 2.3 Investment Limits

The Partnership expects to hold 18 to 30 Portfolio Funds. At the time of investment, no more than 12% of net asset value may be invested in any one Portfolio Fund, no more than 15% with any one Portfolio Manager and its affiliates, and no more than 20% in Portfolio Funds that permit withdrawals less often than annually or impose lock-ups exceeding two years. The Partnership does not invest in funds managed by the Investment Manager or its affiliates and generally holds less than 5% of the voting securities of any Portfolio Fund.

### 2.4 Liquidity Terms of Portfolio Funds

The Partnership's liquidity depends on the terms of the Portfolio Funds, which are set by the Portfolio Managers, not by the Investment Manager. The Portfolio Funds in which the Partnership currently invests generally permit withdrawals monthly, quarterly or annually on 30 to 90 days' prior written notice, and a minority require 120 days' notice or more. Many impose initial lock-up periods of one to three years, which may be "hard" lock-ups during which no withdrawal is permitted or "soft" lock-ups under which early withdrawals bear redemption fees, typically 2% to 5% of the amount withdrawn. Most Portfolio Funds reserve the right to impose fund-level or investor-level gates, commonly limiting withdrawals on any withdrawal date to 10% to 20% of the Portfolio Fund's net asset value or of the investor's interest, to suspend withdrawals, to hold back proceeds pending audit, to designate side pockets and to pay proceeds in kind. The liquidity terms offered to Limited Partners in Section 5 are set with reference to, but are not the same as, the terms of the Portfolio Funds.

### 2.5 Borrowing and Currency Hedging

The Partnership may borrow, including under a secured revolving credit facility with a commercial bank, up to 15% of its net asset value at the time of borrowing, to fund withdrawals or to bridge investments ahead of subscriptions or withdrawal proceeds, but not to leverage its investments. It does not trade securities or derivatives directly, other than holding cash, U.S. Treasury bills and government money market fund shares and entering into currency forwards with bank counterparties to hedge non-dollar Portfolio Funds.

## SECTION 4. FEES AND EXPENSES

### 4.1 Management Fee

The Partnership pays the Investment Manager a management fee (the "Management Fee") at an annual rate of 1.00% of the net asset value of each Limited Partner's capital account. The Management Fee is payable quarterly in advance, as of the first business day of each calendar quarter, at the rate of 0.25% of the net asset value of each capital account as of that day. A capital contribution accepted as of the first business day of the second or third month of a quarter is charged a prorated Management Fee as of the date of the contribution. No portion of the Management Fee is refunded on a withdrawal. The Management Fee is in addition to, and is not reduced by, the Portfolio Fund management fees described in Section 4.4.

### 4.2 Incentive Allocation

At the end of each Incentive Period, an amount equal to 10% of the Excess Net Profits of each Limited Partner's capital account (the "Incentive Allocation") is reallocated from that capital account to the capital account of the General Partner. "Excess Net Profits" means the amount, if any, by which the Net Profits of the capital account for the Incentive Period exceed the sum of its Loss Carryforward at the start of the period and its Hurdle Amount for the period, each as defined in Section 4.3.

"Net Profits" and "Net Losses" mean the net increase and the net decrease in the net asset value of a capital account for the period, after the Management Fee, Partnership expenses and all fees and allocations borne through the Portfolio Funds, and excluding contributions and withdrawals. "Incentive Period" means each fiscal year and each shorter period ending on a Withdrawal Date (as to the amount withdrawn) or on the dissolution of the Partnership.

### 4.3 Hurdle Amount and Loss Carryforward

The "Hurdle Amount" for a capital account for any Incentive Period equals 5.00% per annum, prorated for periods of less than a full fiscal year, multiplied by the net asset value of the capital account at the beginning of the period (adjusted on a time-weighted basis for contributions and withdrawals). The hurdle is a hard hurdle: the Incentive Allocation is calculated only on Net Profits in excess of the Hurdle Amount, and the General Partner receives no catch-up allocation of the Net Profits below it. The Hurdle Amount does not compound, and a shortfall in one Incentive Period is not carried forward.

The "Loss Carryforward" of a capital account is the cumulative Net Losses allocated to it for prior Incentive Periods that have not been offset by later Net Profits. No Incentive Allocation is made until the Loss Carryforward has been fully recovered, so that the Loss Carryforward functions as a high-water mark for each capital account. It does not expire and is not reset at the end of any fiscal year or for any other reason, except that it is reduced proportionately on a partial withdrawal.

By way of illustration, a capital account of $2,000,000 on January 1, with no Loss Carryforward and no contributions or withdrawals, that earns Net Profits of $180,000 for the fiscal year has a Hurdle Amount of $100,000, Excess Net Profits of $80,000 and an Incentive Allocation of $8,000. If the same account incurs a Net Loss of $150,000, it carries a $150,000 Loss Carryforward into the following year, in which that amount and then that year's Hurdle Amount must be earned before any Incentive Allocation is made.

### 4.4 Fees and Allocations Charged by Portfolio Funds

The Management Fee and the Incentive Allocation are the only fees and allocations charged by the Partnership for the services of the Investment Manager and the General Partner. In addition, the Partnership, and therefore each Limited Partner indirectly, bears the fees, allocations and expenses charged by each Portfolio Fund. These are set by the Portfolio Managers, are not reflected in the rates stated in Sections 4.1 to 4.3, and are negotiated by the Investment Manager only as described in Section 9.2. The Portfolio Managers of the Portfolio Funds in which the Partnership currently invests typically charge asset-based management fees of 1.50% to 2.00% per annum of net asset value and performance fees or allocations of 15% to 20% of net profits, and in a small number of cases up to 30%. Portfolio Fund performance fees are generally determined annually and subject to a high-water mark, but in most cases without any hurdle or with a hurdle tied to a short-term U.S. Treasury rate. Some Portfolio Funds also pass through operating, research and compensation-related expenses that can exceed 0.50% of net asset value per annum. At June 30, 2026, the asset-weighted average Portfolio Fund management fee was approximately 1.62% per annum and the asset-weighted average performance fee rate was approximately 18.4%. Because Portfolio Fund performance fees are charged on each Portfolio Fund's own results, the Partnership may bear them in a period in which it has a loss overall and no Incentive Allocation is made.

### 4.5 Placement Fees and Partnership Expenses

Placement agents engaged by the Investment Manager may charge an investor a placement fee of up to 2.0% of its capital contribution, payable by the investor in addition to, and not credited to, that contribution. Placement fees are not expenses of the Partnership.

The Partnership bears its operating expenses, including administration, custody, audit, tax, legal, borrowing, hedging, insurance and reporting costs and extraordinary expenses. The Investment Manager bears its own overhead and due diligence travel, and reimburses the Partnership to the extent that ordinary operating expenses (excluding the Management Fee, the Incentive Allocation, Portfolio Fund fees, borrowing and hedging costs, taxes and extraordinary expenses) exceed 0.30% per annum of average net asset value. For fiscal year 2025, those expenses were approximately 0.24% of average net asset value.

## SECTION 5. SUBSCRIPTIONS AND WITHDRAWALS

### 5.1 Eligible Investors

Interests are offered only to persons who are accredited investors under Regulation D, qualified purchasers under Section 2(a)(51) of the Company Act (generally, natural persons owning at least $5,000,000 in investments and entities owning or investing on a discretionary basis at least $25,000,000) and qualified clients under Rule 205-3 under the Advisers Act.

### 5.2 Subscriptions

Interests are offered as of the first business day of each calendar month (each, a "Subscription Date"). Completed Subscription Documents must be received by the Administrator at least five business days, and cleared funds at least three business days, before the Subscription Date. The minimum initial capital contribution is $1,000,000, and the minimum additional capital contribution is $100,000, in each case subject to the discretion of the General Partner to accept lesser amounts. The General Partner may reject any subscription in whole or in part and may close the Partnership to new capital where Portfolio Fund capacity is constrained.

### 5.3 Withdrawals

A Limited Partner may withdraw all or part of its capital account as of the last business day of each calendar quarter (each, a "Withdrawal Date") by written notice received by the Administrator at least 95 days before the Withdrawal Date. A withdrawal request is irrevocable without the consent of the General Partner. A partial withdrawal must be at least $100,000 and may not reduce the capital account below $500,000. The General Partner may waive or reduce the notice period in its discretion. Withdrawals are subject to Sections 5.4 to 5.7.

### 5.4 Soft Lock-Up and Early Withdrawal Charge

Each capital contribution is subject to a soft lock-up of one year. Amounts attributable to a capital contribution may be withdrawn as of a Withdrawal Date falling before the first anniversary of that contribution, but only subject to an early withdrawal charge of 4% of the amount withdrawn. The charge is deducted from the proceeds and retained by the Partnership for the benefit of the remaining Limited Partners; it is not paid to the General Partner or the Investment Manager. Withdrawals are treated as made first from the earliest contributions. The Partnership does not impose a hard lock-up. The General Partner may waive the charge, including on death or disability or a change in law affecting the Limited Partner.

### 5.5 Investor-Level Gate

No Limited Partner may withdraw, as of any single Withdrawal Date, more than 25% of the net asset value of its capital account, measured as of the first Withdrawal Date to which its withdrawal request relates (the "Investor Gate"). Any excess is carried forward automatically, without further notice, and withdrawn in successive installments, each subject to the Investor Gate, so that a request for a complete withdrawal will ordinarily be satisfied over four consecutive Withdrawal Dates. Amounts carried forward remain invested, continue to bear the Management Fee, and bear the early withdrawal charge only if withdrawn before the first anniversary of the relevant contribution.

The Investor Gate applies to each Limited Partner separately and does not depend on withdrawal requests from other Limited Partners. The Partnership does not impose a gate by reference to aggregate withdrawals or to the Partnership's net asset value, although withdrawals may be suspended under Section 5.7. The General Partner may waive or raise the Investor Gate as of a Withdrawal Date only for all Limited Partners withdrawing as of that date.

### 5.6 Payment of Withdrawal Proceeds; Holdback

The Partnership will ordinarily pay at least 90% of estimated withdrawal proceeds within 45 days after the Withdrawal Date. On a withdrawal of 90% or more of a capital account, it may hold back up to 10% of the estimated proceeds, without interest, until completion of the audit for the fiscal year that includes the Withdrawal Date, and will pay the balance, adjusted to the audited net asset value, within 30 days thereafter. If a Portfolio Fund delays payment, pays in kind or creates a side pocket, the corresponding portion of the proceeds may be delayed until the Partnership receives cash or paid in kind.

### 5.7 Suspension, Compulsory Withdrawal and Transfers

The General Partner may suspend valuations, withdrawals or payment of proceeds while Portfolio Funds representing a material portion of the Partnership's assets have suspended or gated withdrawals, while the Partnership's assets cannot reasonably be valued or realized, or after it has determined to dissolve the Partnership. Pending requests are effected as of the first Withdrawal Date after the suspension ends. The General Partner may require a Limited Partner to withdraw on 10 days' notice where its continued participation would cause a legal, regulatory, ERISA or tax violation. No Interest may be transferred without the General Partner's prior written consent.

## SECTION 6. VALUATION AND CAPITAL ACCOUNTS

The Administrator determines net asset value as of the last business day of each month under procedures adopted by the General Partner. Portfolio Fund interests are generally valued at the most recent value reported by the Portfolio Fund, which may be an estimate; where no reliable value is available, the General Partner determines fair value in good faith. Prior net asset values are not restated for later revisions except through the holdback adjustment in Section 5.6. Monthly net profits and net losses are allocated among the Partners' capital accounts in proportion to their opening balances, and the Management Fee and any early withdrawal charge are charged only to the capital account to which they relate.

## SECTION 8. CERTAIN RISK FACTORS

An investment in the Partnership is speculative and involves the risk of loss of the entire investment. The following summary is not exhaustive.

### 8.1 Multiple Layers of Fees and Expenses

Limited Partners bear the Management Fee, the Incentive Allocation and the Partnership's expenses and, indirectly, the Portfolio Fund fees described in Section 4.4. Adding the asset-weighted average Portfolio Fund management fee at June 30, 2026 (approximately 1.62%) and the Partnership's ordinary expense ratio for fiscal year 2025 (approximately 0.24%) to the Management Fee (1.00%), aggregate asset-based fees and ordinary expenses would be approximately 2.86% of net asset value per annum, before Portfolio Fund pass-through expenses and before performance fees and allocations at either level.

### 8.2 Limited Liquidity

Withdrawals are permitted only quarterly on 95 days' notice and are subject to the Investor Gate, the early withdrawal charge, the holdback and suspension. Because many Portfolio Funds have longer notice periods, lock-ups, gates and side pockets than the Partnership (Section 2.4), the Partnership may need to borrow, redeem from its most liquid Portfolio Funds, leaving the remaining portfolio less liquid and more concentrated, suspend withdrawals or pay proceeds in kind.

### 8.3 Dependence on the Investment Manager

The success of the Partnership depends on the Investment Manager's ability to select and monitor Portfolio Managers and, in particular, on the judgment of Ms. Throckford and Mr. Varrell as the voting members of the Investment Committee. The loss of the services of either of them could adversely affect the Partnership. Limited Partners have no right to participate in the management of the Partnership or in its investment decisions.

### 8.4 Portfolio Fund, Valuation and Misconduct Risk

Portfolio Managers may use leverage, short selling, derivatives and concentrated or illiquid positions, may change strategies without notice and generally provide limited transparency. The Partnership's net asset value depends on values reported by Portfolio Funds, many of which are estimates. Due diligence cannot eliminate the risk of fraud, misvaluation or operational failure at a Portfolio Fund, which could result in the loss of the Partnership's entire investment in it.

## SECTION 9. CONFLICTS OF INTEREST

### 9.1 Other Accounts and Capacity

The Investment Manager's separate accounts invest in many of the same Portfolio Funds. Where Portfolio Fund capacity is limited, the Investment Manager allocates it in proportion to target allocations or on another basis it considers fair over time. Its principals devote only such time to the Partnership as they consider necessary.

### 9.2 Side Letters and Most Favored Nation Rights

The General Partner and the Investment Manager may enter into side letters with one or more Limited Partners that grant terms different from those in this Memorandum, including reductions of the Management Fee or the Incentive Allocation, additional reporting and transparency, capacity rights and regulatory accommodations, generally for Limited Partners that make substantial investments. Limited Partners will not be notified of the terms of other side letters, and the Partnership does not offer Limited Partners any most favored nation election or other right to receive the benefit of terms granted to another Limited Partner.

Separately, the Investment Manager seeks, on the Partnership's behalf, fee reductions, capacity rights and "most favored nation" rights under which a Portfolio Fund must offer the Partnership any more favorable fee or liquidity terms it grants to other investors of similar or smaller size. The Partnership held such rights with respect to 14 of its 23 Portfolio Funds at June 30, 2026. Those rights run to the Partnership, not to the Limited Partners, and any benefit is reflected only in the Partnership's net asset value.

### 9.3 Valuation and Incentive Conflicts

The Management Fee and the Incentive Allocation are based on net asset values determined in part by the General Partner, which therefore has an interest in higher valuations. The Incentive Allocation is calculated on unrealized appreciation, including estimated values that may later be revised downward, and is not returned if they are.

## SECTION 10. CERTAIN TAX AND ERISA CONSIDERATIONS

### 10.1 Tax Matters

The Partnership expects to be classified as a partnership, and not as a publicly traded partnership, for U.S. federal income tax purposes, so that each Limited Partner is taxed on its distributive share of Partnership income whether or not distributed. Leverage at the Portfolio Fund level, or borrowing by the Partnership, may generate unrelated business taxable income for tax-exempt Limited Partners. Because the Partnership depends on tax information from Portfolio Funds, Schedules K-1 are expected after April 15 of each year, and Limited Partners should expect to extend their own filings. The General Partner serves as the Partnership's partnership representative for federal tax audits.

### 10.2 ERISA

The General Partner intends to limit investment by benefit plan investors to less than 25% of the value of each class of equity interests in the Partnership, disregarding interests held by the General Partner, the Investment Manager and their affiliates, so that the Partnership's assets are not plan assets under Section 3(42) of ERISA and 29 C.F.R. Section 2510.3-101, and may reject subscriptions or require withdrawals to maintain that limit.
