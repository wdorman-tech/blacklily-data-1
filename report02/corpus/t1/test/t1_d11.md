## SECTION 3. INVESTMENT OBJECTIVE AND PROGRAM

### 3.1 Investment Approach

The Master Fund seeks attractive risk-adjusted returns over a full market cycle with limited correlation to equity and credit markets. The approach is discretionary and top-down: positions express themes derived from monetary policy, fiscal trajectory, inflation, balance of payments, capital flows and political developments in developed and emerging economies. Core themes are typically held for several weeks to several months, with tactical trading around them. Relative value positions, such as yield curve steepeners, cross-market spreads and currency crosses, are used alongside directional positions.

### 3.2 Instruments

The Master Fund may trade government bonds and bills; interest rate futures, swaps, swaptions, caps and floors; spot and forward currencies, non-deliverable forwards and currency options; equity index futures, options and total return swaps; energy, metals and agricultural futures and options; credit default swaps on sovereigns and credit indices; exchange-traded funds; and repurchase agreements. It does not invest in private funds, participate in new issues of equity securities or originate loans.

### 3.3 Leverage and Risk Management

The Master Fund employs substantial leverage through the notional exposure embedded in derivatives. Gross notional exposure has historically ranged between 8 and 20 times net asset value, and the margin-to-equity ratio typically ranges between 10% and 30%. The Investment Manager targets annualized volatility of approximately 10% and monitors risk through value-at-risk limits, stress scenarios, liquidity analysis and concentration limits set by a risk committee chaired by the Chief Risk Officer, who is independent of portfolio management. Risk limits are internal guidelines and may be changed without notice.

### 3.4 Investment Restrictions

The Master Fund will not: (a) commit more than 10% of its net asset value to positions the Investment Manager believes cannot be liquidated within 30 days; (b) have net credit exposure exceeding 5% of net asset value to any single issuer other than an investment grade sovereign or its agencies; (c) make loans, other than through repurchase transactions, securities lending and posting of collateral; or (d) take delivery of physical commodities other than transitorily. Restrictions apply at the time of investment.

### 3.5 Changes to the Program

The Investment Manager may modify the program without the consent of the Limited Partners. The General Partner will give prior written notice of any fundamental change to the investment objective.

A memorandum account (the "Loss Carryforward") is maintained for each capital account, increased by Net Losses and decreased, but not below zero, by Net Profits. ## SECTION 9. CERTAIN RISK FACTORS

An investment in the Fund involves significant risks, including those below. This discussion does not purport to be complete, and prospective investors should read this entire Memorandum and consult their own advisers before investing.

### 9.1 General Investment Risk

The Fund's performance depends entirely on the Master Fund, which depends on the judgment of the Investment Manager. Past performance is not indicative of future results. The Master Fund may incur substantial losses in any period, and a Limited Partner could lose all or substantially all of its investment.

### 9.2 Leverage

The use of derivatives results in notional exposure that is a large multiple of the net asset value of the Master Fund. Leverage magnifies gains and losses: a small adverse price movement in a leveraged position may produce a loss substantially greater than the margin deposited. Exchanges, clearing houses, futures commission merchants and prime brokers may increase margin requirements at any time, including during market stress, which may compel the Master Fund to liquidate positions at disadvantageous prices. Borrowings and repurchase agreements expose the Master Fund to financing costs and to the risk that financing is withdrawn or repriced at short notice.

### 9.3 Derivatives

Futures markets may impose daily price limits that prevent prompt liquidation, and exchanges may impose position limits. Over-the-counter swaps, forwards and options are bilateral contracts that expose the Master Fund to counterparty credit, to disputes over valuation and collateral, and to termination at unfavorable times. Centrally cleared swaps expose the Master Fund to default by a clearing member or clearing house. Writing options may produce losses that are large relative to the premium received, and hedges may fail to behave as expected.

### 9.4 Currency Risk

Currency markets are volatile and affected by interest rate differentials, capital flows, central bank intervention and exchange controls. Non-deliverable forwards settle by reference to fixing rates that may be suspended or unavailable, and a currency may become inconvertible or subject to multiple exchange rates. Positions not denominated in U.S. dollars are affected by exchange rate movements.

### 9.5 Interest Rate and Sovereign Debt Risk

Interest rate positions depend on monetary policy, inflation expectations and fiscal developments that are difficult to predict, and yield curve and cross-market positions may lose value if historical relationships change. Sovereign issuers may be unable or unwilling to pay when due, may restructure unilaterally, and are not subject to bankruptcy proceedings. Changes to or discontinuation of reference rates used to price floating-rate instruments and derivatives may adversely affect positions that reference them.

### 9.6 Emerging Markets

Emerging market positions involve political and economic instability, expropriation, confiscatory taxation, capital controls, less developed markets, settlement failures, limited custody arrangements, unreliable economic data and sanctions that may prevent the Master Fund from holding or disposing of positions.

### 9.7 Commodities

Commodity prices are affected by weather, supply disruptions, embargoes, tariffs, storage and transportation capacity and government policy. Rolling futures positions may reduce returns, and speculative position limits may require the Investment Manager to reduce positions across its accounts.

### 9.8 Counterparty, Prime Broker and Custody Risk

Substantially all Master Fund assets are held by its prime brokers, futures commission merchants and custodians. The prime brokers may rehypothecate assets to the extent permitted by the prime brokerage agreements and applicable regulations, and in a prime broker insolvency the Master Fund may be an unsecured creditor as to rehypothecated or unsegregated assets. Securities Investor Protection Act coverage is limited. A shortfall in a futures commission merchant's customer segregated accounts may be shared pro rata among its customers. Because the Master Fund uses two prime brokers, the failure of either could affect a significant portion of its assets.

### 9.9 Liquidity of Positions

Markets may become illiquid during periods of stress, and emerging market bonds and currencies, off-the-run government securities and certain over-the-counter derivatives may be difficult to close near their last valuation. Prices may move discontinuously, and stop-loss disciplines may not limit losses as intended.

### 9.10 Dependence on the Key Person

Trading decisions are discretionary and rely heavily on the judgment of Matthias Orlev. The loss of his services could materially and adversely affect the Master Fund, and a Key Person Event may result in substantial redemptions.

### 9.11 Master-Feeder Structure

Shareholders of the Offshore Feeder have different tax profiles and interests, and their redemptions may require the Master Fund to sell positions, increase the share of fixed expenses borne by the Fund and reduce diversification. Limited Partners have no direct rights against the Master Fund, which is governed by Cayman Islands law and its board. If the Fund withdraws from the Master Fund, it may receive securities in kind and incur transaction costs.

### 9.12 Effect of Substantial Redemptions

The Fund does not limit the aggregate amount of redemptions that may be effected as of any Redemption Date. Substantial redemptions by Limited Partners or by Offshore Feeder shareholders could require the Master Fund to liquidate positions more rapidly than desirable, reduce its asset base, increase the proportion of fixed expenses borne by remaining investors and impair the Investment Manager's ability to maintain the program.

### 9.13 Limited Liquidity of Interests

There is no market for the Interests, and Interests may not be transferred without the consent of the General Partner. Class A Interests redeemed within 12 months bear an early redemption charge, and Class B Interests may not be redeemed for 24 months. Redemptions may be suspended, and proceeds may be partly held back pending the annual audit.

### 9.14 Incentive Allocation

The Incentive Allocation may create an incentive for the Investment Manager to make riskier or more speculative investments. It is calculated on unrealized as well as realized gains, and may be allocated on gains never realized. Because the Loss Carryforward is maintained for each capital account, Limited Partners who invest at different times may bear different Incentive Allocations for the same Master Fund performance.

### 9.15 Regulatory Change

The regulation of derivatives and private funds in the United States, the European Union and the United Kingdom continues to evolve, including margin for uncleared swaps, mandatory clearing and trade reporting, speculative position limits and the phased central clearing of certain U.S. Treasury cash and repurchase transactions. Regulators may take emergency action, such as short sale restrictions or margin increases, with little notice, which may increase trading costs or prevent the Master Fund from implementing its program.

### 9.16 Operational and Cybersecurity Risk

The Investment Manager and service providers rely on information systems that may fail or be compromised by cyber attack, resulting in trading errors, loss of data, disclosure of investor information or disruption of operations.

### 9.17 Valuation Risk

Certain over-the-counter derivatives and less liquid bonds are valued using counterparty quotations or models, and the Administrator relies on information provided by the Investment Manager. Valuations may differ from realizable prices. Because the Management Fee and the Incentive Allocation are based on net asset value, the Investment Manager has a conflict of interest in valuing positions.

### 9.18 Tax Risk

A Limited Partner is taxed on its share of Fund income whether or not distributed, and the Fund does not expect to make distributions. Trading may generate substantial short-term capital gain and ordinary income. Schedules K-1 may not be available before April 15, and Limited Partners may need filing extensions.

### 9.19 Side Letters

Limited Partners with side letters may receive more favorable economic terms, more detailed portfolio information or notice of events that other Limited Partners do not receive, and may redeem on the basis of that information before others.

### 9.20 Concentration

The Master Fund is not subject to diversification requirements other than the restrictions in Section 3.4. At times a substantial portion of its risk may be concentrated in a small number of themes, markets or correlated positions, such as the direction of U.S. or European short-term interest rates, and a single adverse development could affect several positions at once.

### 9.21 Short Positions

The Master Fund takes short positions in futures, currencies, government bonds and index derivatives. A short position has theoretically unlimited loss potential, borrowed securities may be recalled at short notice, and the cost of borrowing may rise sharply. Regulators may restrict short sales during periods of stress.

### 9.22 Geopolitical Events and Market Disruption

Armed conflict, terrorism, sanctions, trade disputes, public health emergencies and elections may cause abrupt repricing, market closures, suspension of currency convertibility and failures of settlement systems. Macro positions are often intended to benefit from such developments, but the timing and direction of market responses are uncertain, and the Master Fund may incur substantial losses when events unfold differently from the Investment Manager's expectations.

### 9.23 Absence of Investment Company Act Protections

The Fund and the Master Fund are not registered under the Investment Company Act, and Limited Partners do not have the protections that Act provides, including limits on leverage, requirements for independent directors and restrictions on transactions with affiliates.

## SECTION 10. CONFLICTS OF INTEREST

The Investment Manager and its principals devote to the Master Fund such time as they consider necessary and may sponsor other vehicles with overlapping programs, including at lower fees. ## SECTION 11. CERTAIN U.S. FEDERAL INCOME TAX CONSIDERATIONS

### 11.1 General

The following summarizes certain U.S. federal income tax consequences of an investment by a U.S. person, based on the Internal Revenue Code of 1986, as amended (the "Code"), Treasury regulations, rulings and judicial decisions in effect on the date of this Memorandum, all of which may change, possibly retroactively. No ruling has been or will be sought from the Internal Revenue Service (the "IRS").

### 11.2 Classification of the Fund and the Master Fund

The Fund expects to be treated as a partnership, and not as a publicly traded partnership taxable as a corporation. The General Partner intends to restrict transfers and redemptions so that Interests are not readily tradable on a secondary market or its substantial equivalent, and in any event expects the Fund to satisfy the qualifying income exception of Section 7704(c) of the Code. The Master Fund has elected under Treasury Regulation Section 301.7701-3 to be classified as a partnership.

### 11.3 Taxation of Limited Partners

The Fund is not itself subject to U.S. federal income tax. Each Limited Partner takes into account its distributive share of the Fund's income, gain, loss and deduction, including the Fund's share of Master Fund items, whether or not distributed. On a redemption, a Limited Partner generally recognizes gain to the extent cash received exceeds its adjusted tax basis. The General Partner may make special allocations of gain or loss to redeeming Limited Partners to reduce differences between their capital accounts and tax basis, and the IRS may challenge such allocations.

### 11.4 Character of Income

A substantial portion of Master Fund positions are expected to be regulated futures contracts, foreign currency contracts and nonequity options described in Section 1256 of the Code. Section 1256 contracts are marked to market at year end, and resulting gain or loss is generally treated as 60% long-term and 40% short-term capital gain or loss regardless of holding period. Gain or loss on certain foreign currency transactions is ordinary under Section 988 of the Code unless an available election is made. Offsetting positions may be straddles under Section 1092 of the Code, which may defer losses, suspend holding periods and require capitalization of interest and carrying charges under Section 263(g). The Master Fund may make mixed straddle elections where available.

### 11.5 Trader Status and Limitations on Deductions

The Master Fund intends to take the position that it is engaged in the trade or business of trading securities and commodities for its own account. If respected, the Fund's share of Master Fund operating expenses should be deductible under Section 162 of the Code, although interest expense allocable to a Limited Partner that does not materially participate is subject to the investment interest limitation of Section 163(d), and losses may be subject to the excess business loss limitation of Section 461(l). If the Master Fund were treated as an investor, the Management Fee and other expenses would be Section 212 expenses, treated as miscellaneous itemized deductions that individuals may not deduct under current law. The Incentive Allocation is an allocation of income to the General Partner rather than a fee, and reduces the income allocated to Limited Partners.

### 11.6 Net Investment Income Tax

Individuals, estates and trusts are subject to a 3.8% tax under Section 1411 of the Code on net investment income above specified thresholds, which generally includes income from the trade or business of trading financial instruments or commodities.

### 11.7 Tax-Exempt Investors

A tax-exempt Limited Partner will realize unrelated business taxable income to the extent Master Fund income derives from debt-financed property within the meaning of Section 514 of the Code. Because the Master Fund borrows and enters into repurchase agreements, tax-exempt investors in the Fund should expect such income and generally invest through the Offshore Feeder instead.

### 11.8 Partnership Audit Procedures

The Fund is subject to the partnership audit procedures enacted by the Bipartisan Budget Act of 2015. The General Partner or its designee will act as partnership representative with authority to bind the Fund and its partners. An adjustment may produce an imputed underpayment payable by the Fund and borne by current Limited Partners who may not have been partners in the reviewed year, unless the Fund elects under Section 6226 of the Code to push the adjustment out to reviewed-year partners.

### 11.9 FATCA and Automatic Exchange of Information

The Master Fund is a financial institution under the intergovernmental agreement between the United States and the Cayman Islands and under the Common Reporting Standard, and reports information on its investors to the Cayman Islands Tax Information Authority. Limited Partners must provide information needed for compliance, and the General Partner may compulsorily redeem a Limited Partner that fails to do so.

### 11.10 Basis Adjustments

The General Partner may cause the Fund to make an election under Section 754 of the Code to adjust the basis of Fund property upon transfers of Interests and certain distributions. Absent an election, adjustments are mandatory where the Fund has a substantial built-in loss or a redemption results in a substantial basis reduction. These adjustments may affect the amount and timing of income allocated to Limited Partners and increase the administrative costs of the Fund.

### 11.11 Reportable Transactions

Treasury regulations require taxpayers to disclose participation in certain reportable transactions, including transactions generating losses above specified thresholds. A Limited Partner that is an individual or trust may be required to file Form 8886 if its share of losses from certain foreign currency transactions under Section 988 of the Code reaches $50,000 in a single taxable year, even if it has no net loss for the year. The Fund will seek to provide the information needed to determine whether a filing is required.

### 11.12 State and Local Taxes

Limited Partners may be subject to state and local income taxes where they reside. Many states exempt nonresident partners from tax on income from trading securities and commodities for a partnership's own account, but no assurance can be given that every jurisdiction does so.

## SECTION 12. ERISA CONSIDERATIONS

### 12.1 Fiduciary Considerations

Fiduciaries of plans subject to Title I of the Employee Retirement Income Security Act of 1974, as amended ("ERISA"), and of plans and individual retirement accounts subject to Section 4975 of the Code, should consider whether an investment in the Fund is prudent, satisfies ERISA's diversification requirements, is permitted by the plan's governing documents and is consistent with its liquidity needs, including the lock-up and redemption terms of the Fund, and should consider the unrelated business taxable income described in Section 11.7. None of the Fund, the General Partner or the Investment Manager undertakes to provide investment advice or act as a fiduciary in connection with any plan's decision to invest.

### 12.2 Plan Asset Regulation

Under Department of Labor Regulation Section 2510.3-101, as modified by Section 3(42) of ERISA (the "Plan Asset Regulation"), the assets of an entity in which a benefit plan investor acquires an equity interest are generally plan assets unless an exception applies. One exception applies if benefit plan investors hold less than 25% of the value of each class of equity interests, disregarding interests held by the General Partner, the Investment Manager and their affiliates. Benefit plan investors include plans subject to Title I of ERISA, plans and accounts subject to Section 4975 of the Code, and entities whose underlying assets include plan assets. The General Partner intends to limit benefit plan investors in each class of Interests to less than 25% (the "25% Limit"), and the Master Fund intends to apply the same limit, counting the Fund as a benefit plan investor to the extent of benefit plan participation in the Fund. The General Partner may refuse subscriptions or compulsorily redeem Interests to remain within the 25% Limit.

### 12.3 Consequences of Plan Asset Status

If the assets of the Fund or the Master Fund were plan assets, the Investment Manager would be a fiduciary with respect to investing plans, certain Master Fund transactions could be non-exempt prohibited transactions under Section 406 of ERISA or Section 4975 of the Code, and fiduciaries who authorized the investment could be liable for resulting losses.

### 12.4 Reporting of Indirect Compensation

The descriptions of the Management Fee, the Incentive Allocation and other compensation in this Memorandum are intended to satisfy the disclosure requirements for "eligible indirect compensation" for purposes of Schedule C of Form 5500.

### 12.5 Governmental and Other Plans

Governmental, church and non-U.S. plans are not subject to Title I of ERISA or Section 4975 of the Code but may be subject to similar laws, and their fiduciaries should consult counsel before investing.
