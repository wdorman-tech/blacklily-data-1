## SECTION 1. INTRODUCTION AND ADMINISTRATION

### 1.1 Purpose and Scope

Hollisbrook Venn Wealth Partners, LLC (the "Firm") is registered with the Securities and Exchange Commission (the "SEC") as an investment adviser under the Investment Advisers Act of 1940 (the "Advisers Act") and provides wealth management services to individuals, families, trusts and charitable organizations. This Compliance Policies and Procedures Manual (the "Manual") is adopted under Rule 206(4)-7 and is reasonably designed to prevent violations of the Advisers Act and the rules under it. "Supervised Person" means any member, officer or employee of the Firm and any other person who provides investment advice on the Firm's behalf and is subject to its supervision and control.

### 1.2 Designation and Duties of the Chief Compliance Officer

The Management Committee has designated Ilse Carrowmore as chief compliance officer (the "CCO"). The CCO administers this Manual and the Code of Ethics, interprets and enforces both, reports directly to the Management Committee and has unrestricted access to Firm personnel and records. The CCO chairs the Compliance Committee, consisting of the CCO, the Chief Executive Officer and the Chief Operating Officer, which meets at least quarterly to review compliance exceptions and regulatory developments. Although the CCO may delegate the performance of specific compliance functions to other Supervised Persons, responsibility for each delegated function remains with the CCO.

### 1.3 Adoption, Amendment and Annual Review

The Management Committee adopts this Manual on the recommendation of the CCO. The CCO reviews the adequacy of this Manual and the effectiveness of its implementation at least annually, as Rule 206(4)-7(b) requires, and on an interim basis after any material change in the Firm's business or in applicable law, and reports the results of each annual review in writing to the Management Committee. Each Supervised Person must certify on hire, on each amendment and at least annually that he or she has read this Manual and will comply with it. This Manual was most recently reviewed by the CCO and approved by the Management Committee in August 2026.

## SECTION 2. CODE OF ETHICS AND PERSONAL TRADING

### 2.1 Standards of Business Conduct

This Section 2 is the Firm's code of ethics under Rule 204A-1 (the "Code of Ethics"). Every Supervised Person must (a) comply with applicable federal securities laws; (b) place client interests ahead of his or her own and the Firm's; (c) avoid actual and potential conflicts of interest or disclose them to the CCO; and (d) not trade, personally or for others, while in possession of material nonpublic information, or communicate such information in breach of a duty. Supervised Persons must promptly report any violation of the Code of Ethics to the CCO, and the Firm will not retaliate against anyone for a report made in good faith. The CCO reviews every holdings and transaction report against client trading and pre-clearance records, and the Chief Executive Officer reviews the CCO's reports. Violations are reported to the Compliance Committee, which may impose sanctions up to and including termination of employment.

### 2.2 Access Persons and Reportable Securities

"Access Person" means any Supervised Person who has access to nonpublic information regarding client transactions or holdings or who is involved in making securities recommendations to clients; all portfolio management, trading and operations personnel are Access Persons. "Covered Account" means any account in which an Access Person has direct or indirect beneficial ownership, including accounts of a spouse or domestic partner, minor children and any relative sharing the household. "Reportable Security" means any security as defined in Section 202(a)(18) of the Advisers Act except (a) direct obligations of the United States government; (b) bankers' acceptances, bank certificates of deposit, commercial paper and high quality short-term debt instruments, including repurchase agreements; (c) money market fund shares; (d) shares of open-end funds, other than exchange-traded funds and funds advised by the Firm or an affiliate; and (e) units of unit investment trusts invested exclusively in funds described in clause (d).

### 2.3 Pre-Clearance of Personal Transactions

Except as provided in this Section 2.3 and in Section 2.4, an Access Person must obtain the CCO's approval through the Firm's compliance portal before effecting any purchase or sale of a Reportable Security in a Covered Account; the Chief Executive Officer pre-clears the CCO's own transactions. Pre-clearance will be denied if the Firm has a pending or contemplated client order in the same or an equivalent security, or if the transaction otherwise appears to conflict with client interests. Approval expires at the close of regular trading on the business day after it is granted. Pre-clearance is not required for a purchase or sale of common stock of an issuer with a market capitalization of at least $10 billion if the value of the transaction, aggregated with all other same-day transactions in that issuer across the Access Person's Covered Accounts, does not exceed $10,000. Acquisitions in an initial public offering or a limited offering, including any private fund, require pre-clearance in every case.

### 2.4 Transactions Exempt from Pre-Clearance

The following are exempt from pre-clearance under Section 2.3 but remain subject to Section 2.5 except as that Section provides: (a) purchases under an automatic investment plan, including a dividend reinvestment plan, provided any change to the plan is pre-cleared; (b) non-volitional transactions, including stock splits, mergers, spin-offs, bequests, the exercise of rights offered pro rata to all holders of a class, and sales by a broker to meet a margin call the Access Person did not cause; (c) purchases and sales of shares of exchange-traded funds, other than an exchange-traded fund that seeks a leveraged or inverse multiple of the daily return of an index; and (d) transactions in a Covered Account over which the Access Person has no direct or indirect influence or control, such as an account managed with full discretion by an unaffiliated adviser, if the Access Person certifies annually that he or she has not directed or suggested any transaction in it.

### 2.5 Holdings and Transaction Reports

Each Access Person must submit through the compliance portal (a) an initial holdings report no later than ten (10) days after becoming an Access Person, current as of a date not more than forty-five (45) days before that date; (b) a quarterly transaction report no later than fifteen (15) calendar days after the end of each calendar quarter, disclosing for each transaction in a Reportable Security in a Covered Account during the quarter the date, title, ticker or CUSIP, interest rate and maturity (if applicable), number of shares or principal amount, nature of the transaction, price, and the broker, dealer or bank that effected it; and (c) an annual holdings report no later than February 14 of each year, current as of December 31 of the prior year. Each holdings report must list every Reportable Security in which the Access Person has any beneficial ownership and each broker, dealer or bank at which a Covered Account is held. Duplicate confirmations and statements received through the portal's broker feed satisfy clause (b) if they contain all required information and arrive within the same period. No report is required for a Covered Account over which the Access Person has no direct or indirect influence or control, and no transaction report is required for transactions under an automatic investment plan. A late report violates the Code of Ethics even if the underlying transactions were permitted.

## SECTION 3. GIFTS AND ENTERTAINMENT

### 3.1 Limits on Gifts and Entertainment

No Supervised Person may give or accept any gift or other thing of value that could reasonably be expected to influence the recipient's judgment in dealing with the Firm or its clients. Without the CCO's prior approval, no Supervised Person may accept gifts with an aggregate value exceeding $250 per calendar year from any one person or entity doing business with the Firm, or give gifts exceeding that amount to any one client, prospective client or service provider. Cash and cash equivalents, including gift cards, may never be given or accepted. Business entertainment at which the host is present is permitted if it is neither so frequent nor so lavish as to raise a question of propriety, but entertainment above $500 per person per event requires prior CCO approval, as does any gift to or entertainment of a government or labor union official.

### 3.2 Gift and Entertainment Log

Each Supervised Person must record on the Firm's gift and entertainment log (the "Gift Log"), within ten (10) business days, each gift given or received and each business entertainment event given or attended in connection with the Firm's business, with the date, giver, recipient, description and actual or estimated value. Gifts received must be recorded whether or not they are retained. The CCO reviews the Gift Log quarterly for patterns suggesting a conflict of interest, particularly gifts from custodians and product sponsors.

## SECTION 4. PORTFOLIO MANAGEMENT AND TRADING

### 4.1 Suitability and Client Profile Reviews

Before advising a client, the Firm documents the client's investment objectives, risk tolerance, time horizon, liquidity needs, tax status and investment restrictions (the "Client Profile"). Portfolio managers must manage each account consistently with its Client Profile, any investment policy statement and any written restrictions. Wealth advisors must contact each client at least annually to confirm the Client Profile and must record the review. A recommendation to roll over assets from an employer-sponsored retirement plan or individual retirement account to an account the Firm manages must document why it is in the client's best interest, including a comparison of fees, services and investment options.

### 4.2 Trade Aggregation and Allocation

Portfolio managers may aggregate orders in the same security for multiple accounts where aggregation is consistent with the Firm's duty to seek best execution. Before an aggregated order is placed, the trader records the participating accounts and the intended allocation in the order management system. Each participating account receives the average price for the day. Partially filled orders are allocated pro rata across participating accounts on the basis of the intended allocation, except that the portfolio manager may reallocate to avoid odd lots or de minimis positions if the reason is documented. No account of a Supervised Person may be favored over a client account in the same order, and any other deviation from the intended allocation requires the CCO's written approval before the end of the trading day.

### 4.3 Trade Error Correction

A trade error is any error in the placement, execution or allocation of a client transaction, including trading the wrong security, quantity or account. A Supervised Person who discovers a trade error must report it to the CCO and the Chief Operating Officer on the day of discovery, and the error must be corrected as soon as practicable without disadvantaging the client. If correction of a trade error results in a loss, the Firm shall reimburse the affected client account for the amount of the loss, so that the client is placed in the position it would have occupied had the error not occurred. Gains from the correction of an error remain in the client account and may not be netted against losses in any other account. The CCO keeps a trade error log recording each error, its cause, its correction and any loss or gain, and reports each loss exceeding $10,000 to the Compliance Committee.

## SECTION 5. COMMUNICATIONS, MARKETING AND RECORDS

### 5.1 Marketing Communications

Every advertisement, as defined in Rule 206(4)-1 (the "Marketing Rule"), must be approved in writing by the CCO before first use, including website content, presentations and performance summaries. No advertisement may contain an untrue statement of material fact, a material statement of fact the Firm cannot substantiate on demand by the SEC, or any endorsement, client statement or third-party rating unless the conditions the Marketing Rule imposes on it are met. Gross performance may be shown only with net performance presented with at least equal prominence, and portfolio performance must include one-, five- and ten-year periods, or the life of the portfolio if shorter.

### 5.2 Electronic Communications

All email relating to the Firm's business must be sent and received through the Firm's email system, which captures and archives every message. The CCO or a designee reviews a risk-based sample of archived email each month. Supervised Persons have no expectation of privacy in communications made through Firm systems or devices. Use of social media for the Firm's business, including any profile describing a Supervised Person's role at the Firm, requires the CCO's prior approval, and no Supervised Person may recommend securities or communicate with clients through social media except as the CCO has approved.

### 5.3 Books and Records

The Firm makes and keeps true, accurate and current the books and records required by Rule 204-2, including journals and ledgers, order memoranda, custodial statements, written communications relating to advice given or proposed and to the placing or execution of orders, each advertisement and its substantiating records, and Code of Ethics records. Records are maintained in an easily accessible place for at least five years from the end of the fiscal year in which the last entry was made, the first two years in an appropriate office of the Firm. No Supervised Person may destroy, alter or discard any Firm record other than as this Section permits.

### 5.4 Client Complaints

A "complaint" is any written or oral statement of a client or a person acting for a client alleging a grievance involving the Firm or any Supervised Person. A Supervised Person who receives a complaint must report it to the CCO within one (1) business day and may not attempt to resolve it, offer compensation or make any admission without the CCO's approval. The CCO records each complaint in the complaint log with the date received, the client, the allegation, the investigation performed and the resolution, and responds to the client in writing. Any complaint alleging fraud, forgery or misappropriation of client assets is reported immediately to the Chief Executive Officer and outside counsel.

### 5.5 Off-Channel Messaging Prohibition

Written business communications may be conducted only through the Firm's email system, its client portal, the archived messaging application installed on Firm-issued mobile devices and any social media account approved under Section 5.2 (the "Approved Channels"). Business communication through any other channel, including personal email, text messages from a personal number, WhatsApp, Signal and personal social media accounts (an "Off-Channel Communication"), is prohibited, whether the other party is a client, a colleague or a service provider. A Supervised Person who receives an Off-Channel Communication relating to the Firm's business must not respond substantively on that channel, must move the conversation to an Approved Channel, and must preserve the message in the Firm's archive and notify the CCO within one (1) business day. Each Supervised Person certifies compliance with this Section 5.5 quarterly.

## SECTION 6. PRIVACY AND INFORMATION SECURITY

### 6.1 Privacy and Safeguarding of Customer Information

The Firm is subject to Regulation S-P. It delivers its privacy notice to each client who is a natural person when the relationship is established and, because it shares nonpublic personal information with nonaffiliated third parties only as the Regulation S-P exceptions permit and has not changed its practices, relies on the exception from annual notice delivery. The Firm's information security program, administered by the Chief Operating Officer under the CCO's oversight, includes role-based access controls, multi-factor authentication for remote access, encryption of client data, annual security training and oversight of service providers that access customer information. Supervised Persons must immediately report any actual or suspected unauthorized access to customer information to the Chief Operating Officer and the CCO, and must also observe the confidentiality obligations in Section 6.2.

### 6.2 Confidentiality of Client Information

Supervised Persons must treat all client information, including identities, financial condition, holdings, transactions and estate planning matters, as confidential. Client information may be used only to service the client and may be disclosed only (a) to Supervised Persons who need it, (b) to custodians, service providers and the client's other professional advisers as necessary to service the account and as the client has authorized, or (c) as required by law or legal process, after consultation with the CCO. Supervised Persons must secure paper files at the end of each business day and must not discuss client matters in public places.

### 6.3 Security Incident Response Program

As required by Regulation S-P, as amended, the Firm maintains an incident response program reasonably designed to detect, respond to and recover from unauthorized access to or use of customer information. For each incident, the Chief Operating Officer and the CCO assess its nature and scope, identify the systems and types of customer information involved, and take steps to contain and control it. Unless the Firm determines after a reasonable investigation that sensitive customer information has not been and is not reasonably likely to be used in a manner that would result in substantial harm or inconvenience, the Firm notifies each affected individual as soon as practicable, and no later than thirty (30) days after becoming aware that unauthorized access has occurred or is reasonably likely to have occurred. Service provider contracts require notice to the Firm within seventy-two (72) hours after the provider becomes aware of a breach affecting a customer information system it maintains.

## SECTION 7. OUTSIDE ACTIVITIES AND BUSINESS CONTINUITY

### 7.1 Outside Business Activities

No Supervised Person may engage in any outside business activity without the prior written approval of the CCO. Outside business activities include serving as an officer, director, partner or employee of any other business, with or without compensation; acting as trustee, executor or attorney-in-fact for anyone other than a family member; and holding any securities or insurance license other than through the Firm. Requests must describe the activity, the time commitment, any compensation and any relationship between the outside organization and the Firm or its clients. Approval may be conditioned or withdrawn at any time, and approved activities are disclosed on Form U4 where required.

### 7.2 Business Continuity Planning

The Firm maintains a written business continuity plan (the "BCP") designed to allow it to continue meeting its obligations to clients during a significant business disruption, including loss of office access, failure of critical systems or the unavailability of key personnel. The Chief Operating Officer serves as business continuity lead (the "Continuity Lead"). The BCP identifies critical functions, alternate work arrangements, data backup and recovery, and succession arrangements for key personnel. The Continuity Lead maintains an emergency contact list for Supervised Persons and critical service providers and updates it semi-annually. The BCP is exercised at least annually, and the Continuity Lead reports the results to the Compliance Committee. During a disruption, the Continuity Lead has authority to invoke the BCP.

### 7.3 Diminished Capacity and Trusted Contact Persons

At account opening and at each annual Client Profile review under Section 4.1, wealth advisors must ask each client who is a natural person to name a trusted contact person and must record the contact information provided or the client's refusal. With the client's authorization, the Firm may contact that person to confirm the client's contact information or health status, to identify any guardian, trustee or agent under a power of attorney, or to address possible financial exploitation. A Supervised Person who reasonably believes that a client is experiencing diminished capacity or financial exploitation must escalate the matter to the CCO within one (1) business day, and the CCO determines whether to contact the trusted contact person, ask the custodian to delay a disbursement or notify adult protective services.
