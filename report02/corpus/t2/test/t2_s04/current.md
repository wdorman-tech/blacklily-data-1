## SECTION 1. GENERAL PROVISIONS

### 1.1 Scope and Application

Quarrendon Capital Management LP (the "Adviser") is registered with the Securities and Exchange Commission (the "SEC") under the Investment Advisers Act of 1940, as amended (the "Advisers Act"), and manages Quarrendon Event-Driven Master Fund, Ltd. and its feeder funds, Quarrendon Event-Driven Fund, LP and Quarrendon Event-Driven Offshore Fund, Ltd. (collectively, the "Funds"). This compliance manual (the "Manual") sets out the policies and procedures the Adviser has adopted under Rule 206(4)-7 under the Advisers Act and applies to every partner, officer and employee of the Adviser and to any consultant or temporary worker the Chief Compliance Officer (the "CCO") designates (each, an "Employee"). The CCO administers and interprets the Manual and reports to the Managing Partner. Where this Manual and applicable law differ, the stricter standard governs.

### 1.2 Annual Review and Amendment

The CCO will review the adequacy of this Manual and the effectiveness of its implementation at least annually, taking into account compliance matters that arose during the year, changes in the Adviser's business, and changes in the Advisers Act and the rules under it. The CCO will deliver a written report of each review to the Managing Partner. The next annual review will be completed on or before February 28, 2027. The CCO may amend this Manual at any time and will distribute each material amendment to all Employees for acknowledgment under Section 1.3.

### 1.3 Acknowledgments and Certifications

Each Employee must acknowledge in writing, within ten days of commencing employment, that he or she has received, read and understood this Manual. Within thirty days after the end of each calendar year, each Employee must certify in writing that he or she has complied with this Manual during that year, has reported every personal securities transaction and holding required by Section 3.4, and has disclosed every outside business activity required by Section 3.5. Each material amendment must be acknowledged within ten business days after its distribution. An Employee who fails to deliver a required acknowledgment or certification will be barred from personal trading until it is delivered.

## SECTION 2. MATERIAL NONPUBLIC INFORMATION

### 2.1 Prohibition on Insider Trading

Section 204A of the Advisers Act requires the Adviser to maintain and enforce written policies reasonably designed to prevent the misuse of material nonpublic information. No Employee may buy or sell a security for any account while in possession of material nonpublic information about the security or its issuer, or communicate such information to any person except under Section 2.4. Information is material if a reasonable investor would likely consider it important to an investment decision or if its disclosure would likely affect the price of the security, and is nonpublic until it has been broadly disseminated, such as by a wire service release or an SEC filing, and the market has had time to absorb it. The prohibition extends to an issuer's debt, swaps and other derivatives, and to the securities of any other issuer, such as a competitor, supplier or acquirer, whose price the information could reasonably be expected to affect. An Employee who believes he or she may have received material nonpublic information must immediately stop trading in the issuer's securities, must not discuss it with any other Employee, and must report it to the CCO.

### 2.2 Restricted List Procedure

The CCO maintains a list of issuers in whose securities trading is prohibited (the "Restricted List"). Compliance will add an issuer to the Restricted List when (a) an Employee reports under Section 2.1 that he or she may have received material nonpublic information about it; (b) the Adviser agrees to be wall-crossed under Section 2.4; (c) an Employee serves as a director or officer of the issuer or on a creditors' committee in its restructuring; or (d) the Adviser signs a confidentiality agreement with the issuer or its agent. Each addition is entered in the order management system and the compliance system, which then block Fund orders and pre-clearance requests in every security and derivative of the issuer. The Restricted List is circulated to the trading desk before each market open. An issuer may be removed from the Restricted List only by the CCO, once the CCO has determined, after consulting the Employee who reported the information, that the information has been publicly disseminated or is no longer material, or that the transaction has been abandoned and any standstill period has expired. Each addition and removal, its reason and the person approving it are recorded in the Restricted List log.

### 2.3 Watch List

The CCO also maintains a confidential list of issuers about which the Adviser may hold sensitive information but in whose securities trading is not prohibited (the "Watch List"), such as an issuer with which a wall-crossing is under discussion. The Watch List is not distributed to the trading desk or to investment personnel, and its contents are known only to Compliance. Compliance reviews all trading in Watch List issuers by the Funds and in Covered Accounts each business day and may move an issuer to the Restricted List at any time without notice.

### 2.4 Information Barriers and Wall-Crossing

A placement agent, underwriter, issuer or lender that proposes to give the Adviser confidential information about a prospective offering, private investment in public equity, financing or restructuring must be referred to the CCO, and only the CCO or a designee in Compliance may agree to a wall-crossing on the Adviser's behalf. Before agreeing, the CCO will confirm the issuer, the expected duration and the Employees who will receive the information, and will add the issuer to the Restricted List under Section 2.2. Employees brought over the wall must keep the information on a need-to-know basis and remain restricted until the CCO gives them written notice that the restriction has ended. Because the Adviser's investment functions are not physically separated, the Adviser relies on the Restricted List, rather than on information barriers, to prevent trading while any Employee holds material nonpublic information.

### 2.5 Expert Network Consultations

An Employee may speak with a paid industry expert, whether engaged through an expert network firm or directly (a "Consultant"), only as this Section 2.5 permits. Each expert network firm must be approved by the CCO and must agree in writing to screen its experts against the Adviser's restrictions. Each consultation must be pre-cleared through the compliance system at least one business day in advance, identifying the Consultant, his or her employers during the preceding three years and the issuers to be discussed. No Employee may consult a current employee, officer or director of an issuer about that issuer, a person who left an issuer less than six months earlier about that issuer, or a current government employee. Each call opens with the Adviser's compliance script, and Compliance chaperones a sample of calls without notice. An Employee who believes a Consultant has disclosed material nonpublic information must end the call and report it under Section 2.1.

## SECTION 3. CODE OF ETHICS AND PERSONAL TRADING

### 3.1 Covered Accounts and Reportable Securities

This SECTION 3 is the Adviser's code of ethics under Rule 204A-1 under the Advisers Act, and every Employee is treated as an access person. A "Covered Account" is any account in which an Employee has a direct or indirect beneficial interest, including accounts of a spouse, domestic partner or other relative sharing the Employee's household, and any account over which the Employee exercises investment discretion. A "Reportable Security" is any security as defined in Section 202(a)(18) of the Advisers Act, including private placements, limited partnership interests, exchange-traded funds and options, but excluding direct obligations of the United States government, bank certificates of deposit, commercial paper, high-quality short-term debt instruments, money market fund shares and shares of open-end funds the Adviser does not advise. Every brokerage Covered Account must be held at a firm that delivers duplicate statements, or an electronic feed, to Compliance.

### 3.2 Pre-Clearance of Personal Trades

No Employee may effect a transaction in a Reportable Security in a Covered Account unless it has first been pre-cleared through the Adviser's electronic compliance system. Pre-clearance will be denied if the issuer is on the Restricted List or if any Fund holds, has a pending order in, or has traded within the preceding seven calendar days, any security of the issuer. A purchase in an initial public offering or a private placement also requires the separate written approval of the CCO. Pre-clearance approval is valid until the close of regular trading on the day on which it is granted, and an order not executed within that period, including an open limit order, must be cancelled and pre-cleared again. The CCO's own transactions are pre-cleared by the Managing Partner. Transactions under an automatic investment plan, or in an account over which the Employee has no influence or control, need not be pre-cleared but remain reportable under Section 3.4.

### 3.3 Minimum Holding Period

A Reportable Security acquired in a Covered Account must be held for at least 60 calendar days from the trade date before it is sold, and the same period applies to covering a short position and closing an options position. Holding periods are measured on a last-in, first-out basis across all of the Employee's Covered Accounts. Profits realized in violation of this Section 3.3 must be disgorged to a charity the CCO selects. The CCO may grant a written hardship exception where the Employee shows an unforeseen financial need and the transaction presents no actual or apparent conflict with any Fund, but no exception will be granted for a security any Fund holds.

### 3.4 Holdings and Transaction Reports

Each Employee must submit an initial holdings report within 10 days after becoming an Employee and an annual holdings report each year, each current as of a date not more than 45 days before submission, and a quarterly transaction report within 30 days after the end of each calendar quarter covering every transaction in a Reportable Security and every Covered Account opened during the quarter. Duplicate statements received by Compliance within the same period satisfy the quarterly requirement if they contain all information Rule 204A-1 requires. Compliance reviews each report against pre-clearance records and Fund trading, and the General Counsel reviews the CCO's reports.

### 3.5 Outside Business Activities

No Employee may, without the prior written approval of the CCO, serve as a director, officer, general partner, trustee, employee or consultant of any business organization other than the Adviser, whether or not compensated, or engage in any other business activity outside the scope of his or her employment. Service on the board of a charitable or civic organization that does not do business with the Adviser requires notice to the CCO rather than approval. The CCO will weigh the time commitment, any compensation, the likelihood of receiving material nonpublic information and any conflict with the Funds. If an Employee is approved to serve as a director of a public company, Compliance will place that issuer on the Restricted List under Section 2.2 for the duration of the service. Each Employee must update his or her disclosure within ten business days after any change.

## SECTION 4. PORTFOLIO TRADING

### 4.1 Best Execution and Broker Review

The Adviser seeks best execution for every Fund transaction, meaning the most favorable total cost or proceeds reasonably obtainable under the circumstances, not necessarily the lowest commission. Traders consider price, commission, market impact, speed and certainty of execution, financial stability, settlement reliability, confidentiality and the value of research provided. The brokerage committee, consisting of the Head of Trading, the Chief Operating Officer and the CCO, meets semi-annually to review each approved broker against these factors, to consider transaction cost analysis prepared by the trading desk, and to assess the reasonableness of commission rates in relation to the brokerage and research services received. Research paid for with Fund commissions must fall within Section 28(e) of the Securities Exchange Act of 1934.

### 4.2 Trade Aggregation and Allocation

Orders for the same security on the same side of the market may be aggregated across the Funds and any other client account where consistent with best execution and each account's guidelines. Before an aggregated order is placed, the portfolio manager must record a written pre-allocation. A filled order is allocated according to the pre-allocation and a partially filled order pro rata to it, at the average price with commissions shared pro rata. Any departure from the pre-allocation must be approved by the CCO before the end of the trade date and documented. Limited opportunities, including new issues, are allocated in proportion to account net asset value, subject to each account's guidelines and to FINRA Rules 5130 and 5131.

### 4.3 Trade Error Correction

A trade error is a mistake in the placement, execution, allocation or settlement of a Fund transaction that results from the Adviser's failure to follow the Fund's instructions or guidelines, such as buying instead of selling, trading the wrong security or quantity, or breaching an investment restriction. A good faith error of investment judgment is not a trade error. An Employee who discovers a trade error must report it immediately to the Head of Trading and the CCO, who will direct its correction as soon as practicable. Errors may not be corrected by reallocating trades to another account, and gains and losses on separate errors may not be netted. The Adviser will reimburse the affected Fund for any net loss resulting from a trade error, other than a loss caused by an executing broker or counterparty that was not acting on inaccurate or incomplete instructions from the Adviser, which the Adviser will pursue against that party on the Fund's behalf. Fund commissions and soft dollars may never be used to compensate for an error. The CCO records each error, its cause, the correction and its cost in the trade error log.

### 4.4 Cross Trades Between Client Accounts

The Adviser may cause a Fund to buy a security from, or sell a security to, another account the Adviser manages (a "cross trade") only if the portfolio manager determines that the trade is in the best interest of both accounts, and the CCO approves it in writing before execution. A cross trade must be executed at the independent current market price, being the last reported sale price or, absent one, the average of the current independent bid and offer, and no commission or other remuneration may be paid to the Adviser or any affiliate. No cross trade may be effected with an account subject to the Employee Retirement Income Security Act of 1974, or with any account in which the Adviser and its Employees own more than 25 percent.

### 4.5 Short Sales and Regulation M

Every short sale by a Fund must comply with Regulation SHO. Before a short sale order is placed, the trader must obtain a locate from the executing broker and record it in the order management system. No Fund may purchase equity securities from an underwriter or other distribution participant in a firm commitment offering for cash if the Fund sold the same security short during the restricted period under Rule 105 of Regulation M, generally the five business days before pricing, unless the CCO confirms in writing before the purchase that an exception applies. Compliance reviews Fund short sales each business day against a calendar of announced offerings.

## SECTION 5. VALUATION

### 5.1 Valuation Committee

The Adviser's valuation committee (the "Valuation Committee") consists of the Chief Financial Officer, as chair, the Chief Operating Officer, the CCO and the Head of Risk, each a voting member; three voting members form a quorum. Portfolio managers may present positions they manage but are not members and may not vote. The Valuation Committee meets at least monthly before net asset value is finalized, approves and annually reviews the Adviser's valuation policy, selects and monitors pricing sources, reviews every Level 3 valuation and every stale or exception price, and reviews overrides applied under Section 5.3.

### 5.2 Pricing Hierarchy

The Funds' administrator, Corranvale Fund Services (Cayman) Ltd. (the "Administrator"), calculates net asset value independently of the Adviser using prices obtained in the following order. Listed securities are valued at the official closing price on their principal exchange and listed options at the mean of the closing bid and ask. Over-the-counter securities and derivatives are valued at the price supplied by an independent pricing service the Valuation Committee has approved. A position no pricing service covers is valued at the mean of quotations from at least two independent broker-dealers, and where fewer than two quotations are available, at fair value determined by the Valuation Committee under a documented methodology. Each day, the Operations Department reconciles the Administrator's prices to the Adviser's records and investigates any single-position difference above 2 percent.

### 5.3 Valuation Overrides

A portfolio manager who believes that a price obtained under Section 5.2 does not reflect fair value may request an override by delivering to the Operations Department a written request stating the proposed price, its basis and the supporting evidence. The Head of Operations will review the request and forward it, with a recommendation, to the Valuation Committee, which may approve or reject it by the vote of a majority of its voting members, including the CCO. No override may be applied to the net asset value of any Fund until the Valuation Committee has approved it in writing. No portfolio manager may approve an override of a position he or she manages. Each override is recorded in the valuation override log with the request, the recommendation and the approval, and the Administrator is notified before net asset value is finalized.

## SECTION 6. OTHER CONDUCT POLICIES

### 6.1 Political Contributions

Rule 206(4)-5 under the Advisers Act bars the Adviser from receiving compensation for advising a government entity for two years after the Adviser or any covered associate contributes to an official of that entity. Every partner, executive officer and person who solicits investors for the Adviser, and every other Employee the CCO designates, is a "Covered Associate". A Covered Associate may not contribute more than $350 per election to a state or local official or candidate for whom he or she is entitled to vote, or $150 per election to any other such official or candidate, and may not solicit or coordinate contributions to any such official or candidate. Before contributing to any state or local official, candidate, party or political action committee, a Covered Associate must obtain pre-clearance from the CCO through the compliance system. Each new Covered Associate must disclose all contributions made in the preceding two years.

### 6.2 Books and Records

The Adviser maintains the books and records Rule 204-2 under the Advisers Act requires for at least five years from the end of the fiscal year in which the last entry was made, the first two years in an office of the Adviser. Electronic records must be indexed for prompt retrieval, protected against alteration and loss, and capable of prompt production to the SEC. Business communications of Employees by email, instant message and every other approved electronic channel are captured by the Adviser's third-party archiving service (the "Archive Provider"), which retains them in non-rewritable, non-erasable form. Compliance reviews a risk-based sample of communications held by the Archive Provider each month, supplemented by lexicon surveillance. No Employee may delete or alter any record, and all destruction is suspended for records subject to a legal hold issued by the CCO.
