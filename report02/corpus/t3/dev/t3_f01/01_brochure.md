### 4.2 Wealth Management Services

We provide continuous and regular supervisory and management services to high-net-worth individuals and families through separately managed accounts held in each client's name at an unaffiliated qualified custodian. We do not pool client assets. The client's wealth advisor gathers information about the household's assets and liabilities, cash flow requirements, tax position, estate plan, time horizon and tolerance for risk, and we document the resulting objectives, asset allocation targets and restrictions in a written Investment Policy Statement that the client signs.

We construct each portfolio using the strategies described in Item 4.3 and monitor and rebalance it as described in Item 13. The service includes tax-aware implementation, including tax-loss harvesting and asset location, coordination with the client's accountant and estate attorney, and ongoing financial planning. Investment decisions for our model strategies are made by our Investment Policy Committee, which is chaired by Mr. Arlow and meets monthly, and wealth advisors implement those decisions subject to each client's Investment Policy Statement.

### 4.3 Investment Strategies Offered

We manage client portfolios using the following strategies, alone or in combination:

- **Core Equity.** A portfolio of 40 to 55 common stocks of U.S. large- and mid-capitalization companies selected through fundamental research.
- **Dividend Growth Equity.** A portfolio of 35 to 50 companies with a record of increasing their dividends and the balance sheet capacity to continue doing so.
- **Tax-Managed Municipal.** Laddered portfolios of investment-grade municipal bonds, generally rated A or better at purchase, with maturities of one to fifteen years, which may emphasize issuers in the client's state of residence.
- **Taxable Fixed Income.** Portfolios of U.S. Treasury and agency securities, investment-grade corporate bonds and brokered certificates of deposit.
- **Global Allocation Models.** Diversified portfolios of exchange-traded funds and mutual funds offered in five risk profiles: Conservative, Moderate Conservative, Moderate, Moderate Growth and Growth.

## Item 5. Fees and Compensation

### 5.1 Wealth Management Fee Schedule

Wealth management clients pay an annual asset-based fee calculated on a tiered basis and applied to the aggregate assets of the household under our management, as follows:

| Household assets under management | Annual fee rate |
|---|---|
| First $1,000,000 | 1.00% |
| Next $2,000,000 (over $1,000,000 up to $3,000,000) | 0.80% |
| Next $2,000,000 (over $3,000,000 up to $5,000,000) | 0.65% |
| Next $5,000,000 (over $5,000,000 up to $10,000,000) | 0.50% |
| Over $10,000,000 | 0.35% |

Because the schedule is tiered, each rate applies only to the assets within its tier. For example, a household with $4,000,000 of assets under management pays an annual fee of $32,500: 1.00% on the first $1,000,000 ($10,000), 0.80% on the next $2,000,000 ($16,000) and 0.65% on the remaining $1,000,000 ($6,500). The annual fee is allocated among the household's accounts in proportion to their values unless the client instructs us to bill a single account.

### 5.2 Billing Method

Fees are billed quarterly in advance, based on the market value of the assets in each account on the last business day of the preceding calendar quarter. Market values are the values reported by the custodian or, for assets the custodian does not price and for private fund interests and other assets without observable market prices, the values determined under our Valuation Policy. Unless a client elects to be invoiced, the client authorizes the custodian in the advisory agreement to deduct our fee directly from the account, and the deduction appears on the custodian's statement. The fee for the first partial quarter is calculated pro rata from the date the account is funded. Additions or withdrawals of $100,000 or more in a single transaction during a quarter result in a pro rata charge or credit on the next quarterly invoice. Smaller flows are not adjusted.

### 5.3 Household Aggregation

For purposes of applying the fee schedule, we aggregate the accounts of a household, which includes the client, the client's spouse or domestic partner, their dependent children, and trusts, foundations and entities of which one or more of them is the grantor, primary beneficiary or controlling owner. Aggregation lowers the blended fee rate. It is the client's responsibility to tell us about related accounts that may qualify for aggregation. Assets in a Fixed Income Mandate are billed under the Fixed Income Mandate schedule and are not included in the household total used to apply the wealth management schedule.

### 5.4 Minimums and Negotiability

The minimum annual fee for a wealth management household is $10,000. The Managing Partner may waive or reduce the account minimum and the minimum annual fee, for example for family members of existing clients. Our fees are negotiable. Households that became clients under earlier fee schedules and employees of the Firm may pay different fees, and comparable services may be available from other advisers at lower cost.

### 5.5 Fixed Income Mandates and Financial Planning Fees

Clients who engage us to manage a standalone Tax-Managed Municipal or Taxable Fixed Income portfolio (a "Fixed Income Mandate") pay 0.40% annually on the first $5,000,000 and 0.30% on assets above $5,000,000. The minimum Fixed Income Mandate is $2,000,000. Standalone financial planning engagements are charged a fixed fee between $5,000 and $25,000, depending on the complexity of the client's circumstances and the scope of the engagement; 50% of the fee is due when the engagement letter is signed and the balance is due when the written plan is delivered. Hourly consulting is charged at $400 per hour.

### 5.6 Other Fees and Expenses

Clients bear separately the charges imposed by custodians and other third parties, including transaction charges on certain trades, dealer markups and markdowns on fixed income trades, wire and transfer fees, and account termination fees. Mutual funds, exchange-traded funds and private funds charge management fees and other expenses, disclosed in their offering documents, that clients bear indirectly in addition to our fee. Uninvested cash is held in the custodian's sweep program, on which the custodian earns compensation. We receive no portion of any of these fees or of any sweep program compensation.

### 5.7 Termination and Refunds

Either party may terminate the advisory agreement upon 30 days' written notice. Prepaid fees are refunded on a pro rata basis for the number of days remaining in the quarter after the effective date of termination, and refunds are paid within 15 business days. ## Item 8. Methods of Analysis, Investment Strategies and Risk of Loss

### 8.1 Methods of Analysis

For individual equity securities, we use fundamental analysis of a company's financial statements, earnings quality, competitive position, management, capital allocation and valuation. For fixed income securities, we use credit analysis, which for municipal bonds includes the issuer's revenue base, debt service coverage and call provisions. For funds, we evaluate the manager's process, organization, fees, liquidity terms and operational controls. We set strategic asset allocations using capital market assumptions that the Investment Policy Committee reviews at least annually. Our sources include company filings, credit rating agency reports, third-party research, offering documents and the financial press.

### 8.2 Investment Strategies

Our approach is long-term. We construct portfolios around strategic asset allocation targets set in each Investment Policy Statement, and the Investment Policy Committee may tilt model allocations by up to three percentage points relative to those targets when it believes valuations warrant. We generally hold individual securities for several years, but we may sell a position within a shorter period when our assessment changes, to harvest a loss, or to raise cash. We do not use margin, sell securities short or write uncovered options in client accounts, although we may write covered call options against concentrated positions at a client's request.

### 8.3 General Risk of Loss

Investing in securities involves risk of loss that clients should be prepared to bear. We do not guarantee the performance of any account or the achievement of any objective, and past performance is not indicative of future results. The following risks are the principal risks of our strategies; not every risk applies to every account.

### 8.4 Principal Risks

**Market risk.** The value of securities may decline because of general market conditions, changes in interest rates or inflation, economic or political developments, or investor sentiment, which may affect an entire market or sector regardless of the merits of an individual issuer.

**Equity securities and concentration risk.** Common stocks are subordinate to an issuer's debt and may lose value rapidly. The Core Equity and Dividend Growth Equity strategies hold a limited number of positions, and an account that holds a concentrated legacy position may be more volatile than a diversified portfolio.

**Interest rate and call risk.** The value of fixed income securities generally falls when interest rates rise, and longer maturities are more sensitive. Issuers of callable bonds may redeem them before maturity when rates fall, and the proceeds may be reinvested at lower yields.

**Credit risk.** An issuer may fail to pay interest or principal when due, or its credit rating may be lowered, reducing the value of its securities.

**Municipal securities risk.** Municipal bonds may be affected by the financial condition of the issuing state or locality, changes in tax laws that reduce the value of tax exemption, and legislative or political developments. Portfolios that emphasize a single state are more exposed to that state's economy and fiscal position.

**Liquidity risk.** Some securities, including certain municipal bonds, brokered certificates of deposit and all private fund interests, trade infrequently or not at all, and may be sold only at a discount or after a delay.

**Fund risk.** Exchange-traded funds and mutual funds are subject to the risks of their underlying holdings and to their own fees, and exchange-traded funds may trade at a premium or discount to net asset value.

**Private fund risk.** Private funds are speculative and illiquid. Investors may be required to meet capital calls over several years, may be unable to redeem or transfer their interests, and may receive Schedule K-1 tax reporting late. Private fund values are reported with a delay and are based on the manager's estimates.

**Tax management risk.** Tax-loss harvesting may not produce the intended benefit, may cause an account to deviate from its model, and is subject to the wash sale rules and to changes in tax law.

**Operational and cybersecurity risk.** We and our custodians and service providers depend on information systems that may fail or be breached, which could delay trading, disrupt reporting or expose client information.

## Item 11. Code of Ethics, Participation or Interest in Client Transactions and Personal Trading

### 11.1 Code of Ethics

We have adopted a Code of Ethics under Rule 204A-1 under the Investment Advisers Act of 1940 (the "Advisers Act"). The Code sets standards of business conduct that reflect our fiduciary duty and governs personal trading, gifts and entertainment, political contributions, outside business activities and material nonpublic information. The Firm treats all of its employees as access persons. We will provide a copy of the Code to any client or prospective client on request.

### 11.2 Personal Trading

Our employees may buy or sell for their own accounts the same securities that we buy or sell for clients. To address the conflicts this creates, the Code requires employees to obtain pre-clearance before trading most reportable securities, prohibits trading during blackout periods around trades in our model strategies, prohibits participation in initial public offerings, requires prior approval for investments in private placements, and requires employees to submit quarterly transaction reports and annual holdings reports, which our compliance staff review.

### 11.3 Participation or Interest in Client Transactions

Employee accounts may be included in aggregated orders with client accounts, in which case they receive the same average price and bear the same transaction costs as participating client accounts. We do not effect principal transactions or agency cross transactions for client accounts, and we do not recommend to clients securities in which the Firm or any related person has a material financial interest.

## Item 12. Brokerage Practices

### 12.1 Selection and Recommendation of Custodians

We require clients to maintain their accounts with a qualified custodian that we recommend. We recommend Harlowe Crest Securities, LLC, a registered broker-dealer and member of FINRA and SIPC, for most accounts, and Brindlemoor Trust Company, N.A., a national trust bank, for trust accounts and certain retirement accounts. We are not affiliated with either custodian. In recommending a custodian we consider the quality and cost of its execution, its custody and reporting capabilities, its technology, its client service and its financial strength. The custodian executes most trades for accounts it holds, and clients may pay more for a transaction than they would pay elsewhere.

### 12.2 Research and Other Soft Dollar Benefits

We have no soft dollar arrangements. Our custodians make available to us, without charge or at a discount, services that benefit us but not any particular client, including trading and rebalancing software, electronic account data, access to institutional share classes, research and educational conferences. These benefits are not conditioned on any volume of business, but they give us an incentive to recommend a custodian for our own benefit rather than for our clients' interest in favorable execution. We address this conflict through the best execution review described in Item 12.5.

### 12.3 Brokerage for Client Referrals

We do not select or recommend broker-dealers or custodians on the basis of client referrals.

### 12.4 Directed Brokerage

We generally do not permit clients to direct us to use a broker-dealer other than our recommended custodians. If we agree to a client's direction, we may be unable to negotiate commissions, obtain volume discounts or achieve best execution, and the client's orders may be placed after aggregated orders for other clients.

### 12.5 Aggregation and Allocation of Trades

When we buy or sell the same security for more than one account, we may aggregate the orders into a block trade if we believe aggregation is consistent with our duty to seek best execution. Each participating account receives the average price for the block and bears its proportionate share of any transaction costs. The allocation of each block is determined before the order is placed. A partially filled order is allocated pro rata based on the intended allocation. Because client accounts are held at two custodians, we sequence model trades between custodians using a rotation designed so that neither custodian's clients are consistently traded first. Our Best Execution Committee regularly reviews execution quality, custodian services and the costs borne by clients.

### 12.6 Trade Errors

When we cause a trade error in a client account, we correct it promptly so that the client is restored to the position it would have occupied had the error not occurred. The Firm reimburses the client for any resulting loss, and any resulting gain remains in the client's account.

## Item 13. Review of Accounts

### 13.1 Periodic Reviews

## Item 16. Investment Discretion

### 16.1 Discretionary and Non-Discretionary Authority

Most clients grant us discretionary authority in the advisory agreement to determine, without obtaining consent for each transaction, the securities to be bought and sold and the amounts, subject to the client's Investment Policy Statement and any restrictions the client has imposed. This authority is a limited power of attorney: it does not permit us to withdraw assets from an account, except for the deduction of our fees and transfers under a standing letter of authorization. For non-discretionary accounts, we obtain the client's approval before each transaction, and the client's account may therefore be traded after discretionary accounts, at a different price.

## Item 17. Voting Client Securities
