## SECTION 1. PURPOSE, SCOPE AND DEFINITIONS

### 1.1 Purpose

This Manual sets out the written policies and procedures that Tennick Arlow Wealth Partners, LLC (the "Firm") has adopted under Rule 206(4)-7 under the Investment Advisers Act of 1940 (the "Advisers Act") to prevent, detect and correct violations of the Advisers Act and the rules under it. Section 3 through Section 7 constitute the Firm's Code of Ethics under Rule 204A-1. This Manual is designed around the risks presented by the Firm's business: discretionary management of separately managed accounts for high-net-worth households, held at unaffiliated qualified custodians, together with financial planning and the recommendation of third-party private funds.

### 1.2 Scope

This Manual applies to every Supervised Person. Temporary staff, interns and consultants who have access to client information or to the Firm's trading systems are subject to this Manual to the extent the Chief Compliance Officer designates in writing. A Supervised Person who is uncertain whether a policy applies to a particular situation must ask the CCO before acting.

### 1.3 Definitions

"Access Person" means every Supervised Person. The Firm treats all Supervised Persons as Access Persons because all of them may have access to nonpublic information about client holdings and trading.

"Beneficial Ownership" has the meaning used in Rule 16a-1(a)(2) under the Securities Exchange Act of 1934 and includes securities held by a Supervised Person's spouse or domestic partner, minor children and other members of the household.

"CCO" means the Chief Compliance Officer designated under Section 2.1.

"Compliance Platform" means the compliance software provided by Kettleby Compliance Systems, Inc., through which Supervised Persons submit pre-clearance requests, reports, certifications and gift and political contribution disclosures.

"Covered Account" means any account in which a Supervised Person has Beneficial Ownership and that can hold Reportable Securities.

"Management Committee" means the committee consisting of the Managing Partner, the Chief Investment Officer, the Chief Operating Officer and the Chief Financial Officer.

"Reportable Security" means any security as defined in Section 202(a)(18) of the Advisers Act, except direct obligations of the U.S. government, money market instruments, shares of money market funds, shares of open-end mutual funds not advised by the Firm and interests in 529 plans.

"Supervised Person" means each member, officer and employee of the Firm and any other person who provides investment advice on behalf of the Firm and is subject to its supervision and control.

### 1.4 Exceptions and Violations

Only the CCO may grant an exception to this Manual, and every exception must be in writing and recorded in the Compliance Platform. A Supervised Person who becomes aware of a violation of this Manual must report it promptly to the CCO. Violations may result in sanctions ranging from a written warning or disgorgement of profits to suspension or termination of employment.

## SECTION 2. CHIEF COMPLIANCE OFFICER

The CCO reports to the Managing Partner and has direct and unrestricted access to the Management Committee. The CCO has full authority to develop and enforce the policies in this Manual, to obtain any record of the Firm and to require any Supervised Person to explain any activity.

### 2.2 Responsibilities

The CCO administers the compliance program. The CCO's responsibilities include maintaining and updating this Manual; administering the Code of Ethics; reviewing and approving advertisements; monitoring trading, allocation and best execution; overseeing the privacy and information security programs together with the Information Security Officer; maintaining the Firm's Form ADV and delivering it to clients; conducting the annual review under Section 15; and training Supervised Persons.

### 2.3 Compliance Manager and Delegation

The Compliance Manager reviews the CCO's own personal trading, gifts and political contributions. The CCO may delegate other tasks to members of the compliance staff, but remains responsible for the compliance program.

### 2.4 Compliance Calendar and Testing

The CCO maintains a compliance calendar of recurring obligations, including Form ADV amendments, Form 13F and Form 13H filings, code of ethics reports, custody examinations and vendor reviews. The CCO performs forensic and transactional testing throughout the year and records the results in the Compliance Platform so that they are available for the annual review.

### 2.5 Regulatory Examinations and Inquiries

Any Supervised Person who receives a telephone call, letter, email or visit from the SEC, a state securities regulator or any other regulatory authority must refer it immediately to the CCO and must not respond substantively. The CCO coordinates each examination, including the entrance interview, document requests and the production log, and determines with the Managing Partner whether to involve outside counsel. The CCO prepares the Firm's written response to any deficiency letter within the time requested by the staff, tracks each corrective action to completion, and retains a complete examination file.

## SECTION 3. CODE OF ETHICS

### 3.1 Standards of Business Conduct

The Firm is a fiduciary. Supervised Persons must place the interests of clients first, must not take advantage of their position, must avoid actual and potential conflicts of interest or disclose and manage those that cannot be avoided, and must comply with the federal securities laws. Supervised Persons may not employ any device, scheme or artifice to defraud a client or engage in any act or practice that would operate as a fraud or deceit on a client.

### 3.2 Outside Business Activities

A Supervised Person may not serve as an officer, director, trustee, general partner or employee of any other entity, or accept compensation from any person other than the Firm, without the prior written approval of the CCO. Service as trustee of a client trust requires the approval of the CCO and is subject to the custody procedures maintained by the Operations department. A Supervised Person may not serve on the board of a public company without the approval of the Management Committee.

### 3.3 Acknowledgment

Each Supervised Person must acknowledge in writing that he or she has received, read and will comply with the Code of Ethics within ten days of joining the Firm, within ten days of any material amendment and annually as part of the certification in Section 16.3.

### 3.4 Reporting Violations

Supervised Persons must report any violation of the Code of Ethics promptly to the CCO. The Firm does not tolerate retaliation against any person who reports a suspected violation in good faith. Reports may be made anonymously through the Compliance Platform.

## SECTION 4. PERSONAL TRADING AND PRE-CLEARANCE

### 4.1 Covered Accounts

Each Supervised Person must disclose every Covered Account on joining the Firm and within ten days of opening a new one. Covered Accounts must be maintained at a broker-dealer or custodian that provides an electronic data feed to the Compliance Platform, unless the CCO grants a written exception.

### 4.2 Pre-Clearance Requirement

A Supervised Person must obtain pre-clearance through the Compliance Platform before buying or selling a Reportable Security in a Covered Account. Pre-clearance is not required for transactions in exchange-traded funds on the broad-based index list maintained by Compliance, transactions under an automatic investment plan, or transactions in an account over which the Supervised Person has no direct or indirect influence or control. Pre-clearance requests are approved or denied by the CCO or the Compliance Manager; requests submitted by the CCO are approved or denied by the Compliance Manager. A request will be denied if the security is on the Restricted List, if the transaction would fall within the Blackout Window in Section 4.4, or if the reviewer determines that the transaction presents a conflict with client trading.

### 4.3 Duration of Approval

A pre-clearance approval is valid only until the close of regular trading on the business day on which it is granted. An order that is not executed within that period, including an unfilled limit order, requires a new request. Good-until-cancelled orders are not permitted in Covered Accounts.

### 4.4 Blackout Window

A Supervised Person may not buy or sell a Reportable Security on any day on which a client order in that security is pending, or within three business days after the completion of a model-wide trade in that security (the "Blackout Window"). If a Supervised Person trades within the Blackout Window, the CCO may require the trade to be reversed and any profit disgorged to a charity selected by the CCO.

### 4.5 Minimum Holding Period

A Reportable Security acquired in a Covered Account must be held for at least 30 calendar days. Profits realized on a sale within 30 calendar days of purchase, measured on a last-in, first-out basis, must be disgorged to a charity selected by the CCO. The CCO may grant a written hardship exception, for example when a sale is required to meet an unforeseen personal liquidity need, and records the reason for each exception.

### 4.6 Initial Public Offerings and Limited Offerings

Supervised Persons may not acquire securities in an initial public offering. A Supervised Person may acquire securities in a limited offering, including an interest in a private fund, only with the prior written approval of the CCO, or in the case of the CCO, of the Managing Partner. In considering a request, the CCO considers whether the opportunity should be reserved for clients.

### 4.7 Reporting

Each Supervised Person must submit an initial holdings report no later than ten days after becoming a Supervised Person, with information current as of a date not more than 45 days before that date. Each Supervised Person must submit a quarterly transaction report no later than 30 days after the end of each calendar quarter, and an annual holdings report by February 14 of each year, with information current as of December 31 of the prior year. Electronic data feeds and duplicate statements received by the Compliance Platform satisfy the reporting obligation for the accounts they cover, provided the Supervised Person confirms their completeness.

### 4.8 Review of Personal Trading

The compliance staff review personal trading monthly against client trading, the Restricted List, the Blackout Window and the holding period, and document each review in the Compliance Platform. The Compliance Manager reviews the CCO's personal trading.

## SECTION 5. GIFTS AND ENTERTAINMENT

### 5.1 General Principle

Supervised Persons may not give or accept any gift or entertainment that could reasonably be expected to influence, or appear to influence, the recipient's judgment, and may never give or accept cash or cash equivalents, including gift cards.

### 5.2 Gifts

No Supervised Person may accept gifts with an aggregate value exceeding $250 per calendar year from any single person or entity that does business with, or seeks to do business with, the Firm, including clients, custodians, fund sponsors and vendors. The same limit applies to gifts given by a Supervised Person on behalf of the Firm. Every gift with a value above $50, given or received, must be reported in the Compliance Platform within ten business days. A gift that exceeds the limit must be returned or, if return is impractical, donated to charity at the direction of the CCO.

### 5.3 Business Entertainment

Business entertainment is permitted when the host is present and the purpose is business. Business entertainment with an estimated cost exceeding $400 per person per event requires the prior approval of the CCO. Entertainment at which the host is not present is treated as a gift under Section 5.2. Travel and lodging offered by a vendor or fund sponsor may be accepted only with the prior approval of the CCO, and the Firm generally pays its own travel costs for due diligence visits.

### 5.4 Union and Government Officials

Gifts and entertainment provided to an official of a labor union or of a union-affiliated benefit plan must be recorded so that the Firm can determine whether it must file Form LM-10 with the Department of Labor. No gift or entertainment may be offered to a government official without the prior approval of the CCO.

## SECTION 6. POLITICAL CONTRIBUTIONS

### 6.1 Background

Rule 206(4)-5 under the Advisers Act prohibits an adviser from receiving compensation for advisory services to a government entity for two years after the adviser or any of its covered associates makes a contribution to certain officials of that entity. The Firm does not currently advise any government entity. Because an existing client may become affiliated with a government entity, and because the Firm may seek such clients in the future, the Firm applies the following controls to all Supervised Persons.

### 6.2 Pre-Clearance of Contributions

All Supervised Persons must obtain pre-clearance from the CCO before making any political contribution, regardless of amount, to a state or local official, a candidate for state or local office, a state or local political party, or a political action committee. This requirement applies to contributions by the Supervised Person's spouse or domestic partner made from jointly held funds. Supervised Persons may not solicit or coordinate contributions for any official or candidate, and may not do indirectly anything they may not do directly. The CCO records each request and decision in the Compliance Platform.

### 6.3 New Hire Look-Back

Before an offer of employment is extended to a candidate who will solicit clients or supervise those who do, the CCO reviews the political contributions made by the candidate during the two years before the hiring date. For all other candidates, the review covers the six months before the hiring date.

### 6.4 Recordkeeping

The CCO maintains a record of the contributions of each Supervised Person, of any government entity clients and of any payments to state or local political parties or political action committees, as required by Rule 204-2(a)(18).

## SECTION 7. INSIDER TRADING AND MATERIAL NONPUBLIC INFORMATION

### 7.1 Policy

No Supervised Person may trade, for a client or for any Covered Account, while in possession of material nonpublic information, or communicate material nonpublic information to anyone who does not need it for a legitimate business purpose. Information is material if a reasonable investor would consider it important to an investment decision, and nonpublic if it has not been disseminated to the investing public.

### 7.2 Sources of Information

Supervised Persons may receive material nonpublic information from clients who are officers, directors or large shareholders of public companies, from Kilnwick Tax Services, LLC in the course of shared engagements, from fund sponsors and from outside business activities. Supervised Persons must treat all such information as confidential.

### 7.3 Procedures on Receipt

A Supervised Person who believes he or she may have received material nonpublic information must stop trading in the affected securities, must not discuss the information with anyone other than the CCO, and must report it to the CCO immediately. The CCO determines whether the information is material and nonpublic and whether to add the issuer to the Restricted List.

### 7.4 Restricted List

The CCO maintains a Restricted List in the Compliance Platform. Issuers are placed on the Restricted List when the Firm or a Supervised Person may possess material nonpublic information about them, or when a client is an officer, director or 10% shareholder of the issuer and the Firm manages that client's holdings of the issuer's securities, including sales under Rule 144 or a Rule 10b5-1 plan. Pre-clearance requests in restricted securities are denied automatically. Client trades in a restricted security require the CCO's approval.

## SECTION 8. MARKETING AND ADVERTISING REVIEW

### 8.1 Scope

This Section implements Rule 206(4)-1 under the Advisers Act (the "Marketing Rule"). An advertisement includes any communication by the Firm that offers the Firm's advisory services to more than one person, including the website, social media content, pitch materials, newsletters, event presentations and performance reports distributed to prospective clients.

### 8.2 Pre-Use Approval

Every advertisement must be approved in writing by the CCO before its first use; in the CCO's absence the Compliance Manager may approve it. The approval, the final version of the advertisement and the support for every statement of fact in it are recorded in the Marketing Review Log. Material changes to an approved advertisement require a new approval.

### 8.3 Performance

Performance in an advertisement must be presented net of fees, with gross performance only alongside net performance with equal prominence, and must include one-, five- and ten-year periods ending on a date no earlier than the most recent calendar quarter-end. The Firm presents performance only for its model strategy composites. Hypothetical performance may be shown only to a prospective client who has requested it and has the resources to evaluate it, and only with the approval of the CCO. Extracted performance must be accompanied by the performance of the total portfolio.

### 8.4 Testimonials, Endorsements and Third-Party Ratings

The Firm does not compensate any person for testimonials, endorsements or client referrals. The use of any testimonial, endorsement or third-party rating in an advertisement requires the approval of the CCO, who confirms that the required disclosures are included and, for ratings, that the rating was not procured through payment.

### 8.5 Social Media

Supervised Persons may use professional networking profiles for business only in the form approved by Compliance. Business content may be posted only if it is pre-approved content from the Firm's library. Supervised Persons may not like, share or comment on third-party content about the Firm's services. Business social media activity is captured by the Firm's archiving system described in Section 14.

## SECTION 9. TRADE AGGREGATION AND ALLOCATION

### 9.1 Policy

No account may be favored over another in the aggregation or allocation of trades. The Head of Trading, Simone Kerrigan, is responsible for the trading desk's compliance with this Section.

### 9.2 Aggregation

The trading desk may aggregate orders for the same security across accounts when doing so is consistent with the duty to seek best execution. Each account participating in an aggregated order receives the average price and bears its pro rata share of transaction costs. Covered Accounts may participate in an aggregated order on the same terms as client accounts, but may never receive a better allocation.

### 9.3 Allocation

The allocation of each aggregated order is recorded in the order management system before the order is placed. A partially filled order is allocated pro rata to the participating accounts based on their intended allocations, rounded to the nearest whole share or, for fixed income securities, to the nearest $5,000 of par value. Any departure from the intended allocation must be approved by the Head of Trading and the CCO and documented with the reason.

### 9.4 Trade Rotation Across Custodians

Because client accounts are held at two custodians, orders for a model-wide trade are sequenced between custodians according to a rotation that the order management system generates randomly each month. The Head of Trading may not change the rotation without the approval of the CCO.

### 9.5 Trade Errors

A trade error is any error in the placement, execution or allocation of a trade, including a trade for the wrong account, security, side or quantity, or a trade that violates a client's restrictions. Trade errors are corrected promptly at no cost to the client. The Firm reimburses the client for any loss, and any gain remains in the client's account. The Head of Trading records every trade error in the Trade Error Log, and the CCO reviews each entry. Any trade error resulting in a loss or gain exceeding $5,000 is reported to the Management Committee at its next meeting.

## SECTION 10. BEST EXECUTION

### 10.1 Duty

The Firm seeks to execute client transactions so that the total cost or proceeds in each transaction is the most favorable reasonably available under the circumstances. Best execution does not require the lowest possible commission; the Firm considers price, speed, likelihood of execution and settlement, the size and type of the order and the quality of custodial services.

### 10.2 Best Execution Committee

The Best Execution Committee consists of the Head of Trading, who chairs it, the Chief Investment Officer, the Chief Operating Officer and the CCO. The Committee meets semi-annually. It reviews execution quality statistics from each custodian, fixed income markups and markdowns, trade-away activity, the economic benefits the Firm receives from custodians and the continued suitability of each recommended custodian, and records its conclusions in minutes.

### 10.3 Fixed Income Execution

For fixed income trades above $250,000 of par value, the trading desk seeks at least three competitive quotes where practicable and records the quotes received. When fewer than three quotes are available, the trader records the reason.

## SECTION 11. CYBERSECURITY AND INFORMATION SECURITY

### 11.1 Program

The Firm maintains a written information security program designed to protect the confidentiality, integrity and availability of client information and the Firm's systems. The program is based on a risk assessment that the Information Security Officer updates at least annually.

### 11.2 Information Security Officer

Owen Stahlberg, Director of Technology, serves as the Firm's Information Security Officer. The Information Security Officer is responsible for the program in this Section, reports on it to the Management Committee quarterly and coordinates with the CCO on matters of regulatory compliance.

### 11.3 Access Controls

Multi-factor authentication is required for every system that contains client information and for all remote access. Access is granted on a least-privilege basis. The Information Security Officer reviews user access rights quarterly. Access for a departing Supervised Person is disabled no later than the end of the business day on which employment ends.

### 11.4 Testing

The Firm engages Tallowmere Security Partners to conduct an external penetration test of the Firm's network and client-facing applications annually. Vulnerability scans are performed quarterly, and Supervised Persons are subject to quarterly simulated phishing exercises. Findings are tracked to remediation by the Information Security Officer.

### 11.5 Vendor Oversight

The Information Security Officer and the Chief Operating Officer conduct due diligence on each vendor with access to client information before it is engaged and annually thereafter. Contracts with such vendors must require the vendor to protect client information and to notify the Firm as soon as possible, and in no event later than 72 hours, after becoming aware of a breach affecting the Firm's information.

### 11.6 Incident Reporting and Response

Any Supervised Person who suspects a cybersecurity incident must report it to the Information Security Officer within one hour of discovery. The Information Security Officer activates the Incident Response Team described in Section 10 of the Business Continuity Plan. Where sensitive customer information has been, or is reasonably likely to have been, accessed or used without authorization, the Firm notifies affected individuals as soon as practicable, and in no event later than 30 days after becoming aware of the incident, as required by Regulation S-P, and makes any notifications required by state law.

### 11.7 Cyber Insurance

The Firm maintains a cyber liability insurance policy. The Chief Operating Officer reviews the scope of coverage at each renewal with the Information Security Officer and the CCO, and the CCO confirms that the incident response procedures in this Section and in the Business Continuity Plan are consistent with the policy's notice requirements.

### 11.8 Identity Theft Prevention

Because Supervised Persons may direct transfers from client accounts under standing letters of authorization, the Firm maintains an identity theft prevention program under Regulation S-ID. Every request to transfer funds to a third party must be verbally confirmed with the client using a telephone number on file, and never a number provided in the request.

## SECTION 12. PRIVACY OF CLIENT INFORMATION

### 12.1 Privacy Notice

The Firm delivers its privacy notice to each client at the start of the relationship. Because the Firm shares nonpublic personal information with nonaffiliated third parties only as permitted by the exceptions in Regulation S-P and has not changed its practices, it relies on the exception from annual delivery of the notice.

### 12.2 Sharing with Kilnwick Tax Services, LLC

The Firm shares client information with Kilnwick Tax Services, LLC only with the client's written consent, which the client may revoke at any time.

### 12.3 Safeguards and Disposal

Client information may be stored only on Firm systems. Paper records containing client information are shredded, and electronic media are wiped or destroyed, before disposal.

## SECTION 13. BOOKS AND RECORDS

### 13.1 Required Records

The Firm makes and keeps the books and records required by Rule 204-2 under the Advisers Act, including trade records, client agreements, written communications, advertisements and their support, code of ethics records, and records of the Firm's policies and procedures and their annual review.

### 13.2 Retention Period

Records are retained for five years from the end of the fiscal year during which the last entry was made on the record, the first two years in an office of the Firm. Partnership articles and other organizational documents are retained until at least three years after the termination of the Firm.

### 13.3 Electronic Storage

Records are stored electronically in the Firm's document management system, hosted by Norcastle Managed Services, LLC, in a form that preserves them from alteration and permits prompt retrieval and production to the SEC.

## SECTION 14. ELECTRONIC COMMUNICATIONS

### 14.1 Approved Channels

Business communications may be conducted only through the Firm's email system, the Firm's internal chat platform and the Firm-provided text messaging application installed on Firm-managed mobile devices.

### 14.2 Prohibited Channels

Personal email accounts, personal text messaging and messaging applications not provided by the Firm may not be used for business communications. If a client sends a business communication through an unapproved channel, the Supervised Person must respond only through an approved channel and forward the original message to an approved channel for capture.

### 14.3 Archiving

All business communications on approved channels are captured and retained by Marrowgate Archiving, Inc., the Firm's archiving vendor, in a non-rewriteable, non-erasable format for at least five years. The archive is searchable by Compliance and can be produced to regulators on request.

### 14.4 Surveillance and Review

Compliance reviews lexicon alerts daily and reviews a random sample of not less than 50 messages each week, drawn from all Supervised Persons. Each review and any escalation is documented in the Compliance Platform.

### 14.5 Quarterly Certification

Each Supervised Person certifies quarterly through the Compliance Platform that he or she has not used an unapproved channel for business communications or, if he or she has, that each such communication was forwarded for capture.

## SECTION 15. ANNUAL REVIEW OF THE COMPLIANCE PROGRAM

### 15.1 Scope

The CCO reviews the adequacy of this Manual and the effectiveness of its implementation at least annually, as required by Rule 206(4)-7. The review considers compliance matters that arose during the year, changes in the Firm's business and changes in applicable law.

### 15.2 Timing and Report

The CCO completes the annual review of the prior calendar year by March 31 of each year and delivers a written report of its findings and recommendations to the Management Committee. The Firm's outside compliance consultant, Harwell Quinby Compliance Advisors, LLC, assists with the transactional testing that supports the review.

### 15.3 Interim Reviews

The CCO conducts an interim review of any policy affected by a significant compliance event, a material change in the Firm's business or a change in applicable law.

## SECTION 16. TRAINING AND ATTESTATIONS

### 16.1 New Hire Training

Each new Supervised Person completes compliance training covering this Manual and the Code of Ethics within 30 days of the start of employment.

### 16.2 Annual Training

All Supervised Persons attend annual compliance training, which the CCO generally conducts in the fourth quarter, and complete annual information security training administered by the Information Security Officer.

### 16.3 Annual Certification

Each Supervised Person certifies by January 31 of each year that he or she has received, read and complied with this Manual and the Code of Ethics during the prior year.
