# Valuation Policy and Procedures

**Tennick Arlow Wealth Partners, LLC**

**Effective February 10, 2026**

## SECTION 1. PURPOSE AND SCOPE

### 1.1 Purpose

Tennick Arlow Wealth Partners, LLC (the "Firm") relies on the values of client holdings to calculate asset-based advisory fees, to report performance and holdings to clients, and to present composite performance in advertisements. This Policy establishes the procedures by which those values are determined, reviewed and, where necessary, challenged or overridden, so that values are determined fairly and consistently and fees are not calculated on inaccurate values.

### 1.2 Scope

This Policy applies to every asset held in an account for which the Firm charges a fee or provides performance reporting. The qualified custodian of each account is the official book of record for the account. The Firm does not calculate a net asset value for any pooled vehicle, and nothing in this Policy changes the value shown on a custodian's statement. Where the value the Firm uses for billing or reporting differs from the custodian's value, the Firm's quarterly report to the client identifies the difference.

### 1.3 Responsibilities

The Operations department, under the Director of Operations, Wendell Ashby, performs the daily pricing controls described in this Policy through the Firm's portfolio accounting system, provided by Tessaline Portfolio Systems, Inc. A Pricing Analyst in the Operations department reviews exception reports each business day. The Valuation Committee oversees the application of this Policy.

## SECTION 2. VALUATION COMMITTEE

### 2.1 Composition

The Valuation Committee consists of the Chief Financial Officer, who serves as Chair, the Chief Operating Officer, the Director of Operations and a senior portfolio manager designated annually by the Chief Investment Officer. The Chief Compliance Officer attends each meeting as a non-voting member. The Chair of the Committee is currently Graham Oyelaran, Chief Financial Officer.

### 2.2 Quorum and Voting

A quorum consists of three voting members, one of whom must be the Chair or the Chief Operating Officer. Decisions are made by a majority of the voting members present. A member who has a personal interest in a matter before the Committee, including a personal investment in the asset under review, discloses the interest and does not vote on it.

### 2.3 Meetings

The Committee meets quarterly, within 15 business days after the end of each calendar quarter, and at other times at the request of any member. At each quarterly meeting the Committee reviews the Override Log, the stale price report, the results of back-testing, every asset valued using Level 3 inputs, and any price challenge that remained unresolved at quarter-end. The Director of Operations prepares the meeting materials, and the minutes are kept by a member of the Operations department.

### 2.4 Responsibilities

The Committee approves valuation methodologies for asset types not addressed in this Policy, resolves disputed values, approves the valuation of any asset that has no reliable price, and recommends amendments to this Policy to the Management Committee.

### 2.5 Conflicts of Interest

Because the Firm's fees are calculated on the value of client assets, the Firm has an incentive to use higher values. Portfolio managers and wealth advisors also have an interest in the reported performance of the accounts they manage. The Firm addresses these conflicts by using custodian prices as the primary source, by requiring that overrides be approved outside the portfolio management function, by reporting every override and every Level 3 value to the Valuation Committee, and by subjecting this Policy to the independent review described in Section 8.

## SECTION 3. PRICING HIERARCHY AND SOURCES

### 3.1 Primary Source

Except for the Level 3 assets described in Section 5, which are valued under that Section, the primary source for the value of every asset is the price reported by the custodian of the account, currently Harlowe Crest Securities, LLC or Brindlemoor Trust Company, N.A. Custodian prices are loaded into the portfolio accounting system each business day.

### 3.2 Secondary Source and Tolerance Checks

The Firm subscribes to an independent pricing service, Ashcombe Pricing Data, LLC, as its secondary source. Each business day the portfolio accounting system compares the custodian price of each holding with the Ashcombe price and generates an exception when the difference exceeds 0.5% for a listed equity security or exchange-traded fund, or 2% for a fixed income security. The Pricing Analyst investigates each exception the same day and documents the resolution.

### 3.3 Fair Value Hierarchy

The Firm classifies each asset according to the inputs used to value it. Level 1 assets are valued using unadjusted quoted prices in active markets, such as the closing prices of listed equity securities and exchange-traded funds and the net asset values of open-end mutual funds. Level 2 assets are valued using observable inputs other than Level 1 prices, such as evaluated prices for municipal and corporate bonds derived from comparable trades and yield curves. Level 3 assets are valued using significant unobservable inputs, as described in Section 5.

### 3.4 Specific Instruments

Listed equity securities and exchange-traded funds are valued at the official closing price on their primary exchange. Open-end mutual funds are valued at their reported net asset value. Fixed income securities are valued at the evaluated bid price reported by the custodian. Listed options, which the Firm writes only as covered calls at a client's request, are valued at the mean of the closing bid and ask prices. Structured notes are valued at the indicative bid provided by the issuer, which the Pricing Analyst compares with the issuer's most recent published valuation. Cash and money market fund balances are valued at their stated balance.

### 3.5 Held-Away and Accommodation Assets

Assets that a client asks the Firm to include in reporting but that the Firm does not manage, such as employer retirement plan accounts or a closely held business interest, are reported at the value provided by the client or the account provider, are labeled as unmanaged in client reports and are excluded from billable assets. The Firm does not verify those values and does not include them in composite performance.

## SECTION 4. PRICE CHALLENGES AND OVERRIDES

### 4.1 Price Challenges

When the Pricing Analyst or a portfolio manager believes that a custodian price does not reflect the value of an asset, the Operations department submits a price challenge to the custodian, or to Ashcombe where the secondary source appears to be in error, within two business days of identifying the issue. The challenge is supported by evidence such as recent trade prints, dealer quotes or issuer information. Each challenge and its outcome are recorded in the Price Challenge Log.

### 4.2 Price Overrides

If a challenge is not resolved before a date on which a value is needed for billing or client reporting, the Firm may use a value other than the primary source price. An override of a primary source price for billing or client reporting must be approved in advance by the Director of Operations. Each override is recorded in the Override Log with the reason for it, the source of the alternative value, the accounts affected and the effect on fees, and is reported to the Valuation Committee at its next meeting. A portfolio manager may request an override but may not approve one.

### 4.3 Effect on Billing

Where an override changes a quarter-end value used to calculate fees, the Operations department recalculates the fee for each affected account and retains the calculation with the Override Log. If a value used for billing is later found to have been materially wrong, the Firm credits any overbilled amount on the next invoice.

## SECTION 5. LEVEL 3 METHODOLOGY

### 5.1 Level 3 Assets

Level 3 assets held in client accounts include interests in private funds recommended by the Firm, shares of non-traded real estate investment trusts and business development companies, private placements and restricted securities for which no observable price exists, and defaulted or suspended securities that no longer trade.

### 5.2 Private Funds

Interests in private funds are valued at the most recent net asset value reported by the fund's administrator or general partner, adjusted for capital contributions and distributions made after the date of that net asset value. When the custodian reports a value for a private fund interest, the Operations department compares it with the value determined under this Section and refers any difference of more than 1% to the Director of Operations.

### 5.3 Non-Traded Vehicles

Shares of non-traded real estate investment trusts and business development companies are valued at the most recent estimated per-share value published by the sponsor. If the sponsor has suspended redemptions, the Valuation Committee considers whether a discount to the published value is appropriate.

### 5.4 Restricted and Defaulted Securities

Restricted securities of an issuer that also has freely traded securities are valued at the price of the freely traded securities less a discount that reflects the restriction's remaining term, as approved by the Valuation Committee. Defaulted or suspended securities for which no reliable price is available are valued at zero once the Valuation Committee determines that recovery is unlikely.

### 5.5 Documentation

For each Level 3 asset, the Operations department maintains a valuation file containing the source documents, the calculation and the approval of the Valuation Committee where one is required. When a fund administrator or sponsor revises a previously reported value, the Operations department records the revision, determines whether any fee or client report was affected, and reports the revision to the Valuation Committee at its next meeting.

## SECTION 6. STALE PRICE MONITORING

### 6.1 Stale Price Thresholds

The portfolio accounting system flags as stale a listed equity security or exchange-traded fund whose price has not changed for five consecutive business days, a fixed income security whose price has not changed for ten consecutive business days, and a private fund whose most recent reported net asset value is dated more than 120 days before the valuation date.

### 6.2 Review and Escalation

The Pricing Analyst reviews the stale price report each business day and investigates each flagged asset. A stale price that is not resolved within five business days is escalated to the Director of Operations, and every stale price outstanding at quarter-end is reported to the Valuation Committee.

## SECTION 7. BACK-TESTING

### 7.1 Procedure

Each quarter the Operations department compares each Level 3 value and each overridden price used in the preceding quarter with the next observable transaction price or subsequently reported net asset value for the same asset. Any variance greater than 5% is investigated, and the Director of Operations documents its cause and whether the methodology should be changed. The results are presented to the Valuation Committee at its next quarterly meeting.

## SECTION 8. INDEPENDENT REVIEW

### 8.1 Annual Compliance Review

The Chief Compliance Officer reviews the adequacy of this Policy and the effectiveness of its implementation annually as part of the Firm's annual compliance review.

### 8.2 External Review

Every two years, an independent valuation firm, currently Wraxall Dunmore Advisory, LLC, reviews this Policy and tests a sample of Level 3 valuations and price overrides. The external reviewer's report is delivered to the Valuation Committee and the Management Committee, and the Chief Compliance Officer tracks each recommendation to resolution.

## SECTION 9. RECORDKEEPING AND AMENDMENTS

### 9.1 Records

The Operations department retains the Price Challenge Log, the Override Log, stale price reports, back-testing results, Level 3 valuation files and Valuation Committee minutes for at least five years.

### 9.2 Amendments

Amendments to this Policy are approved by the Management Committee on the recommendation of the Valuation Committee.
