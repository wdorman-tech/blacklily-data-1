## Item 5. Fees and Compensation

### 5.1 Management Fees

The Flagship Fund pays the Adviser a management fee at the following annual rates, which are also set out in the Adviser's Fee Schedule:

- Class A interests: 2.00% per annum of each limited partner's capital account.
- Class A interests of an investor whose aggregate capital in the Flagship Fund and the Offshore Fund, together with that of its affiliates, is $50 million or more as of the first day of a calendar quarter: 1.50% per annum on the investor's entire capital account (the "Large Investor Rate").
- Class F (founders') interests, closed to new investment since December 31, 2018: 1.25% per annum.
- Class E interests, held only by the Adviser's principals and employees and their family members: no management fee.

Management fees attributable to the Offshore Fund's investment in the Flagship Fund are charged once, at the Flagship Fund level, on a look-through basis by reference to the share class held by each Offshore Fund shareholder. The Offshore Fund does not pay a separate management fee.

### 5.2 Billing

Management fees are calculated and payable quarterly in advance as of the first business day of each calendar quarter, based on each capital account (or, for the Offshore Fund, each shareholder's pro rata share of the Offshore Fund's capital account) as of that date, before accrual of any incentive allocation. The Flagship Fund and the Offshore Fund accept subscriptions monthly, and a subscription accepted as of a date other than the first day of a calendar quarter bears a management fee pro-rated for the balance of that quarter. Management fees are debited from Fund assets by the Administrator (as defined in Item 15).

### 5.3 Other Fees and Expenses

Each Fund bears its own operating expenses, including brokerage commissions, financing and stock borrow charges, interest, fees of the Administrator, audit, tax and legal fees, fees of the directors of the Offshore Fund, and research paid for with commissions as described in Item 12. The Adviser bears its own overhead, including compensation, rent, general office technology and the cost of alternative data and expert network services. Organizational expenses of the Co-Investment Fund were borne by that Fund up to a cap of $150,000, with any excess borne by the Adviser.

### 5.4 Redemptions, Early Redemption Fee and Refunds

Limited partners of the Flagship Fund and shareholders of the Offshore Fund may redeem as of the last business day of any calendar quarter on at least 65 days' prior written notice. Redemptions effective within 12 months after an investor's initial subscription are subject to an early redemption fee of 3% of the amount redeemed, which is retained by the relevant Fund and not paid to the Adviser. A full redemption is subject to a holdback of 10% of the proceeds pending completion of the Fund's annual audit, and 90% of redemption proceeds are ordinarily paid within 30 days after the redemption date. Each of the Flagship Fund and the Offshore Fund may limit aggregate redemptions as of any quarter-end to 20% of its net asset value.

If a General Partner permits a redemption as of a date other than a quarter-end, or if an investment management agreement terminates during a quarter, the unearned portion of the prepaid management fee is refunded pro rata. Each investment management agreement may be terminated by either party on 90 days' prior written notice.

## Item 6. Performance-Based Fees and Side-by-Side Management

### 6.1 Incentive Allocation

As of December 31 of each year, and as of the effective date of any redemption, Sallowmere Partners GP, LLC receives an incentive allocation from each Class A capital account in the Flagship Fund equal to 20% of the net profits allocated to that capital account for the period, and from each Class F capital account equal to 15% of such net profits. Class E capital accounts bear no incentive allocation. The incentive allocation is subject to a high water mark: no incentive allocation is made from a capital account until prior net losses allocated to it have been recovered. There is no hurdle rate. For the Offshore Fund's investment, the incentive allocation is calculated on a look-through basis and allocated once, at the Flagship Fund level.

### 6.2 Co-Investment Fund Carried Interest

Sallowmere Co-Invest GP, LLC is entitled to carried interest equal to 10% of the net realized profits of the Co-Investment Fund, distributed only after investors have received distributions equal to 100% of their capital contributions and their share of Co-Investment Fund expenses. No carried interest is paid on unrealized gains.

### 6.3 Qualified Clients

Performance-based compensation is charged only in accordance with Rule 205-3 under the Investment Advisers Act of 1940 (the "Advisers Act"), and each investor in a Fund must be a "qualified client" as defined in that rule.

### 6.4 Side-by-Side Management and Conflicts of Interest

Performance-based compensation may create an incentive to make riskier or more speculative investments. Because the incentive allocation is calculated on realized and unrealized net profits, valuation directly affects the General Partner's compensation, a conflict addressed through the Valuation Policy described in Item 15.

The Adviser manages the Flagship Fund and the Co-Investment Fund side by side. The Flagship Fund pays a management fee and an annual incentive allocation, while the Co-Investment Fund pays no management fee and a lower carried interest on realized profits only. This difference may create an incentive to favor the Flagship Fund in allocating purchases and sales of securities of the Co-Investment Issuer. The Adviser mitigates this conflict through the allocation priority rule described in Item 12 and through review by the Chief Compliance Officer of every allocation in securities of the Co-Investment Issuer.

## Item 8. Methods of Analysis, Investment Strategies and Risk of Loss

### 8.1 Methods of Analysis

The Adviser employs fundamental, bottom-up research. Sector analysts cover mid- and large-capitalization companies in the industrials, consumer and technology hardware sectors, principally in North America and Western Europe. Research includes public filings, proprietary earnings models, management meetings, channel checks, expert network consultations and licensed alternative data. Short positions are established on specific theses, such as deteriorating unit economics or accounting quality concerns, and as hedges of sector or factor exposure. The Chief Investment Officer makes all final investment decisions.

### 8.2 Investment Strategy and Portfolio Guidelines

The Flagship Fund seeks attractive risk-adjusted returns with lower volatility than the broad equity market by holding long positions in companies the Adviser believes are undervalued and short positions in companies it believes are overvalued. The Flagship Fund invests principally in listed equity securities and also uses total return swaps, listed options, exchange-traded funds, and currency forwards to hedge non-U.S. dollar exposure. The Flagship Fund operates under the following portfolio guidelines, each measured against its net asset value:

- Gross exposure may not exceed 250% of net asset value, and has typically ranged between 160% and 220%.
- Net exposure must remain between negative 10% and positive 80% of net asset value, and has typically ranged between 25% and 55%.
- No single long position may exceed 8% of the Flagship Fund's net asset value, measured at market value.
- No single short position may exceed 4% of the Flagship Fund's net asset value, measured at market value.

The Co-Investment Fund was formed because the Adviser's desired position in the Co-Investment Issuer exceeded the Flagship Fund's single long position guideline and because the Convertible Note is illiquid. The Co-Investment Fund is not subject to the Flagship Fund's guidelines and is expected to remain concentrated in a single issuer for its entire term.

### 8.3 Risk Management

The Head of Risk, Ingrid Vallancey, reports to the Chief Operating Officer and is independent of the investment team. The risk function produces daily exposure, liquidity and stress scenario reports and monitors the portfolio guidelines in Item 8.2 before and after trading through the order management system. A breach of any portfolio guideline is reported to the Chief Investment Officer and the Chief Compliance Officer on the day it is identified.

### 8.4 Material Risks

Investing in securities involves risk of loss that investors should be prepared to bear, including loss of their entire investment. The following summary is not exhaustive; the Offering Documents contain additional risk factors.

**Market and equity risk.** Equity prices fluctuate in response to issuer-specific developments and to general economic, political and market conditions, including interest rates, inflation, trade policy and geopolitical events. Losses may be sudden and severe.

**Short selling risk.** A short sale involves the theoretical risk of unlimited loss. The Flagship Fund may be forced to close short positions at unfavorable prices if a lender recalls borrowed securities, if borrow costs increase or if a short squeeze occurs. Regulators may restrict short selling with little or no notice.

**Leverage risk.** The Flagship Fund employs leverage through margin borrowing and derivatives, which magnifies gains and losses and may force sales to meet margin calls.

**Derivatives and counterparty risk.** Swaps, options and currency forwards may not perform as intended and are subject to the credit risk of the counterparty. The Flagship Fund's swaps are entered into with affiliates of its prime brokers. The insolvency of a prime broker or counterparty could delay or prevent the return of Fund assets, including assets that have been rehypothecated.

**Concentration risk.** The Flagship Fund may hold a relatively small number of positions concentrated in particular sectors. The Co-Investment Fund holds securities of a single issuer, and its performance depends entirely on the Co-Investment Issuer.

**Illiquidity and valuation risk.** The Convertible Note is not listed and may be sold only in privately negotiated transactions, if at all. Its value is determined using a model and unobservable inputs under the Valuation Policy and may differ materially from the price at which it could be sold. The valuation of the Convertible Note does not affect carried interest until the note is sold or converted, but it does affect reported net asset value.

**Reliance on the Chief Investment Officer.** The success of the Funds depends substantially on the judgment of Mr. Everdeen, and his death, disability or departure could adversely affect the Funds and prompt redemptions.

**Operational and cybersecurity risk.** The Adviser and its service providers rely on information systems that may be disrupted or compromised by cyber attacks, system failures or natural disasters. The Adviser's business continuity plan and information security program do not eliminate these risks.

**Redemption and liquidity risk.** Significant redemptions could require the Flagship Fund to liquidate positions at unfavorable prices and increase the concentration and leverage of the remaining portfolio.

**Regulatory, tax and currency risk.** Changes in securities or tax laws may adversely affect the Funds or the Adviser, and investments in Western European issuers are subject to currency risk that hedges may not fully offset.

## Item 12. Brokerage Practices

### 12.1 Selection of Executing Brokers

The Adviser has discretion to select brokers and dealers to execute Fund transactions and to negotiate commission rates. The Adviser seeks best execution, meaning the most favorable overall terms reasonably available under the circumstances, and does not necessarily select the broker offering the lowest commission. Factors considered include execution price, commission rates, handling of large or difficult orders, confidentiality, financial stability, settlement reliability and research. In 2025 the Adviser engaged Tessingham Execution Analytics LLC to provide independent transaction cost analysis, which the Adviser's Best Execution Committee reviews.

### 12.2 Prime Brokers

Brisworth Tallent Securities LLC serves as the primary prime broker to the Flagship Fund and the Co-Investment Fund, and Quarrington Prime Services LLC serves as a second prime broker to the Flagship Fund. The prime brokers provide custody, clearing, margin financing and securities lending, and affiliates of each serve as swap counterparties. The Adviser reviews prime broker financing rates, borrow availability and credit standing at least annually.

### 12.3 Research and Other Soft Dollar Benefits

The Adviser receives research products and services from brokers in connection with Fund brokerage, including through commission sharing arrangements with a limited number of executing brokers under which a portion of the commissions paid on Fund transactions is credited to a pool that the Adviser directs to pay independent research providers. Research obtained includes written research, industry data and market data services. The Adviser pays the non-research portion of any mixed-use product from its own resources.

The Adviser intends that all such arrangements fall within the safe harbor of Section 28(e) of the Securities Exchange Act of 1934. Research obtained with Fund commissions may be used in managing all of the Funds, not only the Fund whose transactions generated the commissions. Because the Adviser does not pay for research obtained with commissions, it has an incentive to select brokers based on its interest in receiving research, and the Funds may pay commissions higher than those charged by other brokers. The Best Execution Committee reviews the commission budget semi-annually through a broker vote.

### 12.4 Brokerage for Client Referrals and Directed Brokerage

The Adviser does not select brokers in consideration of investor referrals. The capital introduction group of Brisworth Tallent Securities LLC has from time to time introduced prospective investors to the Adviser without compensation, and the Adviser does not consider such introductions in allocating brokerage. The Adviser does not permit investors to direct brokerage.

### 12.5 Aggregation and Allocation of Orders

The Adviser may aggregate orders for more than one Fund when it believes aggregation is consistent with best execution. Each aggregated order is pre-allocated in the order management system before it is placed, all participating Funds receive the average price, and partially filled orders are allocated pro rata in proportion to the pre-allocation. Purchases of securities of the Co-Investment Issuer are allocated between the Flagship Fund and the Co-Investment Fund in accordance with the priority rule in the Adviser's allocation policy, which is designed so that the Co-Investment Fund holds only the portion of the position that the Flagship Fund cannot hold consistent with its portfolio guidelines. Employee orders are never aggregated with Fund orders.

### 12.6 Trade Errors

If a trade error occurs as a result of the Adviser's negligence or a breach of a Fund's guidelines, the Adviser reimburses the Fund for any resulting loss. Gains resulting from a trade error are retained by the Fund. The Chief Compliance Officer maintains a log of all trade errors and their resolution.
