## SECTION 1. INTRODUCTION

### 1.1 Purpose and Scope

This Compliance Manual (the "Manual") sets out the written policies and procedures that Sallowmere Capital Management, LP (the "Adviser") has adopted under Rule 206(4)-7 under the Investment Advisers Act of 1940 (the "Advisers Act") to prevent, detect and correct violations of the federal securities laws. The Manual applies to the Adviser, to Sallowmere Partners GP, LLC and Sallowmere Co-Invest GP, LLC (the "General Partners"), and to the management of Sallowmere Partners Fund, LP (the "Flagship Fund"), Sallowmere Partners Offshore Fund, Ltd. (the "Offshore Fund") and Sallowmere Co-Investment Fund I, LP (the "Co-Investment Fund" and, together with the Flagship Fund and the Offshore Fund, the "Funds"). The Valuation Policy and the Business Continuity Plan are separate documents adopted under this Manual.

### 1.2 Supervised Persons

Every partner, officer and employee of the Adviser, and any other person who provides advice on behalf of the Adviser and is subject to its supervision and control, is a "supervised person." Each supervised person must read the Manual, comply with it, and acknowledge it in writing on hire, annually and upon any material amendment. Temporary staff and consultants are subject to the provisions the Chief Compliance Officer designates in writing.

### 1.3 Amendments and Interpretation

Amendments to the Manual, the Valuation Policy, the Business Continuity Plan and the Fee Schedule are approved by the Management Committee, composed of the Managing Partner, the President and Chief Operating Officer, the Chief Financial Officer and the General Counsel and Chief Compliance Officer. Between full revisions, approved amendments are issued by written memorandum of the Chief Compliance Officer, which identifies the provision amended and supersedes it from the stated effective date. Questions of interpretation are resolved by the Chief Compliance Officer, whose determination is final. Where the Manual is silent, supervised persons must act in accordance with the Adviser's fiduciary duty to the Funds.

## SECTION 3. CODE OF ETHICS

### 3.1 Standards of Business Conduct

This Section 3 and Section 4 together constitute the Adviser's Code of Ethics under Rule 204A-1 under the Advisers Act. Supervised persons must act with honesty, integrity and care; place the interests of the Funds and their investors ahead of their own; avoid actual and potential conflicts of interest or disclose them to the CCO; comply with applicable federal securities laws; and safeguard confidential information about Fund holdings and trading.

### 3.2 Access Persons and Covered Accounts

All employees of the Adviser are designated access persons. A "covered account" is any account in which an access person has direct or indirect beneficial ownership, including accounts of a spouse, domestic partner, minor child or other household member, and any account over which the access person exercises investment discretion. Accounts managed by an unaffiliated third party over which the access person has no influence or control may be exempted by the CCO in writing on receipt of a certification from the access person and the manager.

### 3.3 Reporting Violations

Supervised persons must report any actual or suspected violation of this Manual promptly to the CCO or, if the matter involves the CCO, to the Managing Partner. Reports may be made anonymously through the compliance platform. The Adviser does not retaliate against any person for a good faith report.

### 3.4 Sanctions

Violations may result in a warning, disgorgement of profits, reversal of trades at the supervised person's expense, suspension of personal trading privileges, a reduction in discretionary compensation, or termination of employment. The CCO reports material violations to the Management Committee.

### 3.5 Outside Business Activities

Supervised persons may not engage in any outside business activity, including service as an officer, director, general partner, adviser or consultant to any other business, without the prior written approval of the CCO. Service as a director of a public company is prohibited. Unpaid service with a charitable or civic organization requires notice to the CCO, and approval where the organization is an investor or prospective investor in a Fund.

## SECTION 4. PERSONAL TRADING

### 4.1 Reportable Securities and Exemptions

All securities are reportable securities except direct obligations of the U.S. government, bankers' acceptances, bank certificates of deposit, commercial paper, high quality short-term debt instruments, money market fund shares, and shares of open-end mutual funds. Exchange-traded funds, closed-end funds, options, single-stock futures, cryptocurrency-linked exchange-traded products and private placements are reportable.

### 4.2 Pre-Clearance

Access persons must obtain pre-clearance before any transaction in a reportable security in a covered account, other than transactions in the broad-based index exchange-traded funds on the list maintained by the Compliance department and transactions under an automatic investment plan. Requests are submitted through the compliance platform and are approved or denied by the CCO or the Deputy CCO. Pre-clearance requests submitted by the CCO are approved by the President and Chief Operating Officer, Mireille Dansby. Approval is valid only for the business day on which it is granted; an order not executed by the close of regular trading that day requires a new request. Limit orders that remain open beyond that day are not permitted. Compliance may deny any request without stating a reason.

### 4.3 Blackout Periods and Restricted List

An access person may not trade in a security within seven calendar days before or after a Fund trades in that security or in a derivative referencing it, and may not trade while a Fund order in that security is pending. Access persons may not trade in any security on the Restricted List described in Section 7.2. The CCO will review any trade executed during a blackout period and may require it to be reversed with any profit disgorged.

### 4.4 Prohibited Transactions

Access persons may not acquire securities in an initial public offering. Investments in private placements require the prior written approval of the CCO, which considers whether the opportunity should be offered to a Fund. Access persons may not sell short, or buy put options on, any security held long by the Flagship Fund, and may not engage in any transaction designed to profit from, or that front-runs, a Fund trade.

### 4.5 Minimum Holding Period

Access persons must hold any position in a reportable security acquired in a covered account for at least 30 calendar days from the date of acquisition before disposing of it, and may not close a short position within 30 calendar days of opening it. Profits realized on a transaction that violates this Section 4.5 are disgorged to a charity selected by the CCO. The CCO may grant a written hardship exception, including where a security is sold at a loss.

### 4.6 Holdings and Transaction Reports

Each access person must submit an initial holdings report within 10 calendar days after becoming an access person, with information current as of a date not more than 45 days before the person became an access person. Each access person must submit a quarterly transaction report within 30 calendar days after the end of each calendar quarter, and an annual holdings report by February 14 of each year with information current as of December 31 of the prior year. Access persons must arrange for each broker holding a covered account to provide duplicate statements or an electronic data feed to the Compliance department, which satisfies the report content requirements where it contains the required information.

### 4.7 Review of Personal Trading

The Deputy CCO reviews all holdings and transaction reports and compares personal trades against Fund trading, pre-clearance records, the Restricted List and blackout periods. The CCO's reports and trades are reviewed by the President and Chief Operating Officer. Exceptions are documented and reported to the CCO.

## SECTION 5. GIFTS AND ENTERTAINMENT

### 5.1 General Principles

Supervised persons may not give or accept any gift or entertainment that could reasonably be expected to influence a business decision, including the selection of brokers, counterparties, vendors or service providers, or an investor's decision to invest in a Fund. Cash and cash equivalents may never be given or accepted.

### 5.2 Gifts Received

Supervised persons may accept gifts from any person or entity that does business or seeks to do business with the Adviser or the Funds, including brokers and research providers, with an aggregate value not exceeding $250 per source per calendar year. Gifts in excess of that limit must be declined or surrendered to the Compliance department.

### 5.3 Gifts Given

Supervised persons may give gifts to any person associated with a current or prospective investor, service provider or counterparty with an aggregate value not exceeding $250 per recipient per calendar year. All gifts given are paid for by the Adviser and never by a Fund.

### 5.4 Business Entertainment

Business entertainment, such as meals and sporting or cultural events, is permitted only where the host attends and the entertainment is neither lavish nor frequent. Entertainment with a value exceeding $500 per person per event requires the prior approval of the CCO. Travel and lodging provided by a broker or vendor may not be accepted without CCO approval and is ordinarily paid for by the Adviser.

### 5.5 Government Officials, Union Officials and Plan Fiduciaries

No gift or entertainment may be given to a government official, including an official or employee of a public pension plan, without the prior approval of the CCO, who will consider applicable federal, state and local gift and ethics rules and Section 6. Gifts and entertainment provided to officials of labor unions are subject to Department of Labor reporting on Form LM-10, and gifts to fiduciaries of ERISA plans are limited to amounts permissible under Department of Labor guidance.

### 5.6 Recording and Reporting

Supervised persons must record in the compliance platform, within five business days, every gift and every item of entertainment given or received, other than promotional items of nominal value under $50. The Deputy CCO reviews the log monthly, and the Best Execution Committee receives a summary of gifts and entertainment received from brokers at each meeting.

## SECTION 6. POLITICAL CONTRIBUTIONS

### 6.1 Scope

The Adviser has adopted this Section 6 under Rule 206(4)-5 under the Advisers Act. One or more government entities, including public pension plans, are investors in the Flagship Fund. A contribution by a covered associate to an official of a government entity could prohibit the Adviser from receiving compensation from that government entity for two years.

### 6.2 Pre-Clearance and Limits

All supervised persons, whether or not covered associates, must obtain the prior written approval of the CCO before making any political contribution to a candidate for or holder of federal, state or local office, a political party, or a political action committee, and before engaging in any fundraising activity. The CCO will approve contributions by covered associates only within the following limits: up to $350 per election to an official for whom the covered associate is entitled to vote, and up to $150 per election to an official for whom the covered associate is not entitled to vote. Contributions by a spouse or household member that are directed or funded by a supervised person are treated as contributions of the supervised person.

### 6.3 New Covered Associates

Before an offer of employment is made to any person who would be a covered associate, the Compliance department reviews that person's political contributions for the preceding two years, or six months for a person who will not solicit investors. The CCO must approve the hire if any contribution during that period exceeded the limits in Section 6.2.

### 6.4 Solicitation, Bundling and Payments to Third Parties

Supervised persons may not solicit or coordinate contributions to an official of a government entity to which the Adviser provides or seeks to provide advisory services, or to a political party of the state or locality of that entity. The Adviser does not pay any third party to solicit government entities unless the third party is a regulated person under Rule 206(4)-5 and the arrangement is approved by the CCO.

## SECTION 7. INSIDER TRADING AND MATERIAL NONPUBLIC INFORMATION

### 7.1 Policy

Supervised persons may not trade, for a Fund or any covered account, while in possession of material nonpublic information ("MNPI") about an issuer or its securities, and may not communicate MNPI to any other person except as permitted by this Section 7. Information is material if a reasonable investor would consider it important in making an investment decision, and nonpublic if it has not been disseminated in a manner making it available to investors generally.

### 7.2 Restricted List and Watch List

The CCO maintains a Restricted List of issuers in which no trading for the Funds or covered accounts is permitted, and a confidential Watch List of issuers about which the Adviser may possess sensitive information. Restrictions are coded in the order management system to block Fund orders. Issuers are removed from the Restricted List only by the CCO, after confirming that the information has become public or is no longer material.

### 7.3 Wall-Crossings

Only the Chief Investment Officer or the CCO may consent to receive MNPI in connection with a proposed offering or transaction. When an issuer or its agent requests a wall-crossing, the recipient must decline to receive any further detail and refer the request to the CCO. On consent, the issuer is placed on the Restricted List immediately and remains there until cleansing disclosure is made.

### 7.4 Expert Networks

Supervised persons may use only expert network firms approved by the CCO after due diligence on the firm's compliance procedures. Each consultation must be scheduled through the approved network's platform and pre-approved by the Compliance department. The Adviser does not consult current employees of any public company or of any government agency, or any person subject to a duty of confidentiality regarding the topic of the call. Each call opens with a reminder that the expert may not disclose MNPI or confidential information. The Compliance department chaperones the first call with each new expert and at least 10% of all subsequent calls, selected at random. Analysts must retain notes of every call in the research management system.

### 7.5 Alternative Data

Alternative data sets may be licensed only after the Compliance department and the Director of Technology complete due diligence on the vendor, including the source of the data, whether it was obtained in breach of any contractual or legal restriction, and whether it contains personal information.

### 7.6 Procedures on Receipt of MNPI

A supervised person who believes he or she may have received MNPI must stop trading in the issuer for all accounts, refrain from communicating the information to anyone other than the CCO, and report the matter to the CCO immediately. The CCO determines whether to add the issuer to the Restricted List and documents the determination.

## SECTION 8. MARKETING AND INVESTOR COMMUNICATIONS

### 8.1 Review and Approval

All advertisements within the meaning of Rule 206(4)-1 under the Advisers Act, including pitch books, tear sheets, investor letters, website content and presentations to prospective investors, must be approved in writing by the CCO before first use. Material changes to approved materials require re-approval. The Compliance department retains each approved version with its supporting documentation.

### 8.2 Performance Presentation

Performance is presented net of all fees and expenses, and gross performance is shown only alongside net performance calculated over the same period and with equal prominence. Performance of the Flagship Fund is presented for one-, five- and ten-year periods, or since inception where shorter. Extracted, hypothetical and predecessor performance may not be used without the specific written approval of the CCO and the policies and procedures Rule 206(4)-1 requires. The Adviser does not use testimonials. Its only endorsements are the compensated solicitation activities of the placement agent described in Section 8.4, which are conducted under a written agreement and subject to the disclosure and oversight conditions of Rule 206(4)-1.

### 8.3 Due Diligence Questionnaires and Requests for Proposal

Responses to due diligence questionnaires and requests for proposal are drafted by Investor Relations, reviewed for accuracy by the relevant function head, and approved by the CCO before release. Standard responses are refreshed at least annually and whenever an underlying policy changes.

### 8.4 Placement Agent Oversight

Wexcombe Partners Ltd. may use only materials approved under Section 8.1 and must provide the CCO with a quarterly certification of compliance with its placement agreement. The CCO reviews the placement agent's regulatory status annually.

## SECTION 9. TRADE ALLOCATION AND AGGREGATION

### 9.1 Aggregation

Orders for more than one Fund in the same security may be aggregated where the Head of Trading believes aggregation is consistent with best execution. Orders for covered accounts are never aggregated with Fund orders.

### 9.2 Pre-Allocation

Before an aggregated order is placed, the portfolio management team records the intended allocation to each Fund in the order management system. Each participating Fund receives the average execution price and bears its pro rata share of commissions.

### 9.3 Partial Fills and Deviations

Partially filled orders are allocated pro rata in proportion to the pre-allocation, subject to rounding to round lots. Any allocation that deviates from the pre-allocation must be approved by the CCO before the end of the trade date, with the reason documented.

### 9.4 Co-Investment Issuer Allocations

Purchases of securities of the Co-Investment Issuer are allocated first to the Flagship Fund until the Flagship Fund's position reaches the single long position limit in its portfolio guidelines, and the balance of the order is allocated to the Co-Investment Fund. Sales of securities of the Co-Investment Issuer are allocated pro rata in proportion to each Fund's holdings immediately before the sale, unless the CCO approves a different allocation in writing to restore the Flagship Fund's compliance with its guidelines. The CCO reviews every allocation in securities of the Co-Investment Issuer on the trade date.

### 9.5 Trade Errors

A trade error is any error in the placement, execution, allocation or settlement of a Fund trade, including a trade that breaches a Fund guideline. Errors must be reported to the Head of Trading and the CCO on discovery and corrected promptly. The Adviser reimburses a Fund for any loss resulting from an error caused by the Adviser's negligence or a guideline breach, and any gain is retained by the Fund. Errors resulting in a loss of more than $10,000 are reported to the Management Committee. The CCO maintains the trade error log.

## SECTION 10. BEST EXECUTION

### 10.1 Duty

The Adviser seeks to obtain the most favorable overall terms reasonably available for each Fund transaction, considering price, commission, speed and likelihood of execution, market impact, confidentiality, counterparty credit and the value of research.

### 10.2 Best Execution Committee

The Best Execution Committee consists of the President and Chief Operating Officer (chair), the Head of Trading, the Chief Financial Officer and the CCO. The Committee meets quarterly and reviews independent transaction cost analysis prepared by Tessingham Execution Analytics LLC, commission rates and totals by broker, execution quality exceptions, the soft dollar budget and gifts and entertainment received from brokers. Minutes are kept by the Compliance department.

### 10.3 Approved Brokers

Fund orders may be placed only with brokers on the approved broker list. A new broker requires the approval of the Best Execution Committee following review of its financial condition, regulatory history and operational capabilities, and each broker's standing is reviewed at least annually.

### 10.4 Commission Sharing Arrangements

Research credits under commission sharing arrangements are used only for research products and services that qualify for the safe harbor of Section 28(e) of the Securities Exchange Act of 1934. The Best Execution Committee approves the research budget semi-annually following a broker vote in which the investment team rates the research and execution of each broker. Mixed-use products are allocated in writing by the Chief Financial Officer, and the non-research portion is paid by the Adviser.

## SECTION 11. CYBERSECURITY AND INFORMATION SECURITY

### 11.1 Information Security Officer

Kofi Brannigan, Director of Technology, serves as Information Security Officer and is responsible for the Adviser's written information security program, which is designed to protect the confidentiality, integrity and availability of the Adviser's systems and data and of investor information.

### 11.2 Access Controls

Multi-factor authentication is required for all remote access, email, the order management system and all cloud applications. Access is granted on a least-privilege basis on the approval of the relevant department head, reviewed quarterly by the Information Security Officer, and removed on the day an employee departs. All firm devices use full-disk encryption, and personal devices may access firm email only through a managed, encrypted container.

### 11.3 Security Assessments and Awareness

The Information Security Officer conducts an annual cybersecurity risk assessment. The Adviser engages an independent firm to perform annual external and internal penetration testing, and remediation of findings is tracked to completion. The Adviser conducts quarterly simulated phishing exercises, and employees who fail an exercise complete additional training.

### 11.4 Vendor Oversight

Vendors with access to the Adviser's systems or confidential data, including the Adviser's IT managed service provider, Oskerby Technology Group LLC, are subject to due diligence before engagement and annually thereafter, including review of their information security controls, independent control reports where available, and incident notification obligations.

### 11.5 Incident Response and Insurance

Cybersecurity incidents are handled under the incident response procedures in Section 9 of the Business Continuity Plan. The Adviser maintains a cyber liability insurance policy, and the President and Chief Operating Officer reviews the adequacy of its coverage at each annual renewal.

## SECTION 12. PRIVACY

### 12.1 Regulation S-P

The Adviser protects nonpublic personal information of investors who are natural persons in accordance with Regulation S-P. Access to investor information is limited to personnel and service providers who need it to perform their duties.

### 12.2 Privacy Notices

The Adviser, the General Partners and the Offshore Fund provide an initial privacy notice to each natural person investor at subscription. Because the Adviser shares information only as permitted by the exceptions in Regulation S-P and has not changed its practices, it relies on the exception from delivery of an annual privacy notice.

### 12.3 Notification of Unauthorized Access

The Adviser's incident response program includes procedures to assess, contain and control incidents involving unauthorized access to or use of customer information. If the Adviser determines that sensitive customer information has been, or is reasonably likely to have been, accessed or used without authorization, it will notify each affected individual as soon as practicable, but not later than 30 days after becoming aware that the unauthorized access or use has occurred or is reasonably likely to have occurred, unless an exception applies. Service providers with access to customer information must notify the Adviser as soon as possible, and in any event within 72 hours after becoming aware of a breach of security affecting that information.

### 12.4 Non-U.S. Data Protection

Personal data of Offshore Fund investors is processed in accordance with the Data Protection Act (As Revised) of the Cayman Islands and other applicable non-U.S. data protection laws. The Offshore Fund's privacy notice describes the data collected, the purposes of processing and investors' rights.

### 12.5 Disposal

Paper records containing investor information are destroyed by cross-cut shredding or a bonded shredding vendor, and electronic media are wiped or physically destroyed before disposal.

## SECTION 13. BOOKS AND RECORDS

### 13.1 Required Records

The Adviser maintains the books and records required by Rule 204-2 under the Advisers Act, including trade records, order tickets, written communications relating to recommendations and trades, advertisements and their support, performance records, the Code of Ethics records described in Section 4, political contribution records, and records of the annual review.

### 13.2 Retention Period

The Adviser retains all required books and records for at least seven years from the end of the fiscal year during which the last entry was made on the record, which exceeds the five-year period required by Rule 204-2. Records are kept for the first two years in an appropriate office of the Adviser or in electronic form that is immediately accessible.

### 13.3 Storage and Access

Electronic records are stored in a manner that protects them from alteration or loss, permits prompt retrieval and production to the SEC, and maintains a duplicate copy in a separate location. The Compliance department must approve the destruction of any record, and destruction is suspended for any record subject to a litigation or regulatory hold.

## SECTION 14. ELECTRONIC COMMUNICATIONS

### 14.1 Approved Channels

Business communications may be conducted only through the Adviser's email system, its approved messaging platform, and the captured text messaging application installed on firm-issued mobile devices. The use of personal email, personal text messaging, or consumer messaging applications for business communications is prohibited.

### 14.2 Capture and Archiving

All communications on approved channels are captured and archived by Quillon Archive Services, Inc. in a non-rewriteable, non-erasable format, indexed and searchable by the Compliance department, and retained for the period in Section 13.2.

### 14.3 Off-Channel Communications

A supervised person who receives a business communication on an unapproved channel must not respond on that channel, must forward the communication to an approved channel within one business day, and must continue the conversation on an approved channel. Repeated off-channel use is a violation subject to sanction under Section 3.4.

### 14.4 Surveillance

The Deputy CCO reviews archived communications monthly, using lexicon-based alerts and a random sample of each employee's communications, and documents the review and any escalation to the CCO.

## SECTION 15. ANNUAL REVIEW

### 15.1 Scope

The CCO conducts an annual review of the adequacy of the policies and procedures in this Manual, the Valuation Policy and the Business Continuity Plan, and of the effectiveness of their implementation, taking into account compliance matters that arose during the year, changes in the Adviser's business and changes in applicable law. Ferrisburg Regulatory Consulting LLC assists the CCO with the annual review, including transaction sampling and interviews of personnel.

### 15.2 Report

The CCO delivers a written report of the annual review to the Management Committee by March 31 of the year following the year reviewed. The report identifies any material compliance matters and recommended changes. The CCO also conducts interim reviews in response to significant compliance events, business changes or regulatory developments.

## SECTION 16. TRAINING AND CERTIFICATIONS

### 16.1 Training

Each new employee must complete compliance training covering this Manual and the Code of Ethics within 30 days after his or her start date. All employees must attend annual compliance training, conducted by the CCO in the fourth quarter of each year, and additional targeted training is provided on topics such as MNPI, expert networks, marketing and cybersecurity. Attendance is recorded by the Compliance department.

### 16.2 Certifications

Each supervised person must certify annually that he or she has read and complied with this Manual and the Code of Ethics, has reported all covered accounts, holdings and transactions, and has disclosed all outside business activities and political contributions.
