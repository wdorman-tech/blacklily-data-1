## SECTION 1. PURPOSE, SCOPE AND PRINCIPLES

### 1.1 Purpose

This Valuation Policy and Procedures (the "Policy") governs the valuation of all investments held by Sallowmere Partners Fund, LP (the "Flagship Fund"), Sallowmere Partners Offshore Fund, Ltd. (the "Offshore Fund") and Sallowmere Co-Investment Fund I, LP (the "Co-Investment Fund" and, together, the "Funds"), each managed by Sallowmere Capital Management, LP (the "Adviser"). The Policy was adopted by the Management Committee and is administered by the Valuation Committee. Because the Adviser's management fee and the incentive allocation and carried interest received by its affiliates depend on the value of Fund assets, the Policy is designed to ensure that valuations are fair, consistently applied, independent of the investment team and fully documented.

### 1.2 Scope and Frequency

The Policy applies to the monthly net asset value of each Fund calculated by Carvell Brink Fund Services Ltd. (the "Administrator"), to the Adviser's daily estimated net asset values used for risk management and investor estimates, and to the fair value measurements reported in each Fund's annual audited financial statements.

### 1.3 Fair Value Standard

Investments are valued at fair value as defined in Accounting Standards Codification Topic 820, meaning the price that would be received to sell an asset, or paid to transfer a liability, in an orderly transaction between market participants at the measurement date. Each investment is classified in the fair value hierarchy as Level 1 (unadjusted quoted prices in active markets for identical instruments), Level 2 (observable inputs other than Level 1 prices) or Level 3 (significant unobservable inputs). Classification is proposed by the Controller and approved by the Valuation Committee.

## SECTION 2. VALUATION COMMITTEE

### 2.1 Composition

The Valuation Committee (the "Committee") has four voting members: the Chief Financial Officer, Oren Pellisier, who chairs the Committee; the President and Chief Operating Officer, Mireille Dansby; the Head of Risk, Ingrid Vallancey; and the Controller, Lydia Marchbanks, who also acts as secretary. The General Counsel and Chief Compliance Officer attends every meeting as a non-voting member. The Chief Investment Officer and members of the investment team may attend to provide information about an investment, but may not vote and are excused before any vote is taken.

### 2.2 Quorum and Voting

A quorum consists of three voting members, one of whom must be the Chief Financial Officer or the Chief Operating Officer. Determinations are made by majority vote of the voting members present. A member with a personal interest in a determination must disclose it and abstain. Determinations may be made by unanimous written consent of all voting members when a meeting cannot be convened in time.

### 2.3 Meetings

The Committee meets quarterly, within ten business days after the end of each calendar quarter, and on an ad hoc basis whenever the Chief Financial Officer, the Chief Operating Officer or the Chief Compliance Officer determines that a valuation matter requires prompt determination, including a trading halt, a suspension, a corporate event or a significant market dislocation affecting a Fund holding.

### 2.4 Responsibilities

The Committee approves pricing sources and methodologies; approves the fair value hierarchy classification of each investment; determines the fair value of each Level 3 investment and of any investment for which a reliable market price is unavailable; reviews price challenges, overrides, stale price reports, tolerance exceptions and back-testing results; reviews the work of the independent valuation firm; and recommends amendments to this Policy to the Management Committee. The secretary records minutes of every meeting, including the materials reviewed and the basis for each determination.

## SECTION 3. PRICING HIERARCHY AND SOURCES

### 3.1 Primary and Secondary Pricing Vendors

The Committee has approved Orrisdale Market Data LLC as the primary pricing vendor and Pennick Pricing Services Inc. as the secondary pricing vendor. The Administrator obtains prices directly from the approved vendors under its own agreements, and the Controller receives the same feeds for the Adviser's daily estimates. A change in an approved vendor requires Committee approval.

### 3.2 Listed Equities and Exchange-Traded Funds

Equity securities and exchange-traded funds listed on a securities exchange are valued at the official closing price on the primary exchange as reported by the primary pricing vendor. If no trade occurred on the valuation date, long positions are valued at the closing bid price and short positions at the closing ask price. Non-U.S. equities are converted into U.S. dollars at the 4:00 p.m. London closing rate supplied by the primary pricing vendor.

### 3.3 Listed Options

Listed options are valued at the mean of the closing bid and ask prices on the exchange with the greatest volume. Where the spread exceeds 20% of the mean, the Controller refers the position to the Chief Financial Officer, who may approve use of the last traded price or a model price.

### 3.4 Total Return Swaps

Total return swaps on single equities and baskets are valued using the closing price of the underlying securities determined under Section 3.2, adjusted for accrued financing and dividends. The Controller reconciles each swap valuation daily to the counterparty statement, and differences exceeding 0.10% of notional are investigated and resolved with the counterparty.

### 3.5 Currency Forwards and Cash

Currency forwards are valued using forward points and spot rates supplied by the secondary pricing vendor, interpolated to the settlement date. Cash, margin balances and money market fund shares are valued at their reported balances or net asset value.

### 3.6 Vendor Tolerance Checks

Each business day the Controller compares the prices supplied by the primary and secondary pricing vendors for every position. Differences exceeding 1% for equities and exchange-traded funds, or 2% for over-the-counter derivatives, are investigated and resolved before the daily estimate is released, and any unresolved difference is reported to the Chief Financial Officer. The Controller also investigates any daily price change of more than 15% for a single position that is not explained by public news.

## SECTION 4. LEVEL 3 METHODOLOGY

### 4.1 Scope

Level 3 investments currently consist of the senior unsecured convertible note held by the Co-Investment Fund (the "Convertible Note") and issued by the single listed issuer whose common stock the Co-Investment Fund also holds (the "Co-Investment Issuer"), together with any security that becomes subject to a prolonged trading suspension or for which the Committee determines that no reliable market price exists.

### 4.2 Convertible Note Model

The Convertible Note is valued using a binomial lattice model that values the note as a combination of a straight debt instrument and an embedded option to convert into the Co-Investment Issuer's common stock, and that reflects the issuer's call right and the holder's put right under the note indenture. The model is calibrated at inception so that the model value equals the transaction price, and the calibrated inputs are carried forward and updated at each valuation date.

### 4.3 Unobservable Inputs

The significant unobservable input is the credit spread applied to the straight debt component, which is determined by reference to the calibrated spread at inception, adjusted for changes in the spreads of comparable issuers' senior unsecured debt and for issuer-specific developments such as changes in leverage and liquidity. Volatility is derived from 180-day historical volatility of the Co-Investment Issuer's common stock, cross-checked against implied volatility on listed options where available. The Controller prepares a sensitivity analysis showing the effect of a 100 basis point change in credit spread and a five-point change in volatility.

### 4.4 Documentation

For each Level 3 valuation the Controller prepares a valuation memorandum setting out the methodology, each input and its source, changes since the prior valuation, and the sensitivity analysis. The memorandum is approved by the Committee and provided to the independent valuation firm, the Administrator and the auditor.

## SECTION 5. PRICE CHALLENGES AND OVERRIDES

### 5.1 Price Challenges

If the Head of Trading, the Controller or the Chief Financial Officer believes that a vendor price does not reflect fair value, the Controller submits a written challenge to the vendor with supporting evidence, such as executed trades, broker quotes or corporate event information. While a challenge is pending, the vendor price continues to be used unless an override is approved under Section 5.2. The Controller maintains a log of every challenge and its outcome.

### 5.2 Overrides

Any override of a vendor price, broker quote or model value used by the Administrator requires the written approval of the Chief Financial Officer and one other voting member of the Committee. The request must state the reason for the override, the proposed price, its source and the effect on net asset value. The Chief Investment Officer and members of the investment team may not approve an override. The Controller notifies the Administrator of each approved override on the day of approval.

### 5.3 Reporting

All challenges and overrides since the prior meeting are reported to the full Committee, together with the subsequent market price of each overridden position where available.

## SECTION 6. STALE PRICE MONITORING

### 6.1 Stale Price Reports

The Administrator and the Controller each produce a daily report of positions whose price has not changed. A price that is unchanged for three consecutive business days is flagged and investigated by the Controller. A price that is unchanged for five consecutive business days is escalated to the Chief Financial Officer. A price that is unchanged for ten consecutive business days may not continue to be used without a fair value determination by the Committee. Positions subject to a trading halt are escalated immediately.

## SECTION 7. BACK-TESTING

### 7.1 Back-Testing Procedures

Each quarter the Controller compares the value assigned to each Level 3 investment, and to each position that was valued by override or fair value determination, with the price realized on any subsequent sale or the first subsequent observable market price. Differences exceeding 5% are documented with an explanation and reported to the Committee, which considers whether the methodology or inputs should be changed. The results of back-testing are also provided to the independent valuation firm.

## SECTION 8. INDEPENDENT REVIEW AND OVERSIGHT

### 8.1 Independent Valuation Firm

Kilbride Sayer Valuation Advisors LLC performs a quarterly review of each Level 3 investment and provides a written range of reasonable values. If the value approved by the Committee falls outside that range, the Chief Financial Officer must convene the Committee to reconsider the value before the Administrator finalizes the quarter-end net asset value, and the Chief Compliance Officer must be notified of the outcome.

### 8.2 Administrator

The Administrator independently obtains prices for all Level 1 and Level 2 positions, calculates each Fund's net asset value, and reports to the Controller every position for which its price differs from the Adviser's price. The Administrator applies Level 3 values approved by the Committee and receives the supporting valuation memorandum.

### 8.3 Auditor

Brennock Lyle LLP, the Funds' independent auditor, examines the Funds' valuations as part of its annual audit. The Controller provides the auditor with the Committee's minutes, all Level 3 valuation memoranda and the reports of the independent valuation firm.

### 8.4 Conflicts of Interest

No person whose compensation is determined by the performance of a particular position may approve its value. The Chief Compliance Officer reviews the Committee's minutes and the override log each quarter for indications of bias and reports any concern to the Management Committee.

## SECTION 9. RECORDKEEPING AND POLICY REVIEW

### 9.1 Records

All pricing data, challenge and override records, stale price reports, Level 3 valuation memoranda, back-testing results and Committee minutes are retained in accordance with Section 13 of the Compliance Manual.

### 9.2 Policy Review

The Committee reviews this Policy at least annually and recommends any amendments to the Management Committee. The Chief Compliance Officer reviews the Policy as part of the annual compliance review.
