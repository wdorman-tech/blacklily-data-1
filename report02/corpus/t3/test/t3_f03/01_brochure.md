## Item 5. Fees and Compensation

### 5.1 Advisory Fee

The Firm charges an annual advisory fee based on the aggregate value of all assets under management in a Family Relationship, calculated on a tiered basis as follows:

| Family Relationship assets | Annual rate |
|---|---|
| First $25,000,000 | 0.80% |
| Next $25,000,000 (above $25,000,000 to $50,000,000) | 0.60% |
| Next $50,000,000 (above $50,000,000 to $100,000,000) | 0.45% |
| Next $150,000,000 (above $100,000,000 to $250,000,000) | 0.30% |
| Above $250,000,000 | 0.20% |

Each rate applies only to the portion of Family Relationship assets within its tier. Reporting-only assets are billed at an annual rate of 0.05%. The Firm's complete Schedule of Fees is delivered to each client before the Agreement is signed, and the Agreement controls in the event of any inconsistency with this brochure.

### 5.2 Calculation and Billing

The advisory fee is billed quarterly in advance, at one-quarter of the annual fee, based on the aggregate market value of the Family Relationship on the last business day of the preceding calendar quarter. The fee for a partial first quarter is prorated based on the number of days remaining in the quarter. Additions to or withdrawals from a single account exceeding $2,000,000 within a quarter result in a prorated adjustment on the following quarter's invoice.

Values used for billing are determined under the Firm's Valuation Policy and may differ from values reported by the custodian, principally for private investments. Because the value of private investments affects the fee, the Firm has an incentive to value them favorably. See "Valuation Risk" in Item 8.3. Fees are deducted from custodial accounts designated by the client under a written authorization delivered to the custodian or, at the client's election, invoiced and payable within 30 days.

### 5.3 Minimum Fee and Negotiability

The minimum annual advisory fee is $150,000 per Family Relationship. Fees, including the minimum, are negotiable based on the complexity of a family's affairs, the scope of services, the composition of assets and the terms of legacy engagements. Any non-standard fee arrangement requires the written approval of the Managing Partner. As a result, some clients pay lower fees than others for comparable services.

### 5.4 Trust Company Fees

When a client appoints QHTC as trustee, QHTC charges trustee fees under its own schedule. Trustee fees charged by Quenby Holt Trust Company are in addition to, and are not offset against, the Firm's advisory fee. Trust assets remain part of the Family Relationship for purposes of calculating the advisory fee. The combined fees may be higher than a family would pay if it appointed an unaffiliated trustee that charges lower fees, or an individual trustee who serves without compensation.

### 5.5 Other Fees and Expenses

The advisory fee does not include custodial fees charged by Norrell Bay Trust & Custody, N.A. or Corvedale Clay Securities, LLC; brokerage commissions, markups, markdowns and spreads; fees of third-party managers; management fees, performance fees, carried interest and expenses of mutual funds, exchange-traded funds and private funds, which are borne indirectly by the investing client; fees of the family's attorneys and accountants; or supplemental fees listed in the Schedule of Fees. Neither the Firm nor any of its employees accepts compensation for the sale of securities or other investment products. Where a mutual fund offers more than one share class, the Firm selects the lowest-cost class available to the account on the custodian's platform.

### 5.6 Termination and Refunds

Either the client or the Firm may terminate the Agreement on 30 days' written notice. Upon termination, the advisory fee is prorated to the effective date of termination, and any unearned fee paid in advance is refunded within 30 days after that date. A client who terminates within five business days of signing the Agreement receives a full refund of any fee paid. No termination fee is charged.

## Item 8. Methods of Analysis, Investment Strategies and Risk of Loss

### 8.1 Methods of Analysis

The Investment Committee meets monthly. It sets the Firm's capital market assumptions and strategic asset allocation ranges and maintains the approved lists of strategies, managers and funds. For individual securities, the Firm relies principally on fundamental analysis of financial statements and filings, competitive position, balance sheet strength, management and valuation relative to expected cash flows. For fixed income, the Firm analyzes credit quality, concentration, call features, tax status and the yield curve. For third-party managers and private funds, the Firm conducts investment due diligence (strategy, team, track record, portfolio construction and terms) and operational due diligence (governance, valuation practices, service providers, audited financial statements and regulatory history). The Head of Private Investments leads private fund due diligence, and the Investment Committee must approve each fund before any client commits capital.

### 8.2 Investment Strategies

The Firm implements a strategic asset allocation for each Family Relationship within the ranges set in its IPS, on a tax-aware basis that considers asset location, realization of gains and losses and the use of municipal securities. Principal strategies are: **Core Equity**, a concentrated portfolio of 30 to 45 large and mid capitalization United States companies; **fixed income** portfolios of investment grade municipal, Treasury, agency and corporate bonds; **third-party managers** for international, small capitalization and high yield mandates; **private markets** funds and co-investments in private equity, venture capital, private credit and real estate, subscribed for in the name of each investing client; and **concentrated position strategies**, including staged sales, charitable transfers, exchange funds and collars implemented at Corvedale Clay Securities, LLC.

### 8.3 Risk of Loss

Investing in securities involves risk of loss that clients should be prepared to bear. The Firm does not guarantee the performance of any account. The following are the principal risks of the Firm's strategies; not every risk applies to every Family Relationship.

**Market Risk.** Securities may decline in value because of economic recession, inflation, changes in interest rates, political events, public health events and changes in investor sentiment. Diversification does not eliminate market risk.

**Interest Rate and Credit Risk.** Fixed income securities decline in value when interest rates rise. Issuers may fail to pay interest or principal, and downgrades may reduce market value before any default. Municipal securities are also affected by the fiscal condition of the issuer and by changes in tax law.

**Equity Concentration Risk.** The Core Equity strategy holds a limited number of issuers. A decline in a single holding has a larger effect than it would on a broadly diversified portfolio. Many families also hold legacy positions in a single security, often with a low tax basis, which the family may elect to retain for tax, control or personal reasons.

**Liquidity and Capital Call Risk.** Private funds and co-investments are illiquid, generally cannot be redeemed, require the general partner's consent to transfer and have terms of ten years or longer. Funds call committed capital over several years. A client that fails to meet a capital call may forfeit all or part of its interest.

**Valuation Risk.** Private investments are valued under the Firm's Valuation Policy, principally on the basis of values reported by fund sponsors, which are estimates and are often reported months after the valuation date. Values used in client reports and fee calculations may differ materially from the value realized on disposition. The Firm's fee increases with the reported value of private investments, which creates a conflict of interest that the Valuation Policy is designed to manage.

**Third-Party Manager Risk.** The Firm does not control the day-to-day decisions of third-party managers or fund sponsors, which may deviate from their stated strategies, suffer operational failures or engage in misconduct. Because the Firm recommends the same funds to many families, a single failure may affect multiple Family Relationships.

**Leverage and Options Risk.** Securities-based lines of credit magnify losses, and a decline in collateral value may force repayment or sales at an inopportune time. Collars limit upside participation, may create tax consequences and involve counterparty risk.

**Tax Risk.** Tax-aware management relies on current law and on information the client provides. Changes in law or incomplete information may reduce the benefit of tax management. The Firm does not guarantee any tax outcome.

**Allocation Risk.** Opportunities of limited capacity cannot be made available to every family that wants them. A family may receive a smaller allocation than it requested, or none.

**Cybersecurity and Operational Risk.** The Firm, its custodians and its service providers depend on information systems that may be disrupted or compromised through cyber attacks, system failures or human error, which may interrupt trading and reporting, expose confidential family information or enable fraudulent transfers.

**Custodian and Counterparty Risk.** Client assets are exposed to the insolvency or operational failure of a custodian, broker-dealer or option counterparty, and recovery of segregated assets may be delayed.

## Item 11. Code of Ethics, Participation or Interest in Client Transactions and Personal Trading

### 11.1 Code of Ethics

The Firm has adopted a Code of Ethics under Rule 204A-1 of the Investment Advisers Act of 1940 (the "Advisers Act"). The Code applies to all officers, members and employees of the Firm and to officers and employees of QHTC who have access to nonpublic information about client transactions or holdings. It sets standards of business conduct based on the Firm's fiduciary duties, requires reporting of personal holdings and transactions, prohibits the misuse of material nonpublic information, and governs gifts, entertainment, political contributions and outside activities. A copy is available to any client or prospective client upon request.

### 11.2 Personal Trading

Employees may buy or sell for their own accounts securities that the Firm also buys or sells for clients, which creates a risk that an employee could trade ahead of clients. The Code therefore requires pre-clearance of personal transactions through the Firm's compliance system, subject to limited exemptions; prohibits personal trades within blackout periods around Core Equity trades; imposes a minimum holding period; prohibits participation in initial public offerings; requires the prior approval of the Chief Compliance Officer for private placements; and requires quarterly transaction reports and annual holdings reports, which the compliance department reviews.

### 11.3 Participation or Interest in Client Transactions

The Firm does not effect principal transactions or agency cross transactions. Cross trades between accounts of unrelated Family Relationships are prohibited. Transfers of securities between accounts within the same Family Relationship are permitted at the custodian's closing price, without commission, with the approval of the Chief Compliance Officer. Employees may invest in private funds and co-investments that the Firm recommends to clients only after the allocations of all eligible Family Relationships have been satisfied, only with the approval of the Chief Compliance Officer, and only on the same terms available to clients.

## Item 12. Brokerage Practices

### 12.1 Selection and Recommendation of Custodians

The Firm recommends that clients establish accounts with Norrell Bay Trust & Custody, N.A. ("Norrell Bay"), and clients enter into custodial agreements directly with Norrell Bay. In recommending Norrell Bay, the Firm considers its financial strength, custody and trust accounting capabilities, data feeds to the Firm's reporting platform, ability to hold alternative investments and process capital calls, execution quality and overall cost. For families that require securities-based lines of credit or option strategies on concentrated positions, the Firm recommends accounts at Corvedale Clay Securities, LLC ("Corvedale Clay"). Clients may use a different custodian, which may limit the Firm's ability to aggregate trades and may increase costs. The Trade Oversight Committee reviews the Firm's custodial relationships annually in connection with its best execution review.

### 12.2 Custodial Services; No Soft Dollars

Norrell Bay makes available institutional services, including an electronic trading platform, electronic statements, data feeds, fee deduction and access to institutional share classes. These services are not conditioned on the Firm directing a specified amount of trading to Norrell Bay, but their availability gives the Firm an incentive to recommend Norrell Bay. The Firm does not have soft dollar arrangements and does not direct client brokerage in exchange for research or other products and services. The Firm does not consider client referrals in selecting broker-dealers or custodians.

### 12.3 Directed Brokerage

A client may direct the Firm to use a specific broker-dealer. In that case the Firm may be unable to aggregate the client's orders or negotiate commissions, and the client may pay higher costs or receive less favorable execution.

### 12.4 Aggregation and Allocation

The Firm may aggregate orders for the same security across accounts when doing so is consistent with best execution and fair to each participating account. Each account receives the average price and bears its own transaction costs. Aggregated orders are allocated under a written pre-allocation prepared before the order is placed, and partially filled orders are allocated pro rata. Opportunities of limited capacity in private funds and co-investments are allocated under written procedures adopted by the Investment Committee that consider each family's eligibility, suitability and IPS target allocation.

### 12.5 Trade Errors

The Firm corrects trade errors promptly so that the affected account is restored to the position it would have occupied had the error not occurred. The Firm bears any loss resulting from an error for which it is responsible, and any gain is retained in the client's account.

## Item 13. Review of Accounts

### 13.1 Periodic Reviews

Each Family Relationship is assigned a Relationship Lead, who is a senior advisor of the Firm, and a supporting associate. The Relationship Lead reviews each account at least quarterly for consistency with the IPS, including allocation relative to targets, projected cash needs and capital calls, adherence to restrictions and tax matters, and reviews the IPS with the family at a formal meeting at least annually. The Investment Committee reviews the Core Equity strategy and the approved managers and funds monthly. Operations staff reconciles positions with custodian data each business day. Additional reviews are triggered by changes in a family's circumstances, liquidity events and large contributions or withdrawals.

### 13.2 Reports

Clients receive quarterly consolidated reports through the Firm's reporting portal showing holdings, performance net of fees, allocation relative to IPS targets, private investment commitments, contributions, distributions and unfunded commitments, and fees charged. These reports supplement, and do not replace, the account statements that clients receive directly from their qualified custodians.

## Item 15. Custody

### 15.1 Qualified Custodians

Client assets are held by Norrell Bay Trust & Custody, N.A., a national banking association, which serves as the qualified custodian for substantially all client accounts. A limited number of accounts, principally accounts that require securities-based lines of credit or option strategies on concentrated positions, are held at Corvedale Clay Securities, LLC, a broker-dealer registered with the SEC. Uncertificated private fund interests that are transferable only with the prior consent of the issuer are recorded on the books of the issuer in the name of the client, as permitted by Rule 206(4)-2 under the Advisers Act.

### 15.2 Circumstances in Which the Firm Has Custody

The Firm is deemed to have custody of client assets because (1) it has authority to deduct advisory fees from client accounts; (2) many families authorize the Firm, through standing letters of authorization held by the custodian, to transfer funds to third parties for bill payment, and the Firm complies with the conditions set out in SEC staff guidance for those arrangements; (3) QHTC, a related person, serves as trustee of client trusts; (4) employees serve in an individual capacity as trustees, executors or holders of powers of attorney for certain clients; and (5) the Firm holds signatory authority over certain family operating accounts at banks for the payment of household expenses.

### 15.3 Surprise Examination

The Firm engages Aldwick Brennan LLP, an independent public accountant, to conduct an annual surprise examination of client funds and securities over which the Firm or a related person has custody by reason of trustee appointments, individual appointments of employees or bill payment authority. The examination is conducted at a time chosen by the accountant without prior notice to the Firm, and the accountant files Form ADV-E with the SEC within 120 days after the examination date.

### 15.4 Account Statements

Clients receive account statements directly from their qualified custodian at least quarterly and should compare them with the reports provided by the Firm. Differences may arise from the timing of valuations, accrued income and the valuation of private investments.

## Item 16. Investment Discretion

The Firm accepts discretionary authority under a limited power of attorney contained in the Agreement, which permits the Firm, without prior consultation with the client, to determine the securities to be bought or sold, the amounts, the timing of transactions and the selection and termination of third-party managers, subject to the IPS and any client restrictions. The Firm does not sign private fund subscription documents on a client's behalf; each commitment requires the signature of the client or its trustee. For non-discretionary accounts, the client approves each transaction before it is placed, which may delay implementation and exclude the account from aggregated orders.

## Item 17. Voting Client Securities

### 17.1 Proxy Voting Authority

The Firm votes proxies for securities held in discretionary accounts, other than securities held in sleeves managed by third-party managers, unless the client has retained voting authority in writing. The Firm has engaged Stannard Proxy Services, Inc. to provide proxy research, vote recommendations under guidelines adopted by the Firm, and vote execution and recordkeeping. Third-party managers vote proxies for their sleeves under their own policies, which the Firm reviews in due diligence.

### 17.2 Conflicts of Interest

Where the Firm has a conflict of interest in a vote, for example because the issuer appears on the Firm's Client-Affiliated Issuer List, the Firm votes in accordance with the recommendation of Stannard Proxy Services, Inc. without exercising discretion, and the Chief Compliance Officer documents the conflict. A family may direct the vote of securities held in its own accounts.

### 17.3 Private Fund Consents and Class Actions

The Firm reviews consents and amendments relating to private funds and makes recommendations, but the client or its trustee executes them. The Firm does not file class action claims on behalf of clients. Clients may obtain the Firm's proxy voting policies and their voting records from the Chief Compliance Officer.
