## SECTION 1. INTRODUCTION AND SCOPE

### 1.1 Purpose

This Manual sets out the written policies and procedures that Quenby Holt Family Office, LLC (the "Firm") has adopted under Rule 206(4)-7 under the Investment Advisers Act of 1940 (the "Advisers Act") to prevent violations of the Advisers Act and the rules thereunder, together with the Firm's Code of Ethics under Rule 204A-1. The Manual is supplemented by the Firm's Valuation Policy and Business Continuity Plan, each of which is incorporated by reference. Where this Manual is more restrictive than applicable law, the Manual governs the conduct of Supervised Persons.

### 1.2 Persons Covered

This Manual applies to all members, officers and employees of the Firm, and to any other person who provides investment advice on behalf of the Firm and is subject to its supervision and control (each, a "Supervised Person"). Officers and employees of Quenby Holt Trust Company ("QHTC") who have access to nonpublic information regarding the Firm's client transactions or holdings are Supervised Persons for purposes of Sections 3, 4, 5 and 7. Temporary staff and consultants with access to Firm systems are subject to Sections 7, 11, 12 and 14 and must sign the Firm's confidentiality undertaking before access is granted.

### 1.3 Definitions

"Access Person" means any Supervised Person who has access to nonpublic information regarding any client's purchase or sale of securities or the portfolio holdings of any client, or who is involved in making securities recommendations to clients. All Firm employees are designated Access Persons.

"Reportable Account" means any account in which an Access Person has a direct or indirect beneficial interest and that holds or can hold Reportable Securities, including accounts of the Access Person's spouse, domestic partner, minor children and any other relative sharing the same household.

"Reportable Security" has the meaning given in Rule 204A-1 and excludes direct obligations of the United States government, bankers' acceptances, bank certificates of deposit, commercial paper, high quality short-term debt instruments, money market fund shares and shares of open-end funds that are not advised by the Firm.

"Family Relationship" means all accounts of a client family aggregated under a single investment management agreement.

"Compliance System" means the Tollard Compliance Suite, through which pre-clearance requests, holdings and transaction reports, gifts and entertainment logs, political contribution requests and attestations are submitted and retained.

## SECTION 3. CODE OF ETHICS

### 3.1 Standards of Business Conduct

The Firm is a fiduciary to each of its clients. Supervised Persons must place the interests of clients ahead of their own and the Firm's, must not take inappropriate advantage of their positions, must avoid or disclose conflicts of interest, and must comply with applicable federal securities laws. Supervised Persons must keep confidential all information about client families, including their holdings, transactions, family relationships, estate plans and business interests, and may use that information only to serve the family.

### 3.2 Acknowledgment

Each Supervised Person must acknowledge in writing, through the Compliance System, receipt of the Code and this Manual upon hire, upon each amendment and annually.

### 3.3 Reporting Violations

Supervised Persons must report promptly to the CCO any violation or apparent violation of the Code or this Manual of which they become aware, including violations by others. If the matter involves the CCO, the report is made to the Managing Partner. Reports may be made anonymously in writing. The Firm prohibits retaliation against any person who makes a report in good faith.

### 3.4 Sanctions

Violations may result in a written warning, disgorgement of profits, reversal of trades at the Access Person's expense, suspension of personal trading privileges, reduction of discretionary compensation, suspension or termination of employment, and referral to regulatory authorities. Disgorged amounts are donated to a charity selected by the CCO. The CCO records each violation and sanction in the Compliance System and reports material violations to the Management Committee.

## SECTION 4. PERSONAL TRADING AND PRE-CLEARANCE

### 4.1 Reportable Accounts

Each Access Person must disclose every Reportable Account and must arrange for the broker-dealer or custodian to deliver duplicate statements or an electronic data feed to the Compliance System. A Reportable Account over which the Access Person has no direct or indirect influence or control, such as a discretionary account managed by an unaffiliated adviser, may be exempted from pre-clearance by the CCO upon receipt of a written certification from the account manager, but remains subject to reporting.

### 4.2 Pre-Clearance

Except as provided in Section 4.3, an Access Person must obtain pre-clearance through the Compliance System before effecting any transaction in a Reportable Security, including exchange-traded funds and closed-end funds. Pre-clearance requests are approved by the CCO or the Compliance Manager. The CCO's own requests are approved by the Chief Financial Officer. Pre-clearance approval is valid only for the business day on which it is granted. Orders not executed by the close of that business day must be resubmitted. Good-until-cancelled and other standing orders are not permitted unless pre-cleared each day on which they may execute. The CCO or Compliance Manager will deny a request if the security is on the Restricted List, if there is an open or pending order for the security in any client account, or if the transaction would violate Section 4.4 or 4.5.

### 4.3 De Minimis Exemption

Transactions in the equity securities of an issuer with a market capitalization of at least $10 billion are exempt from pre-clearance where the Access Person's aggregate transactions in that issuer do not exceed $25,000 in any 30 calendar day period. Transactions relying on this exemption remain subject to reporting, to the holding period in Section 4.5, and to the prohibitions in Sections 4.4 and 7. The exemption is not available for securities on the Restricted List or the Client-Affiliated Issuer List. Broad-based index exchange-traded funds designated by the CCO on the Compliance System are also exempt from pre-clearance but remain reportable.

### 4.4 Prohibited and Restricted Transactions

Access Persons may not:

1. acquire any security in an initial public offering;
2. acquire any security in a limited offering, including a private fund or co-investment, without the prior written approval of the CCO, which will be granted for a private fund or co-investment recommended to clients only after the allocations of all eligible Family Relationships have been satisfied;
3. trade in any security while in possession of material nonpublic information about the issuer;
4. trade in any security on the Restricted List;
5. purchase or sell options on, or sell short, any security held in the Core Equity strategy; or
6. trade ahead of, or on the basis of knowledge of, any client order.

### 4.5 Holding Period and Blackout Periods

Reportable Securities acquired by an Access Person must be held for at least 30 calendar days. The CCO may grant exceptions in cases of hardship, provided the security has not been purchased or sold for a client account in the preceding 30 days. Profits realized in violation of the holding period are disgorged. No Access Person may trade a security within three business days before or after a transaction in that security for the Core Equity strategy. Transactions in securities held only in sleeves managed by third-party managers are not subject to the blackout period.

### 4.6 Holdings and Transaction Reports

Each Access Person must submit through the Compliance System: an initial holdings report within 10 days after becoming an Access Person, with information current as of a date not more than 45 days before the report; an annual holdings report no later than February 14 of each year, with information current as of December 31 of the prior year; and a quarterly transaction report no later than 30 days after the end of each calendar quarter, covering every transaction in a Reportable Security during the quarter. Transactions reported through an electronic data feed satisfy the quarterly transaction report requirement for the covered account if the Access Person confirms the completeness of the feed in the Compliance System within the same deadline.

### 4.7 Review of Personal Trading

The Compliance Manager reviews all holdings and transaction reports and compares personal transactions with client trading, the Restricted List and the Client-Affiliated Issuer List. The CCO's reports are reviewed by the Chief Financial Officer. Exceptions are documented in the Compliance System and escalated to the CCO.

## SECTION 5. GIFTS AND ENTERTAINMENT

### 5.1 General Principles

Supervised Persons may not give or accept any gift, entertainment or other benefit that could reasonably be expected to influence the recipient's business judgment or to create an obligation. Gifts of cash or cash equivalents, including gift cards, are prohibited regardless of amount. Soliciting gifts or entertainment is prohibited.

### 5.2 Gifts

No Supervised Person may give or accept gifts with an aggregate value exceeding $250 per source or recipient per calendar year without the prior written approval of the CCO. Gifts of nominal value bearing a company logo and customary holiday food items shared with the office are excluded from the aggregate. All gifts given or received, other than excluded items, must be logged in the Compliance System within five business days.

### 5.3 Business Entertainment

Business entertainment is permitted where the provider attends and the entertainment is reasonable in frequency and value. Entertainment with a value exceeding $500 per person per event requires the prior approval of the CCO. Supervised Persons may not accept travel or lodging paid for by a third-party manager, fund sponsor or broker-dealer. The Firm pays its own travel costs for manager due diligence visits.

### 5.4 Gifts from Client Families

Client families frequently give gifts to Firm personnel on holidays and family occasions. Such gifts are subject to Section 5.2. A gift from a client family that exceeds the threshold may be accepted only with the CCO's written approval, which will consider whether the gift could be perceived as influencing the treatment of that family relative to others. Supervised Persons may not accept bequests or be named as beneficiaries under any client's estate plan, other than a relative's.

## SECTION 6. POLITICAL CONTRIBUTIONS

### 6.1 Regulatory Framework

Rule 206(4)-5 under the Advisers Act prohibits an adviser from providing advisory services for compensation to a government entity within two years after a contribution to certain officials of that entity by the adviser or any of its covered associates. The Firm does not currently advise any government entity, but applies this Section so that no contribution restricts the Firm's future business.

### 6.2 Pre-Clearance of Contributions

Every Supervised Person must obtain the CCO's pre-clearance through the Compliance System before making any contribution to, or soliciting or coordinating contributions for, a state or local official, a candidate for state or local office, a state or local political party, or a political action committee. Pre-clearance is also required for contributions to a candidate for federal office who currently holds state or local office. Contributions within the de minimis amounts permitted by the rule ($350 per election for a candidate for whom the contributor is entitled to vote and $150 per election otherwise) still require pre-clearance.

### 6.3 New Employees

Each new Supervised Person must disclose all contributions described in Section 6.2 made during the two years before hire, or the six months before hire for a person who will not solicit clients, so that the CCO can determine whether any restriction applies.

### 6.4 Prohibited Conduct

Supervised Persons may not make contributions through spouses, family members, entities or other persons to avoid this Section, may not bundle contributions, and may not use Firm resources for political activity.

## SECTION 7. INSIDER TRADING AND MATERIAL NONPUBLIC INFORMATION

### 7.1 Policy

No Supervised Person may trade, for any client or personal account, in a security while in possession of material nonpublic information ("MNPI") about the issuer, or communicate MNPI to others in violation of law. Information is material if a reasonable investor would consider it important in making an investment decision, and nonpublic until it has been broadly disseminated. Client families are frequently founders, directors and senior officers of public companies, and Supervised Persons must treat information obtained from families about those companies as presumptively MNPI.

### 7.2 Restricted List and Watch List

The CCO maintains a Restricted List of issuers about which the Firm or a Supervised Person may possess MNPI, and a Watch List of issuers under heightened review. No trade in a Restricted List issuer may be effected for any client account or Reportable Account while the issuer remains on the list. The Watch List is confidential to the compliance department.

### 7.3 Client-Affiliated Issuers

The CCO maintains a Client-Affiliated Issuer List of public companies of which a member of a client family is an officer, director or beneficial owner of 10% or more of any class of equity securities. No transaction in a security of an issuer on the Client-Affiliated Issuer List may be effected for any client account or Reportable Account without the prior approval of the CCO. For a transaction in the affiliated family member's own accounts, the CCO will also require written confirmation from the family member, or the issuer's general counsel, that the transaction is permitted under the issuer's trading window or is made under a Rule 10b5-1 plan, and that any required Rule 144 or Section 16 filings will be made.

### 7.4 Outside Board Service

No Supervised Person may serve as a director of a public company without the prior approval of the Management Committee. The CCO places the issuer of any approved directorship on the Restricted List.

### 7.5 Escalation

A Supervised Person who believes he or she may have received MNPI must immediately notify the CCO and refrain from trading and from discussing the information with anyone else. The CCO will determine whether to add the issuer to the Restricted List and will document the determination.

## SECTION 8. MARKETING AND COMMUNICATIONS REVIEW

### 8.1 Scope

This Section applies to every advertisement within the meaning of Rule 206(4)-1, including presentations to prospective families, the Firm's website, newsletters, social media content and any communication that offers the Firm's advisory services to more than one person.

### 8.2 Review and Approval

Every advertisement must be approved in writing by the CCO before first use. The Compliance Manager may approve advertisements that contain no performance information. Approvals and final versions are retained in the Compliance System.

### 8.3 Performance Presentation

Any presentation of gross performance must be accompanied by net performance calculated with equal prominence over the same periods. Performance for any portfolio must be presented for one-, five- and ten-year periods, or since inception if shorter. The Firm does not use hypothetical or extracted performance in advertisements. Composite performance of the Core Equity strategy is calculated by the Controller and reviewed by the CCO before each use.

### 8.4 Testimonials, Endorsements and Third-Party Ratings

The Firm does not use testimonials of client families in any advertisement. Endorsements and third-party ratings may be used only with the prior approval of the CCO and the disclosures required by Rule 206(4)-1.

### 8.5 Social Media

Supervised Persons may share Firm content approved under Section 8.2 on professional networking sites but may not create content describing the Firm's services, investment views or client matters.

## SECTION 9. TRADE AGGREGATION AND ALLOCATION

### 9.1 Aggregation

Orders for the same security for more than one account may be aggregated when the portfolio manager determines that aggregation is consistent with best execution and with the IPS of each account, and that no account will be systematically disadvantaged. Before an aggregated order is placed, the portfolio manager records a pre-allocation specifying the participating accounts and the amount for each.

### 9.2 Allocation of Aggregated Orders

Each account participating in an aggregated order receives the average execution price and bears its own custodial transaction charges. A partially filled order is allocated pro rata in proportion to the pre-allocation, except that the portfolio manager may allocate to fewer accounts where a pro rata allocation would give an account a position too small to be meaningful, provided the basis is documented. Accounts of Access Persons included in an aggregated order receive allocations only after client accounts are filled in full.

### 9.3 Deviations

Any allocation that departs from the pre-allocation, other than as permitted in Section 9.2, requires the approval of the CCO before the end of the trade date, with a written explanation retained in the order management system.

### 9.4 Limited Private Investment Opportunities

Private funds with limited capacity and co-investment opportunities are allocated as follows. The Head of Private Investments presents the opportunity to the Investment Committee, which first determines which Family Relationships are eligible, taking into account investor qualification status, suitability, liquidity, concentration and the IPS. Where aggregate eligible demand exceeds available capacity, the Investment Committee allocates the opportunity among eligible Family Relationships pro rata in proportion to each Family Relationship's shortfall relative to the private markets target allocation in its Investment Policy Statement, subject to a minimum allocation of $250,000 per Family Relationship. The Head of Private Investments documents the eligibility determination and the allocation, and the CCO reviews each allocation before commitments are submitted. Supervised Persons may participate only after the demand of all eligible Family Relationships has been satisfied.

### 9.5 Cross Trades and Intra-Family Transfers

Cross trades between accounts of different Family Relationships are prohibited. Transfers between accounts within the same Family Relationship are permitted at the custodian's closing price, without commission, with the prior approval of the CCO.

## SECTION 10. BEST EXECUTION

### 10.1 Duty

The Firm seeks to execute client transactions so that the total cost or proceeds in each transaction is the most favorable under the circumstances, considering price, commission, speed and likelihood of execution, market impact, the broker-dealer's financial condition and the quality of its services.

### 10.2 Trade Oversight Committee

The Trade Oversight Committee, consisting of the Chief Investment Officer, the Director of Operations and the CCO, meets semi-annually, in January and July, to review execution quality. The review covers custodian execution quality reports, commission and markup levels on fixed income transactions compared with contemporaneous market data, the performance of each approved broker-dealer and trade errors, and, at the July meeting, the Firm's recommendation of custodians. The Committee's conclusions and any resulting changes are recorded in minutes retained by the CCO.

### 10.3 Approved Broker-Dealers; No Soft Dollars

Transactions may be executed only through broker-dealers on the approved list maintained by the Trade Oversight Committee. The Firm does not accept research or other products or services in exchange for client brokerage.

## SECTION 11. CYBERSECURITY AND INFORMATION SECURITY

### 11.1 Program and Responsibility

The Firm maintains a written information security program designed to protect the confidentiality, integrity and availability of client information and Firm systems. Ingrid P. Solheim, Director of Technology and Information Security, serves as Information Security Officer (the "ISO") and reports on the program to the Management Committee at least quarterly.

### 11.2 Access Controls

Multi-factor authentication is required for email, remote access, the reporting platform and every custodian portal. Access is granted on a least-privilege basis and is reviewed quarterly by the ISO with each department head. Access of a departing employee is disabled no later than the end of the employee's last day. Firm laptops and mobile devices are encrypted and managed centrally.

### 11.3 Monitoring and Managed Services

Pellow Stack IT, LLC provides managed information technology services, including endpoint management and patching. Critical security patches are applied within 14 days of release. Northgale Security Operations, Inc. provides 24-hour monitoring of the Firm's endpoints, email and network through a managed detection and response service and escalates alerts to the ISO in accordance with agreed severity levels.

### 11.4 Vulnerability Management and Penetration Testing

Northgale Security Operations, Inc. performs vulnerability scans of the Firm's external and internal networks quarterly. Ostler Vane Security Testing, LLC performs an external penetration test annually, and its findings are reported to the Management Committee with a remediation plan and target dates tracked by the ISO. The ISO conducts phishing simulations quarterly, and employees who fail a simulation complete supplemental training.

### 11.5 Vendor Oversight

Before engaging any vendor that will store or access client information, and annually for each critical vendor thereafter, the ISO obtains and reviews the vendor's most recent SOC 2 Type II report or equivalent and its incident notification commitments. Contracts with such vendors must require notice to the Firm as soon as possible, and in any event within 72 hours, after the vendor becomes aware of a breach affecting the Firm's customer information.

### 11.6 Incident Response

Any Supervised Person who suspects a cybersecurity incident must report it immediately to the ISO. The ISO acts as Incident Response Lead and notifies the CCO within one hour of determining that an incident may involve client information or funds. Northgale Security Operations, Inc. will be engaged to perform containment, forensic investigation and eradication. The ISO and CCO will engage the outside breach counsel designated in the Business Continuity Plan and ask the Chief Financial Officer to notify the Firm's cyber liability insurer. Where client accounts may be affected, the Director of Operations will ask the relevant custodian to apply heightened review to disbursements. The CCO determines, with counsel, whether notice to clients, regulators or law enforcement is required.

### 11.7 Cyber Liability Insurance

The Firm maintains a cyber liability insurance policy covering incident response costs, business interruption, funds transfer fraud and third-party liability. The Chief Financial Officer reviews the adequacy of coverage annually at renewal with the ISO and the CCO.

### 11.8 Disbursement and Wire Controls

Every request to transfer funds to a third party, or to change standing payment instructions, must be confirmed by a telephone call to the client at a number on file, not a number supplied in the request. Disbursements above $100,000 require approval by two authorized employees. Requests received only by email or text are never executed without that call.

## SECTION 12. PRIVACY OF CLIENT INFORMATION

### 12.1 Regulation S-P

The Firm protects nonpublic personal information of clients in accordance with Regulation S-P. The Firm delivers its privacy notice at the inception of each client relationship. Because the Firm shares nonpublic personal information only as permitted by the exceptions in Regulation S-P and has not changed its policies, it is not required to deliver an annual privacy notice.

### 12.2 Sharing of Information

The Firm shares nonpublic personal information with QHTC, custodians, the reporting platform, the family's attorneys and accountants and other service providers only as needed to provide services, and does not share information with any non-affiliated third party for marketing purposes.

### 12.3 Family Information Authorizations

Information about one family member's accounts is shared with another family member only in accordance with a written Family Information Authorization signed by the account owner or trustee, which records which family members may receive which information. Relationship Leads must confirm the authorization before sharing any account information within a family.

### 12.4 Customer Notification

If sensitive customer information has been, or is reasonably likely to have been, accessed or used without authorization, the Firm will notify each affected individual as soon as practicable, and not later than 30 days after becoming aware of the incident, unless the CCO determines, after a reasonable investigation, that the information has not been and is not reasonably likely to be used in a manner that would result in substantial harm or inconvenience.

### 12.5 Disposal

Records containing client information are disposed of by shredding or secure electronic destruction. Vendors that dispose of Firm records must certify destruction in writing.

## SECTION 13. BOOKS AND RECORDS

### 13.1 Retention Period

The Firm retains all books and records required by Rule 204-2 for seven years from the end of the fiscal year during which the last entry was made on the record, the first two years in an appropriate office of the Firm. Partnership and organizational documents of the Firm are retained for the life of the Firm and for three years after its termination.

### 13.2 Electronic Storage

Email, collaboration platform messages and business text messages are captured and retained by Arcwell Archive Services, Inc. in a non-rewriteable, non-erasable format, indexed and searchable. Other electronic records are retained on Firm systems with access controls and backups described in the Business Continuity Plan. The Firm must be able to produce any record promptly upon request.

### 13.3 Trust Company Records

QHTC retains its trust records in accordance with South Dakota law and its own policies. Where a record is required of both the Firm and QHTC, each entity retains its own copy.

## SECTION 14. ELECTRONIC COMMUNICATIONS

### 14.1 Approved Channels

Business communications must be conducted only through the Firm's email system, the Firm's collaboration platform and the Firm-issued mobile messaging application, each of which is archived by Arcwell Archive Services, Inc. Communications with families through the reporting portal are retained by the portal provider and captured by the Firm.

### 14.2 Prohibited Channels and Off-Channel Messages

Personal email, personal text messaging and consumer messaging applications may not be used for Firm business. If a client or other person sends a business communication to a Supervised Person through an unapproved channel, the Supervised Person must respond only through an approved channel and must forward the message to the Firm's archive within one business day.

### 14.3 Surveillance

The Compliance Manager reviews messages flagged by lexicon-based surveillance weekly and reviews a random sample of at least 50 messages each month. Reviews and escalations are documented in the archive.

## SECTION 15. ANNUAL REVIEW

### 15.1 Requirement

Rule 206(4)-7 requires the Firm to review, no less frequently than annually, the adequacy of its policies and procedures and the effectiveness of their implementation.

### 15.2 Conduct and Reporting

The CCO conducts the annual review with the assistance of Hethersett Compliance Advisors, LLC. The review covers the prior calendar year and includes forensic testing of personal trading, allocation, fee calculations, custody arrangements, marketing and cybersecurity controls, and consideration of any compliance matters, changes in the Firm's business and changes in applicable law. The CCO delivers a written report of its findings to the Management Committee no later than April 30 of each year. The Management Committee documents its response to each recommendation.

### 15.3 Interim Reviews

The CCO conducts an interim review upon any significant compliance event, change in business, or change in law, and amends this Manual as necessary. Amendments are approved by the Management Committee and communicated to Supervised Persons in writing.

## SECTION 16. TRAINING AND ATTESTATIONS

### 16.1 Training

Each new Supervised Person must complete compliance training covering this Manual and the Code of Ethics within 30 days after the start of employment. All Supervised Persons must complete annual compliance training, delivered in the fourth quarter, which addresses changes in the Manual and law, the prior year's compliance matters and cybersecurity.

### 16.2 Attestations

Each Supervised Person must certify through the Compliance System, by January 31 of each year, that he or she has read and complied with this Manual and the Code of Ethics during the prior year, has disclosed all Reportable Accounts and outside activities, and has reported all gifts, entertainment and political contributions required to be reported.
