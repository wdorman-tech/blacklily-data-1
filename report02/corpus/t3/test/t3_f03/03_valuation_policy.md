## SECTION 1. PURPOSE AND SCOPE

### 1.1 Purpose

This Policy establishes the methods and controls by which Quenby Holt Family Office, LLC (the "Firm") values client holdings for consolidated client reporting and for the calculation of advisory fees. Because the Firm's advisory fee is based on the value of each Family Relationship, including private investments, the Firm has an interest in higher valuations. This Policy is designed to ensure that values are determined consistently, from independent sources wherever available, and with oversight by persons who do not make investment decisions for clients.

### 1.2 Scope

This Policy applies to all assets included in regulatory assets under management and to reporting-only assets included in consolidated reports. The account statements issued by qualified custodians remain the official record of client holdings for custody purposes. Quenby Holt Trust Company ("QHTC") values trust assets for fiduciary accountings under its own procedures, which rely on values determined under this Policy for assets advised by the Firm.

## SECTION 2. VALUATION COMMITTEE

### 2.1 Composition

The Valuation Committee (the "Committee") consists of four voting members: the Chief Financial Officer, the Controller, the Director of Operations and the President of Quenby Holt Trust Company. The Chief Investment Officer, the Head of Private Investments and the Chief Compliance Officer attend as non-voting members. Non-voting members may present information and recommendations but may not vote, and the Head of Private Investments is excused from deliberation on any position for which he or she prepared the valuation recommendation.

### 2.2 Quorum and Voting

A quorum consists of three voting members, one of whom must be the Chief Financial Officer or the Controller. Determinations are made by a majority of the voting members present. A voting member who has a personal interest in a position, including through a Reportable Account or a family relationship with the issuer, must disclose the interest and abstain.

### 2.3 Meetings

The Committee meets quarterly, within 15 business days after the end of each calendar quarter, to approve the valuations of all Level 3 positions as of the quarter end and to review the reports described in Sections 5 through 7. The Chief Financial Officer or the Controller may call a special meeting at any time, including upon a significant market event, a credit event affecting a held issuer, or the receipt of information suggesting that a reported value is unreliable. Minutes of every meeting are kept by the Controller and retained with the supporting materials.

### 2.4 Responsibilities

The Committee is responsible for approving pricing sources and the hierarchy in Section 3; approving the valuation of each Level 3 position; reviewing price challenges, overrides and stale price reports; reviewing back-testing results; engaging and overseeing the independent valuation firm; and reviewing this Policy annually and recommending amendments to the Management Committee.

## SECTION 3. PRICING HIERARCHY AND SOURCES

### 3.1 Hierarchy

Assets are valued using the first available source in the following order: (a) a price from an approved pricing vendor under Sections 3.2 and 3.3; (b) the price provided by the qualified custodian; (c) a broker quotation obtained by Operations from a broker-dealer that is not the counterparty to a pending client transaction; and (d) fair value determined under Section 4. Assets are classified within the fair value hierarchy of ASC 820 (Level 1, Level 2 or Level 3) for reporting purposes.

### 3.2 Listed Securities

Equity securities, exchange-traded funds, closed-end funds and listed options traded on a national securities exchange are valued at the official closing price on the primary exchange, or, if no trade occurred, the mean of the closing bid and ask prices, as supplied by Ostrander Valuation Data, Inc. Securities traded principally on a non-United States exchange are valued at the closing price on that exchange converted to United States dollars at the 4:00 p.m. Eastern time exchange rate supplied by the same vendor.

### 3.3 Fixed Income

For fixed income securities, including municipal, Treasury, agency, corporate and securitized debt, Ostrander Valuation Data, Inc. is the primary pricing source and supplies evaluated bid prices. Kilbride Evaluated Pricing LLC serves as the secondary source and is used when Ostrander does not price a security, when a challenge to an Ostrander price under Section 5 remains unresolved, or when the Controller determines that the Ostrander price is unreliable. Use of the secondary source for any security for more than five consecutive business days is reported to the Committee.

### 3.4 Funds and Cash

Open-end mutual funds and money market funds are valued at the net asset value published by the fund. Cash and bank deposits are valued at their balances as reported by the custodian or depository bank. Separately managed account sleeves of third-party managers are valued using the sources in Sections 3.2 and 3.3, and differences from the manager's own valuations above the tolerances in Section 5.1 are investigated.

### 3.5 Custodian Price Comparison

Each business day Operations compares vendor prices for all positions with the prices reported by Norrell Bay Trust & Custody, N.A. and Corvedale Clay Securities, LLC. The comparison is the basis for the tolerance checks in Section 5.1.

## SECTION 4. PRIVATE INVESTMENTS AND LEVEL 3 METHODOLOGY

### 4.1 Classification

Private investments include interests in private funds, co-investments, direct investments in private companies, and interests in real estate and operating businesses held for families within the Firm's management authority. Where no observable input is available, the investment is classified as Level 3.

### 4.2 Interests in Private Funds

Interests in private funds are valued at the most recent net asset value reported by the general partner or fund administrator, adjusted for capital calls and distributions occurring after the reporting date. The Firm does not apply its own adjustments to a reported net asset value unless the Committee determines that the reported value does not reflect fair value, for example because of a known subsequent event, a reported value that is not based on fair value principles, or a qualified audit opinion. Any such adjustment requires the approval of the Committee and is disclosed in client reports.

### 4.3 Direct Private Investments

Co-investments and direct investments in private companies are valued at fair value using one or more of the following methods, applied consistently from period to period:

1. **Calibration to transaction price.** At acquisition, the investment is valued at cost, and the inputs to subsequent valuations are calibrated to the transaction.
2. **Market approach.** Enterprise value is estimated by applying multiples of earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization, or other appropriate metrics, derived from comparable public companies and precedent transactions, adjusted for differences in size, growth and margins.
3. **Income approach.** Enterprise value is estimated by discounting projected cash flows at a rate reflecting the risk of the investment.
4. **Recent transactions.** A subsequent financing round or secondary sale involving unrelated parties at arm's length is considered, weighted according to the size of the transaction and the rights of the securities involved.

Equity value is allocated among classes of securities in accordance with their rights and preferences. A discount for lack of marketability, generally within a range of 10% to 30%, is applied where the Firm's clients hold minority interests without liquidity rights. The selected method, key inputs and the rationale for any change in method are documented in the valuation memorandum for each position.

### 4.4 Real Estate and Operating Company Interests

Directly held real estate within the Firm's management authority is valued on the basis of an independent appraisal obtained at least every three years, updated in intervening quarters for capital expenditures, material changes in occupancy and changes in capitalization rates observed in the relevant market. Interests in family operating companies that are reporting-only assets are reported at values supplied by the family and are labeled as such in client reports; they are not included in the fee base except at the reporting-only rate.

### 4.5 Documentation

For every Level 3 position, the Controller maintains a valuation memorandum containing the valuation as of each quarter end, the method and inputs used, the sources of information, a comparison to the prior valuation with an explanation of significant changes, and the Committee's approval.

## SECTION 5. PRICE CHALLENGES AND OVERRIDES

### 5.1 Tolerance Checks

A vendor price that differs from the custodian's price by more than 2% for an equity security or exchange-traded fund, or by more than 1% of price for a fixed income security, is reported on the daily exception report. Operations also reports any fixed income price that moves more than 3% from the prior business day without a corresponding movement in comparable securities.

### 5.2 Price Challenges

Operations submits a challenge to the relevant vendor within one business day of identifying an exception that it cannot resolve by reference to market information, providing the evidence supporting an alternative price. The challenge and the vendor's response are logged. If the vendor maintains its price after review, Operations may use the vendor price or propose an override under Section 5.3.

### 5.3 Overrides

If a challenge remains unresolved after two business days, or if Operations or the Controller determines that no vendor or custodian price reflects fair value, an override price may be used. Any override of a price supplied by a pricing source must be approved in advance by the Chief Investment Officer, on the basis of a written recommendation from the Controller stating the proposed price, the reason for the override and the supporting evidence. Overrides remain in effect only until a reliable vendor price becomes available and are reviewed at every business day's exception review.

### 5.4 Override Log

The Controller maintains a log of all overrides, recording the security, the vendor price, the override price, the approver, the dates of approval and removal, and the market value effect on each affected Family Relationship. The log is reported to the Committee at each meeting and reviewed by the Chief Compliance Officer as part of the annual review.

## SECTION 6. STALE PRICE MONITORING

### 6.1 Daily Stale Price Report

Operations produces a daily stale price report that flags any security whose price has not changed for five consecutive business days. Operations investigates each flagged security and documents whether the unchanged price reflects actual market conditions, such as an illiquid municipal issue, or a failure of the pricing feed. A security whose price has not changed for ten consecutive business days is escalated to the Valuation Committee, which determines whether to continue using the price, obtain a broker quotation, or apply a fair value under Section 4.

### 6.2 Private Fund Reporting Lag

Operations flags any private fund interest for which the most recent reported net asset value is dated more than 120 days before the valuation date. The Head of Private Investments contacts the general partner for an updated value, and the Committee considers at its next meeting whether an adjustment under Section 4.2 is warranted.

## SECTION 7. BACK-TESTING

### 7.1 Quarterly Back-Testing

Each quarter, the Controller compares the prices used for positions sold during the quarter with the actual sale prices, and compares the prior quarter-end values of private fund interests with the net asset values subsequently reported by the general partners for the same date. Any variance greater than 5% is investigated and explained in the back-testing report delivered to the Committee.

### 7.2 Level 3 Realization Review

Upon the realization of any direct Level 3 position, the Controller compares the realized proceeds with the valuations reported over the preceding four quarters and presents the comparison to the Committee, which considers whether the methods or inputs used for similar positions should be revised.

## SECTION 8. INDEPENDENT REVIEW

### 8.1 Independent Valuation Firm

Tessier Arkwright Valuation Group performs an independent valuation of each direct Level 3 position with a carrying value above $10,000,000 at least annually, and of every other direct Level 3 position at least once every three years. The independent valuation firm reports to the Committee, and its engagement is approved by the Committee rather than by the investment team. Where the Firm's valuation of a position differs from the independent valuation by more than 10%, the Committee documents the reasons for the value it adopts.

### 8.2 Annual Policy Review

The Committee reviews this Policy at least annually, with the participation of the Chief Compliance Officer, and recommends any amendments to the Management Committee for approval. The Chief Compliance Officer also tests the application of this Policy to advisory fee calculations as part of the annual review of the compliance program.
