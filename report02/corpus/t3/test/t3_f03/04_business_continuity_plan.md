## SECTION 1. PURPOSE AND PLAN OWNERSHIP

### 1.1 Purpose and Scope

This Business Continuity Plan (the "Plan") describes how Quenby Holt Family Office, LLC (the "Firm") will continue to meet its obligations to client families, including trading, cash management, bill payment, capital call funding and reporting, during and after a significant business disruption. The Plan covers the Firm's principal office at 333 Harmon Place, Suite 1400, Minneapolis, Minnesota, all Firm personnel and all systems and vendors on which the Firm depends. Quenby Holt Trust Company ("QHTC") maintains its own plan as required by the South Dakota Division of Banking, and the two plans are coordinated as described in Section 3.3.

### 1.2 Plan Owner

The Plan is owned by Renata S. Oyelaran, Director of Operations, who serves as BCP Coordinator. Ingrid P. Solheim, Director of Technology and Information Security, serves as alternate BCP Coordinator. The BCP Coordinator maintains the Plan, the contact lists and the vendor inventory, directs recovery activities after a declaration, and reports to the Managing Partner. The alternate BCP Coordinator assumes those responsibilities whenever the BCP Coordinator is unavailable.

### 1.3 Approval and Review

The Management Committee approves the Plan at least annually and upon any material change in the Firm's operations, locations or critical vendors. The Chief Compliance Officer reviews the Plan as part of the annual review of the compliance program.

## SECTION 2. DECLARATION AND ESCALATION

### 2.1 Disruption Scenarios

The Plan addresses loss of access to the principal office (including fire, severe weather, utility failure and civil disturbance); loss of systems or data (including ransomware and other cyber incidents); failure of a critical vendor; a pandemic or other public health event that reduces available staff; and a regional disruption affecting the Minneapolis area.

### 2.2 Declaration Authority

A business disruption may be declared by the Managing Partner or, if she is unavailable, by the Chief Financial Officer or, if neither is available, by the BCP Coordinator. The person declaring records the time of declaration, which starts the recovery time objectives in Section 4. Any employee who becomes aware of an event that may require a declaration must notify the BCP Coordinator immediately.

## SECTION 3. ALTERNATE SITE AND REMOTE OPERATIONS

### 3.1 Alternate Site

The Firm's alternate site is a dedicated work-area recovery suite of 12 seats operated by Halvern Recovery Services, LLC in Bloomington, Minnesota. The suite is contracted for the Firm's exclusive use upon declaration and is available for occupancy within four hours of the Firm's notice of declaration. Each seat is equipped with a workstation configured to the Firm's standard image, dual monitors and telephone service capable of receiving forwarded calls from the Firm's main line. The BCP Coordinator maintains the list of personnel assigned to the alternate site, who include trading, operations, bill payment and technology staff.

### 3.2 Remote Operations

All employees are issued Firm laptops and can work remotely through the Firm's secure remote access service, which requires multi-factor authentication. Remote operation is the primary recovery strategy for personnel not assigned to the alternate site. Bill payment and wire approvals performed remotely remain subject to the callback and dual approval controls in the Compliance Manual.

### 3.3 Trust Company Operations

QHTC's office at 101 South Phillips Avenue, Suite 610, Sioux Falls, South Dakota serves as a secondary location for trust administration and for approval of trust distributions. If the Minneapolis area is affected, QHTC staff in Sioux Falls may process urgent distributions from trust accounts under QHTC's own plan, and the Firm's Relationship Leads coordinate with them by telephone and secure email.

## SECTION 4. RECOVERY OBJECTIVES

### 4.1 System Tiers and Objectives

Each system is assigned to a tier according to the effect of its loss on client obligations. Tier 1 systems carry a recovery time objective of four hours and a recovery point objective of one hour. Tier 2 systems carry a recovery time objective of 24 hours and a recovery point objective of 24 hours. Tier 3 systems carry a recovery time objective of five business days and a recovery point objective of 24 hours.

| Tier | Systems | Recovery time objective | Recovery point objective |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tier 1 | Custodian portals and trading platforms, order management system, email, remote access, bill payment platform | 4 hours | 1 hour |
| Tier 2 | Portfolio accounting and reporting platform, compliance system, document management, customer relationship management | 24 hours | 24 hours |
| Tier 3 | Internal file shares for non-client matters, marketing systems, intranet | 5 business days | 24 hours |

### 4.2 Priority of Functions

After a declaration, functions are restored in the following order: (1) protection of personnel; (2) safeguarding of client assets, including heightened review of disbursements; (3) processing of time-sensitive payments, capital calls and trades; (4) communication with families; and (5) reporting and other functions.

## SECTION 5. DATA BACKUP AND CRITICAL VENDORS

### 5.1 Data Backup

Ferncastle Data Vault, Inc. performs the Firm's offsite data backups. Incremental backups of the Firm's file servers, document management system and locally hosted applications are performed every hour, and a full backup is performed each week. Backup copies are replicated to Ferncastle facilities in Omaha, Nebraska and Columbus, Ohio and are immutable for 90 days, so that they cannot be altered or deleted by a person who gains access to Firm systems. Email, messages and collaboration content are also retained by Arcwell Archive Services, Inc. Software-as-a-service applications, including the custodian portals and the reporting platform, are backed up by their providers under contractual commitments reviewed annually by the Director of Technology and Information Security.

### 5.3 Vendor Continuity Due Diligence

The BCP Coordinator obtains from each critical vendor, at engagement and annually thereafter, a summary of its business continuity arrangements and the results of its most recent recovery exercise. For custodians, the Firm maintains contacts for each custodian's service team and secondary operations center, and a written procedure for placing instructions by telephone with callback verification if electronic platforms are unavailable.

## SECTION 6. TESTING

### 6.1 Frequency

The Plan is tested through a full-scale exercise at least annually, which includes relocation of designated personnel to the alternate site and restoration of Tier 1 systems from backup, and through tabletop exercises at least semi-annually. The communication tree in Section 7 is tested quarterly by an unannounced notification to all employees, and the time to obtain responses is recorded.

### 6.2 Most Recent Tests

The most recent full-scale test was conducted on November 14, 2025. Designated personnel occupied the alternate site, and all Tier 1 systems were restored and available within 3 hours and 10 minutes of the simulated declaration. One deficiency was identified: printers at the alternate site had not been mapped to the Firm's standard image. It was remediated by Pellow Stack IT, LLC by December 5, 2025. The most recent tabletop exercise was conducted on March 19, 2026 and addressed a ransomware scenario affecting the Firm's file servers, including decision-making on restoration from immutable backups and communication with families.

### 6.3 Reporting of Results

The BCP Coordinator prepares a written report of each full-scale and tabletop exercise, including deficiencies and remediation, which is delivered to the Management Committee and the Chief Compliance Officer.

## SECTION 7. COMMUNICATION TREE

### 7.1 Internal Notification

Upon a declaration, the Managing Partner or her delegate notifies the BCP Coordinator, who activates the Firm's mass notification service to send voice and text messages to all employees. The BCP Coordinator notifies the department heads (the Chief Investment Officer, the Chief Financial Officer, the Chief Compliance Officer, the Director of Technology and Information Security and the President of QHTC), each of whom confirms the status of every employee in the department and reports to the BCP Coordinator within two hours. Employees who cannot be reached are contacted at secondary numbers and emergency contacts recorded in the contact list.

### 7.2 Clients

A recorded message describing the disruption and alternative contact methods is placed on the Firm's main telephone line within two hours of declaration. Relationship Leads contact the primary contact of each Family Relationship within one business day of declaration, and immediately for any family with a pending wire, capital call or trade. Families are reminded that the Firm will never change payment instructions by email.

### 7.3 Custodians, Regulators and Counterparties

The Director of Operations notifies Norrell Bay and Corvedale Clay of the declaration and of any change in authorized contacts. The Chief Compliance Officer determines whether notice to the Securities and Exchange Commission or any other regulator is required, and coordinates with QHTC regarding notice to the South Dakota Division of Banking.

## SECTION 8. PANDEMIC AND CYBER INCIDENT RESPONSE

### 8.1 Pandemic and Public Health Events

In a pandemic or other public health event, the Firm will shift to remote operations under Section 3.2, divide trading, operations and bill payment staff into separate teams that do not share office space, and cross-train personnel so that each critical function can be performed by at least two employees. The BCP Coordinator monitors public health guidance and determines, with the Managing Partner, when the office reopens.

### 8.2 Cyber Incident Response

A cyber incident is managed under the incident response procedures in Section 11.6 of the Compliance Manual, with the Director of Technology and Information Security as Incident Response Lead. If the incident impairs operations, the BCP Coordinator may declare a business disruption. Northgale Security Operations, Inc. performs containment and forensic investigation. Vellacott Pryor LLP serves as the Firm's outside breach counsel and advises on privilege, notification obligations and communications. The Chief Financial Officer notifies the Firm's cyber liability insurer through the insurer's incident reporting line. Restoration of affected systems from backup begins only after Northgale confirms that the threat has been contained, so that restored systems are not reinfected.

### 8.3 Fraudulent Disbursement Attempts

If there is reason to believe that an email account or client instruction has been compromised, the Director of Operations asks Norrell Bay and Corvedale Clay to apply heightened review to all disbursements from affected accounts, suspends processing of new third-party payment instructions until they are confirmed by callback, and notifies the Chief Compliance Officer. Any disbursement already sent is reported to the custodian and the receiving bank for recall.

## SECTION 9. PLAN MAINTENANCE

The BCP Coordinator reviews the contact lists quarterly and the Plan as a whole annually, and updates the Plan upon any change in office location, alternate site, system tier or critical vendor. Each employee receives a copy of the Plan's employee procedures and wallet card upon hire and upon each revision.
