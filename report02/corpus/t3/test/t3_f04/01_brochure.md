### 4.2 Advisory Services and Strategies

Wrenholt Pryce provides investment management services exclusively in U.S. dollar denominated fixed income. The Firm manages separately managed accounts ("separate accounts") for public pension plans and insurance companies, and it sponsors and manages one private commingled fund. The Firm offers three strategies:

- **Core Fixed Income.** Investment grade portfolios of U.S. Treasury, agency, agency mortgage-backed, investment grade corporate, taxable municipal and asset-backed securities, managed against a broad investment grade aggregate index chosen by the client. Portfolio duration is generally kept within 0.5 years of the duration of the index.
- **Long Duration Credit.** Portfolios of long maturity investment grade corporate and taxable municipal bonds, together with U.S. Treasury STRIPS and Treasury futures used to manage duration and key rate exposures, for defined benefit pension plans seeking to align the interest rate sensitivity of plan assets with that of plan liabilities. Target duration, generally between 12 and 16 years, is set with the client and its actuary or investment consultant.
- **Insurance Core.** Investment grade portfolios for insurance company general accounts, managed to client objectives for book yield, risk-based capital, NAIC designation mix, asset and liability duration, and the timing of realized gains and losses. Insurance Core portfolios may hold privately placed corporate notes issued in reliance on Section 4(a)(2) of the Securities Act of 1933.

The Firm does not sponsor or participate in wrap fee programs and does not provide financial planning, asset allocation advice across asset classes, or advice to individuals.

### 4.3 Client Guidelines and Restrictions

Each separate account is managed under an investment management agreement ("IMA") and written investment guidelines negotiated with the client. Guidelines typically specify permitted sectors, minimum average and per-issue credit quality, the maximum allocation to below investment grade securities (often zero for public pension clients and up to 5% for insurance clients), issuer and industry concentration limits, duration bands, permitted derivatives and the reference index. Clients may prohibit investment in specific issuers, industries or countries. Several public pension clients are subject to state statutes requiring divestment from issuers named on lists maintained by a state agency; when a client provides such a list, the Firm codes it as a hard restriction in its order management system within five business days of receipt. Guideline limits are measured at the time of purchase unless the guidelines provide otherwise.

## Item 8. Methods of Analysis, Investment Strategies and Risk of Loss

### 8.1 Methods of Analysis

The Firm combines top-down and bottom-up analysis. The Investment Policy Committee, chaired by the Chief Investment Officer, meets at least monthly to set ranges for duration, yield curve positioning and sector allocation based on its view of growth, inflation, monetary policy and relative value. Within those ranges, portfolio managers select securities based on fundamental credit research.

The Firm's credit research team, led by Rafael Denholm, Head of Credit Research, assigns an internal credit rating to every corporate and taxable municipal issuer approved for purchase, based on business risk, leverage and cash flow coverage, liquidity, covenant protection and, for municipal issuers, the revenue pledge and legal structure. Approved issuers are reviewed at least annually and upon material events. Structured and mortgage-backed securities are evaluated with prepayment, default and loss-severity models.

The risk management function, led by the Chief Risk Officer, measures each portfolio's duration, key rate duration, spread duration, tracking error, issuer concentration and liquidity, and runs rate shock and spread widening scenarios. For Long Duration Credit clients, the Firm also measures the portfolio's hedge ratio against the liability cash flows provided by the client or its actuary.

### 8.2 Investment Strategies

The Firm's strategies emphasize sector allocation and issuer selection over large deviations in duration. Long Duration Credit portfolios use Treasury futures and, where guidelines permit, cleared interest rate swaps to adjust duration without selling cash bonds; the notional exposure of these instruments may create economic leverage. Insurance Core portfolios are managed with a buy-and-maintain orientation because realized gains and losses affect the statutory income of insurance clients. The Firm does not engage in securities lending on behalf of clients.

### 8.3 Risk of Loss

Investing in securities involves risk of loss that clients should be prepared to bear. A portfolio managed by the Firm may lose value or underperform its reference index, and the Firm does not guarantee any return or the achievement of any liability-matching objective. The principal risks are summarized below; the list is not exhaustive.

### 8.4 Principal Risks

**Interest rate risk.** Fixed income prices generally fall when interest rates rise. The longer the duration of a portfolio, the more sensitive it is to rate changes; a Long Duration Credit portfolio with a duration of 14 years would be expected to lose approximately 14% of its value if yields rose one percentage point across the curve, before accounting for income and convexity.

**Credit risk.** An issuer may fail to make interest or principal payments when due, or its creditworthiness may deteriorate. Credit rating downgrades can reduce prices and, for insurance clients, increase required statutory capital.

**Spread risk.** Yield spreads over Treasury securities can widen because of economic conditions or investor demand, reducing prices even where issuer fundamentals are unchanged.

**Liquidity risk.** Fixed income markets are dealer markets, and dealer capacity to hold inventory can decline sharply in periods of stress. The Firm may be unable to sell a position at a price close to its carrying value, particularly for long maturity, small issue or privately placed securities.

**Call, prepayment and extension risk.** Callable bonds may be redeemed and mortgages prepaid when rates fall, forcing reinvestment at lower yields; when rates rise, prepayments may slow and extend duration.

**Private placement risk.** Privately placed notes held in Insurance Core portfolios are not registered, trade infrequently and are subject to resale restrictions. Their values are determined by the Firm using the matrix methodology described in its Valuation Policy and may differ from the price that could be realized in a sale.

**Below investment grade risk.** Where guidelines permit, securities rated below investment grade carry a higher risk of default and greater price volatility, and securities downgraded after purchase may be held when the Firm believes a sale would be disadvantageous.

**Derivatives and leverage risk.** Treasury futures and interest rate swaps can produce gains or losses larger than the margin posted, and a hedge may be less effective than expected. Cleared derivatives expose the account to the risk of its clearing member.

**Valuation risk.** Many fixed income securities are valued using evaluated prices from pricing services, dealer quotations or models rather than transaction prices. Values used by the Firm for reporting and fee calculation may differ from the prices at which securities could be sold and from the values reported by a client's custodian.

**Rating agency and NAIC designation risk.** The capital requirements of insurance clients depend on ratings and NAIC designations assigned by third parties, which may change in ways that increase capital charges or require sales.

**Operational and cybersecurity risk.** Failures of systems, vendors or processes, including cyberattacks, could disrupt trading, compromise confidential information or result in losses. The Firm maintains a business continuity plan and an information security program, but no plan can eliminate these risks.

Neither the Firm nor any affiliate receives a commission, markup or other compensation in connection with a cross trade. ### 11.4 Principal Transactions

## Item 12. Brokerage Practices

### 12.1 Selection of Broker-Dealers

The Firm has discretion to select the broker-dealers through which transactions are executed for discretionary accounts. The Firm executes fixed income transactions with dealers on its approved counterparty list, which is maintained by the Best Execution Committee. In selecting a dealer, the Firm considers the net price available, the dealer's willingness to commit capital in the size required, its knowledge of the security, the likelihood of execution, settlement capability and financial condition. For most investment grade transactions, traders solicit competitive quotations from multiple dealers by voice or through electronic request-for-quote platforms; for large or illiquid positions, the Firm may negotiate with a single dealer to limit information leakage.

The Best Execution Committee, chaired by the Chief Operating Officer, meets quarterly to review execution quality, including transaction cost analysis prepared by an independent vendor that compares execution prices with evaluated prices at the time of trade, the use of electronic platforms, dealer concentration and settlement failures.

### 12.2 Research and Other Soft Dollar Benefits

The Firm does not use client brokerage to obtain research or other products or services, and it has no soft dollar arrangements. Fixed income securities are generally traded on a principal basis at net prices, and the Firm pays for research, market data and analytical services from its own resources.

### 12.3 Brokerage for Client Referrals

The Firm does not consider whether a broker-dealer or any third party refers clients or investors to the Firm in selecting broker-dealers.

### 12.4 Directed Brokerage and Diverse Broker-Dealer Programs

The Firm does not generally permit clients to direct brokerage. Several public pension clients encourage the use of minority-owned, women-owned and veteran-owned broker-dealers, and the Firm includes qualified firms of this type on its approved counterparty list. Where a client's guidelines establish a utilization goal, the Firm seeks to meet it only to the extent consistent with best execution; such trades may not be aggregated and may receive less favorable execution.

### 12.5 Aggregation and Allocation

The Firm may aggregate orders for multiple accounts when it believes aggregation is consistent with its duty to seek best execution. Before an aggregated order is placed, the portfolio manager records the intended allocation in the order management system. If an aggregated order is completely filled, it is allocated according to that pre-allocation; if it is partially filled, it is allocated pro rata based on the pre-allocated amounts, subject to minimum denomination and minimum lot size considerations. New issue allotments are allocated pro rata based on indications recorded before the allotment is received. Each account participating in an aggregated order receives the average price for the order. Any departure from the pre-allocation requires the written approval of the Chief Compliance Officer or the Deputy Chief Compliance Officer.

### 12.6 Trade Errors

The Firm seeks to correct trade errors promptly after discovery so that the affected account is placed in the position it would have occupied had the error not occurred. The Firm reimburses the account for any loss resulting from an error for which the Firm is responsible, and any gain remains in the account. Errors and corrections are documented and reviewed by Compliance.
