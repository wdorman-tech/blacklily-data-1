## SECTION 1. INTRODUCTION AND SCOPE

### 1.1 Purpose

This Compliance Manual (the "Manual") sets out the written policies and procedures that Wrenholt Pryce Asset Management LLC (the "Firm") has adopted under Rule 206(4)-7 under the Investment Advisers Act of 1940 (the "Advisers Act") to prevent, detect and correct violations of the Advisers Act and the rules under it. The Code of Ethics in Section 3 and the personal trading, gifts and political contribution procedures in Sections 4 through 6 together constitute the Firm's code of ethics under Rule 204A-1.

### 1.2 Applicability

"Supervised persons" means all members, officers and employees of the Firm, and any temporary worker, contractor or consultant whom the Chief Compliance Officer ("CCO") designates because the person has access to nonpublic information regarding client holdings or transactions or is engaged for more than 30 days. Every supervised person must read the Manual, certify compliance on hire and annually, and report violations as described in Section 3.3.

### 1.3 Related Policies and Amendments

The Manual is supplemented by the Valuation Policy and Procedures, the Business Continuity Plan, the Proxy Voting and Corporate Actions Policy and the Information Security Program. Where a supplemental policy addresses a subject in greater detail, that policy governs. Amendments to the Manual are adopted by the Management Committee on the CCO's recommendation. Between scheduled revisions, the CCO may issue a written compliance memorandum, approved by the Management Committee, that amends a provision of the Manual or of any supplemental policy; the memorandum supersedes the affected provision from its effective date until the provision is revised.

### 1.4 Exceptions

Exceptions to any provision of the Manual must be requested in writing and approved in writing by the CCO, or by the Chief Executive Officer for an exception requested by the CCO. Exceptions are logged and reported to the Management Committee quarterly.

## SECTION 3. CODE OF ETHICS

### 3.1 Standards of Business Conduct

Supervised persons must act with honesty and integrity and in the best interest of clients; place the interests of clients ahead of their own and those of the Firm; comply with the federal securities laws; avoid conflicts of interest or, where a conflict cannot be avoided, disclose it to the CCO; and keep client information confidential.

### 3.2 Access Persons

All employees are designated access persons. The CCO may designate other supervised persons as access persons.

### 3.3 Reporting Violations

Supervised persons must promptly report any actual or suspected violation of the Code or the Manual to the CCO or, if the matter involves the CCO, to the Chief Executive Officer. Reports may also be made anonymously through the Firm's third-party ethics hotline. The Firm prohibits retaliation against anyone who reports in good faith. The CCO records each reported violation, the investigation performed and the outcome in the violations log.

### 3.4 Sanctions

Violations may result in a written warning, reversal of the transaction, disgorgement of profits, a fine, suspension of personal trading privileges, reduction of discretionary bonus or termination of employment. Disgorged amounts are donated to a charity selected by the Firm. The CCO determines sanctions for employees; sanctions for a member of the Management Committee are determined by the Management Committee without the participation of that member.

### 3.5 Acknowledgments

Each supervised person must acknowledge receipt of the Code within 10 days after hire, annually by January 31, and within 10 business days after receiving any amendment.

## SECTION 4. PERSONAL TRADING AND PRE-CLEARANCE

### 4.1 Covered Accounts and Covered Securities

"Covered accounts" include every account in which an access person has a direct or indirect beneficial interest, including accounts of a spouse, domestic partner, minor child or other household member, and every account over which the access person has investment discretion. "Covered securities" include all securities except direct obligations of the U.S. government, bankers' acceptances, bank certificates of deposit, commercial paper, high quality short-term debt instruments including repurchase agreements, money market fund shares and shares of open-end funds not advised by the Firm. Interests in the Commingled Fund are covered securities.

### 4.2 Pre-Clearance

Access persons must obtain pre-clearance through the Firm's personal trading platform, provided by Tarnwell Compliance Software, before any transaction in a covered security, including an exchange-traded fund. Compliance reviews each request against the Restricted List, pending and recent client transactions and the restrictions in Section 4.3. The CCO's own requests are reviewed by the Deputy CCO. A pre-clearance approval is valid through the close of regular trading on the business day following the day on which the approval is granted; an order not executed within that period, including an unfilled limit order, must be pre-cleared again. Transactions in an account managed by an unaffiliated adviser over which the access person has no influence or control are exempt from pre-clearance upon written certification to Compliance.

### 4.3 Prohibited and Restricted Transactions

Access persons may not:

(a) purchase individual corporate bonds, municipal bonds, asset-backed or mortgage-backed securities, or privately placed debt;
(b) trade any security of an issuer on the Restricted List;
(c) trade any equity or equity-linked security of an issuer whose debt securities a client account has bought or sold within three business days before or after the transaction (the "blackout period"), and any profit on a transaction later found to fall within the blackout period is subject to disgorgement;
(d) acquire securities in an initial public offering;
(e) acquire securities in a limited offering, including an interest in the Commingled Fund or any other private fund, without the prior written approval of the CCO; or
(f) sell short, or buy put options on, the securities of any issuer whose securities are held in a client account.

### 4.4 Holding Period

Covered securities, other than exchange-traded funds that track broad market indices, must be held for at least 30 calendar days from the date of acquisition. Holding periods are measured on a last-in, first-out basis. The CCO may grant an exception in writing for hardship or for a sale at a loss. Profits realized on a sale in violation of this Section are subject to disgorgement.

### 4.5 Reporting

Each access person must submit an initial holdings report within 10 days after becoming an access person, with information current as of a date no more than 45 days before the person became an access person; a quarterly transaction report within 30 days after the end of each calendar quarter; and an annual holdings report within 30 days after the end of each calendar year, with information current as of a date no more than 45 days before submission. Broker data feeds received in the Tarnwell platform satisfy these requirements if complete. Covered accounts must be held at broker-dealers that provide data feeds unless the CCO approves otherwise.

### 4.6 Review

Compliance reviews all reports and pre-clearance activity, compares personal transactions with client trading, and documents its review within 30 days after each report is due. The Deputy CCO reviews the CCO's reports and transactions.

## SECTION 5. GIFTS AND ENTERTAINMENT

### 5.1 General Principles

Supervised persons may not give or accept any gift, entertainment or other benefit that could reasonably be expected to influence a business decision or create an appearance of impropriety. Cash and cash equivalents, including gift cards, may never be given or accepted. All gifts and entertainment given or received must be recorded in the gifts and entertainment log in the Tarnwell platform within 10 business days.

### 5.2 Gifts Received

Supervised persons may not accept gifts from any single business contact, including any broker-dealer, issuer, vendor or consultant, with an aggregate value exceeding $250 per calendar year. A gift exceeding that limit must be declined or returned or, with the CCO's approval, donated to charity. Promotional items of nominal value bearing a company logo do not count toward the limit but must be logged.

### 5.3 Entertainment

Business entertainment, meaning a meal or event attended by both the supervised person and a representative of the host, is permitted if it is reasonable in value and frequency and has a legitimate business purpose. Entertainment with a value exceeding $500 per person per event requires the prior approval of the CCO. Supervised persons may not accept travel or lodging paid for by a broker-dealer, issuer or vendor; the Firm pays for employee travel to dealer-sponsored conferences and issuer meetings. Traders may not accept entertainment from any single broker-dealer more than four times per calendar year.

### 5.4 Gifts and Entertainment Given

No gift or entertainment may be provided to any trustee, official or employee of a public pension plan client or prospect, or to any other government official, unless the CCO has approved it in advance after confirming that it is permitted under the ethics laws that apply to the recipient. Many state and local ethics codes prohibit public officials from accepting anything of value from an investment manager. Gifts to representatives of insurance company clients, other non-governmental clients, prospects and consultants may not exceed $200 per recipient per calendar year.

## SECTION 6. POLITICAL CONTRIBUTIONS

### 6.1 Scope

Rule 206(4)-5 under the Advisers Act (the "pay-to-play rule") prohibits an adviser from receiving compensation for advisory services to a government entity for two years after the adviser or a covered associate makes a contribution to an official of that government entity, subject to limited exceptions. Because the Firm's clients include public pension plans and any employee may become a covered associate through a change in responsibilities, the Firm treats every employee as a covered associate for purposes of this Section.

### 6.2 Pre-Clearance and Limits

Every employee must obtain the CCO's written pre-clearance before making any political contribution, regardless of amount, to an incumbent, candidate or successful candidate for state or local office (including a candidate for federal office who currently holds a state or local office), a state or local political party, or a political action committee. The CCO will approve a contribution to an official or candidate only if it does not exceed $350 per election for an official or candidate for whom the employee is entitled to vote, or $150 per election for any other official or candidate. Primary and general elections are separate elections. A contribution by a spouse or household member made at the employee's direction is treated as a contribution by the employee.

### 6.3 Pre-Employment Review

Each candidate for employment must disclose all political contributions made during the two years before the anticipated start date, and the CCO reviews the disclosure before an offer becomes final.

### 6.4 Solicitation, Coordination and Placement Agents

Employees may not solicit or coordinate contributions to an official of a government entity, or payments to a political party of a state or locality, where the Firm provides or seeks to provide advisory services. The Firm does not compensate placement agents or other third parties to solicit government entities.

### 6.5 Records

The CCO maintains a list of government entity clients and government entity investors in the Commingled Fund, records of all contributions pre-cleared and made, and the list of covered associates, as required by Rule 204-2.

## SECTION 7. INSIDER TRADING AND MATERIAL NONPUBLIC INFORMATION

### 7.1 Policy

Supervised persons may not trade, for themselves or for any client, while in possession of material nonpublic information ("MNPI"), and may not communicate MNPI to others in violation of law. Information is material if a reasonable investor would consider it important to an investment decision, and nonpublic until it has been disseminated in a manner that makes it available to investors generally. The prohibition applies to debt securities and credit derivatives as well as equity securities.

### 7.2 Sources of MNPI in Fixed Income

Common sources of MNPI at the Firm include due diligence materials for private placements provided under confidentiality agreements; noteholder information provided in connection with amendments, consent solicitations or restructurings; participation in ad hoc creditor committees; and wall-crossing by dealers marketing a new issue before its public announcement.

### 7.3 Wall-Crossing and Private-Side Information

No supervised person may agree to be wall-crossed or accept private-side information without first obtaining the approval of the CCO or the Deputy CCO. When approval is given, Compliance places the issuer on the Restricted List before the information is received and records the persons who received it. The issuer remains on the Restricted List until the CCO determines that the information has become public or is no longer material.

### 7.4 Restricted List and Watch List

Compliance maintains the Restricted List, which is coded in the order management system to block client trades and in the Tarnwell platform to block personal trades. Compliance also maintains a confidential Watch List of issuers about which the Firm may hold sensitive information, which it uses to monitor trading without restricting it.

### 7.5 Expert Networks

The Firm does not engage expert network firms.

## SECTION 8. MARKETING REVIEW

### 8.1 Scope

The Firm treats as advertisements subject to Rule 206(4)-1 (the "Marketing Rule") its presentations and pitch books, requests for proposal responses, submissions to investment consultant databases, market commentary distributed to prospects, and website content.

### 8.2 Review and Approval

All marketing materials must be reviewed and approved in writing by the CCO or the Deputy CCO before first use. Before submission to Compliance, the portfolio analytics team verifies every performance figure against the Firm's performance system of record. One-on-one communications with existing clients are generally not advertisements but remain subject to the antifraud provisions of the Advisers Act.

### 8.3 Performance Presentation

Net performance must be presented with at least equal prominence to gross performance, for one, five and ten year periods or since inception if shorter. Extracted performance must be accompanied by, or offered with, the performance of the total portfolio. Hypothetical performance, including projected liability hedge outcomes, may be provided only to institutional recipients with the resources to evaluate it, together with the assumptions and limitations on which it rests.

### 8.4 Testimonials, Endorsements and Third-Party Ratings

The Firm does not use testimonials or endorsements. A third-party rating or ranking may be used only with the disclosures required by the Marketing Rule and the CCO's approval.

## SECTION 9. TRADE ALLOCATION AND AGGREGATION

### 9.1 Aggregation

Portfolio managers may aggregate orders for multiple accounts when aggregation is consistent with the duty to seek best execution and with each account's guidelines. Before an aggregated order is placed, the portfolio manager enters the pre-allocation in the order management system provided by Pelloran Systems. Orders for non-discretionary accounts and trades placed to meet a client's broker utilization goal are not aggregated.

### 9.2 Allocation of Partial Fills

If an aggregated order is partially filled, the executed amount is allocated pro rata among participating accounts based on their pre-allocated amounts. Where a pro rata allocation would give an account less than $250,000 par value, or less than the security's minimum denomination, the trader may reallocate that amount pro rata among the other participating accounts. Each participating account receives the average price.

### 9.3 New Issues

Indications of interest for a new issue must be entered by account in the order management system before the order book closes. The allotment received is allocated pro rata to those indications, and any adjustment for guideline constraints or cash availability is documented by the portfolio manager.

### 9.4 Departures from Pre-Allocation

Any allocation that departs from the pre-allocation requires a written explanation by the portfolio manager and the approval of the CCO or the Deputy CCO before the end of the trade date.

### 9.5 Review

Compliance reviews the allocation of all aggregated orders monthly and, with the Chief Risk Officer, reviews performance dispersion among accounts in each strategy quarterly.

## SECTION 10. BEST EXECUTION REVIEW

### 10.1 Standard

The Firm seeks to execute each transaction on the most favorable terms reasonably available under the circumstances. For fixed income securities traded with dealers as principal, the net price is the principal factor, considered together with size, liquidity, market conditions, settlement capability and the counterparty's financial condition.

### 10.2 Best Execution Committee

The Best Execution Committee is chaired by the Chief Operating Officer, Dmitri Halloway. Its other members are the Head of Trading, Marcus Ebbing; the Chief Risk Officer, Yuki Ashbury; the Director of Portfolio Operations, Leopold Sarkis; and the CCO. A majority of members constitutes a quorum.

### 10.3 Meetings and Review Data

The Committee meets quarterly. At each meeting it reviews transaction cost analysis prepared by Orrinmoor Analytics, which compares each execution with the evaluated price supplied by the primary pricing service at the time of trade and with composite dealer quotations; the number of competitive quotations obtained, against a target of at least three quotations for investment grade trades over $1 million par when market conditions permit; the share of volume executed with each dealer and through each electronic platform; settlement failures; and use of minority-owned, women-owned and veteran-owned broker-dealers against client goals. Minutes record the Committee's conclusions and any follow-up.

### 10.4 Approved Counterparty List

A new dealer may be added to the approved counterparty list only with the Committee's approval after review of its financial condition and settlement capability. The list is reviewed annually, and the Chief Risk Officer may suspend a counterparty immediately for credit reasons.

## SECTION 11. CYBERSECURITY AND INFORMATION SECURITY

### 11.1 Governance

The Chief Technology Officer, Samuel Treloar, serves as the Firm's Information Security Officer ("ISO") and is responsible for the Information Security Program. The ISO conducts an annual cybersecurity risk assessment and reports on the program to the Management Committee quarterly.

### 11.2 Technical Controls

The Firm requires multi-factor authentication for all remote and privileged access; encrypts data at rest and in transit; grants access on a least-privilege basis and reviews entitlements quarterly; and deploys endpoint detection and response software on all Firm devices. Grisby Lane Security provides managed security services, including monitoring by its security operations center 24 hours a day. Vulnerability scans are performed monthly. An external penetration test is performed annually by a qualified independent firm that is not otherwise engaged to provide managed security services to the Firm, and the ISO tracks each finding to remediation.

### 11.3 Vendor Oversight

Vendors with access to Firm systems or confidential information are reviewed before engagement and annually thereafter, including review of available SOC 1 or SOC 2 reports, security questionnaires and contractual breach notification terms.

### 11.4 Incident Reporting and Response

Any supervised person who suspects an information security incident must report it to the ISO and the CCO immediately, and in any event within one hour after discovery. The ISO leads the response under Section 9 of the Business Continuity Plan, and notifications to affected individuals are made under Section 12.4.

### 11.5 Cyber Insurance

The Firm maintains a cyber liability insurance policy. The Chief Financial Officer reviews the adequacy of coverage annually with the ISO and the Firm's insurance broker, and the ISO ensures that the notice requirements of the policy are reflected in the incident response plan.

### 11.6 Employee Awareness

Phishing simulations are conducted at least quarterly. An employee who fails two simulations within 12 months must complete remedial training.

## SECTION 12. PRIVACY

### 12.1 Scope

The Firm's clients are institutions, but the Firm holds nonpublic personal information about natural persons who invest in the Commingled Fund, including employees, and about its own employees. This Section applies Regulation S-P to customer information and applies the same safeguards to other personal information the Firm holds, including any plan participant data received from pension clients for liability modeling.

### 12.2 Privacy Notice

The Firm delivers its privacy notice to each natural person investor in the Commingled Fund at subscription. Because the Firm shares nonpublic personal information only as permitted by the exceptions in Regulation S-P and has not changed its policies, it relies on the exception from delivering an annual notice.

### 12.3 Safeguards and Disposal

Access to customer information is limited to personnel who need it. Paper records are destroyed by cross-cut shredding, and electronic media are destroyed by a certified vendor that provides a certificate of destruction.

### 12.4 Incident Response and Notification

The Firm's incident response program includes procedures to assess, contain and control incidents involving unauthorized access to or use of customer information. If sensitive customer information was, or is reasonably likely to have been, accessed or used without authorization, the Firm notifies each affected individual as soon as practicable, but not later than 30 days after becoming aware of the incident, unless it determines after reasonable investigation that the information has not been and is not reasonably likely to be used in a manner that would result in substantial harm or inconvenience. Service providers must notify the Firm within 72 hours after becoming aware of a breach affecting the Firm's customer information.

## SECTION 13. RECORDKEEPING

### 13.1 Required Records

The Firm maintains the books and records required by Rule 204-2, including trade blotters and order memoranda, IMAs and investment guidelines, written communications relating to recommendations and transactions, marketing materials and the records supporting any performance they contain, Code of Ethics records, political contribution records and the records of the CCO's reviews.

### 13.2 Retention Period

The Firm retains all required books and records for not less than seven years from the end of the fiscal year during which the last entry was made on the record, the first two years in an appropriate office of the Firm. This period exceeds the five-year period required by Rule 204-2. Organizational documents are retained for the life of the Firm and for three years after its termination.

### 13.3 Electronic Storage

Email and approved electronic messages are captured by Kestle Archive Services in a non-rewriteable, indexed archive with a duplicate copy held separately. Other electronic records are stored on Firm systems that are backed up as described in the Business Continuity Plan.

### 13.4 Production and Legal Holds

Records must be capable of prompt production to the SEC staff on request, and the CCO coordinates all such requests. The CCO, with outside counsel where appropriate, may suspend the destruction of records relevant to actual or anticipated litigation or inquiries.

## SECTION 14. ELECTRONIC COMMUNICATIONS

### 14.1 Approved Channels

Business communications may be conducted only through Firm email; the chat functions of approved electronic trading platforms and market data terminals whose messages are captured by Kestle Archive Services; and the Firm's approved mobile messaging application, which is installed on Firm-managed mobile devices and captured by Kestle Archive Services.

### 14.2 Prohibited Channels

Supervised persons may not use personal email, SMS or iMessage text messaging, WhatsApp, Signal, WeChat, social media direct messaging or any other unapproved channel for business communications. A supervised person who receives a business communication through an unapproved channel must not respond substantively through that channel, must forward the message to the Firm's archive within one business day, and must notify Compliance.

### 14.3 Surveillance

Compliance reviews communications weekly, including all messages of traders and portfolio managers flagged by the lexicon and a random sample of other messages, and documents each review in the Kestle archive.

### 14.4 Social Media and Public Statements

Supervised persons may not discuss clients, holdings or Firm business on social media. Professional networking profiles may state only the employee's title and general responsibilities. Media inquiries must be referred to the Chief Executive Officer and the CCO.

## SECTION 15. ANNUAL REVIEW

### 15.1 Scope and Timing

The CCO conducts an annual review of the adequacy of the Firm's policies and procedures and the effectiveness of their implementation, as required by Rule 206(4)-7. Each review covers the preceding calendar year and considers compliance matters that arose, changes in the Firm's business and changes in applicable law. The CCO delivers a written report on the review to the Management Committee by March 31.

### 15.2 Independent Assessment

Every second year, Hessendale Compliance Advisors, an outside compliance consulting firm, performs an independent assessment of the compliance program as part of the annual review, including sample reviews of personal trading, allocation, marketing and pay-to-play records. The most recent independent assessment was performed as part of the review of calendar year 2024 and was completed in the first quarter of 2025.

### 15.3 Contents of the Annual Report

The annual report summarizes material compliance matters; Code of Ethics violations and the sanctions imposed; the results of the CCO's reviews of each area of this Manual; recommended changes; and the status of prior remediation items.

### 15.4 Interim Reviews

The CCO performs an interim review when the Firm adds a strategy or service, when there is a material change in its business or systems, after a material violation, and when applicable law changes.

## SECTION 16. TRAINING

### 16.1 New Hire Training

Each new employee must complete compliance training covering the Code of Ethics, this Manual, MNPI, electronic communications and information security within 30 days after the start date.

### 16.2 Annual Training

All supervised persons must attend the Firm's annual compliance training, held in the fourth quarter of each year. A supervised person who misses the session must complete a recorded session by January 31 of the following year.

### 16.3 Role-Specific Training

Traders receive annual training on best execution and allocation; portfolio managers and credit analysts on MNPI and wall-crossing; and client service and marketing personnel on the Marketing Rule and the pay-to-play rule.

### 16.4 Records

Training materials, attendance records and certifications are retained under Section 13.
