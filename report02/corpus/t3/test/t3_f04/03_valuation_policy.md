## SECTION 1. PURPOSE AND SCOPE

### 1.1 Purpose

This Policy establishes the procedures by which Wrenholt Pryce Asset Management LLC (the "Firm") determines the fair value of investments held in the accounts it manages. Values determined under this Policy are used to calculate the net asset value of Wrenholt Pryce Long Duration Credit Fund, L.P. (the "Commingled Fund") and to prepare its financial statements, to calculate management fees, and to prepare client and performance reports. Fair value is the price that would be received to sell an asset in an orderly transaction between market participants at the measurement date, consistent with ASC Topic 820.

### 1.2 Scope

This Policy applies to every position held in the Commingled Fund and in each separate account. For separate accounts, the client's custodian maintains the official book of record. Portfolio Operations reconciles the Firm's values to custodian values monthly and investigates any account-level difference greater than 0.10% of account market value.

### 1.3 Valuation Time

Positions are valued as of 4:00 p.m. Eastern time on each business day. Month-end values are finalized by Portfolio Operations by the fifth business day after month end and are subject to review by the Valuation Committee at its next meeting.

## SECTION 2. VALUATION COMMITTEE

### 2.1 Composition

The voting members of the Valuation Committee (the "Committee") are the Chief Financial Officer, Ines Morrow-Tate, who chairs the Committee; the Chief Risk Officer, Yuki Ashbury; and the Director of Portfolio Operations, Leopold Sarkis. The Chief Compliance Officer, Priya Castellane, and the Head of Credit Research, Rafael Denholm, attend as non-voting members. Portfolio managers and traders are not members; they may be invited to provide market information but leave the meeting before any vote.

### 2.2 Quorum and Voting

A quorum consists of two voting members, at least one of whom must be the Chief Financial Officer or the Chief Risk Officer. Decisions require the vote of a majority of the voting members present. Members may participate by video or telephone. For a time-sensitive matter, the Committee may act by unanimous written consent of the voting members, which is ratified at the next meeting.

### 2.3 Meetings

The Committee meets monthly, within 10 business days after each month end, and at any other time at the request of any member. Portfolio Operations prepares the minutes, which the Chief Compliance Officer reviews.

### 2.4 Responsibilities

The Committee approves pricing sources and valuation methodologies; reviews Level 3 positions, price challenge logs, override reports, stale price reports and back-testing results; approves fair value determinations; and reports quarterly to the Management Committee on valuation matters, including any material change in methodology.

### 2.5 Conflicts of Interest

Because management fees and reported performance depend on the values determined under this Policy, the Firm has an incentive to overstate values. The Committee addresses this conflict by vesting pricing responsibility in Portfolio Operations, which reports to the Chief Financial Officer and not to the Chief Investment Officer, by excluding portfolio managers and traders from its votes, and by the independent review described in Section 8. A voting member who has a personal interest in a matter before the Committee must disclose it and abstain.

## SECTION 3. PRICING HIERARCHY AND SOURCES

### 3.1 Hierarchy

Each position is valued using the first available source in the following order: (1) for exchange-traded instruments, the official settlement or closing price on the primary exchange; (2) the evaluated price supplied by the primary pricing service; (3) the evaluated price supplied by the secondary pricing service, where the primary service does not cover the security or its price has been rejected under Section 5; (4) broker quotations obtained under Section 3.4; and (5) a fair value determined by the Committee under Section 4.

### 3.2 Primary Pricing Service

Lanthorn Evaluated Pricing, Inc. is the primary pricing service for all fixed income securities held by the Firm's clients, including U.S. Treasury and agency securities, agency mortgage-backed pools and to-be-announced contracts, corporate and taxable municipal bonds, and asset-backed and commercial mortgage-backed securities. Long positions are valued at the service's bid-side evaluation as of 4:00 p.m. Eastern time.

### 3.3 Secondary Pricing Service

Brayford Pricing Data LLC is the secondary pricing service. Its evaluations are used for the daily tolerance comparison in Section 5.1 and as the pricing source for any security that Lanthorn Evaluated Pricing does not cover or whose Lanthorn price has been rejected.

### 3.4 Broker Quotations

When neither pricing service provides a reliable price, Portfolio Operations, and not the trading desk, obtains bid-side quotations from dealers. At least two independent dealer quotations are required. Where two are obtained, the position is valued at their average; where three or more are obtained, at their median. A price based on a single quotation is a Level 3 valuation and requires the approval of the Committee.

### 3.5 Instrument-Specific Sources

Treasury futures are valued at the exchange settlement price. Cleared interest rate swaps are valued at the end-of-day settlement valuation published by the clearing house. Money market fund shares are valued at their reported net asset value. Privately placed notes are valued under Section 4.2.

### 3.6 New Issues

A newly issued security is valued at its offering price until the primary pricing service begins coverage. If coverage has not begun by the second business day after settlement, Portfolio Operations obtains broker quotations under Section 3.4 or requests coverage from the secondary pricing service.

## SECTION 4. LEVEL 3 METHODOLOGY

### 4.1 Classification

At each month end, Portfolio Operations classifies each position in the fair value hierarchy. Level 1 includes exchange-traded futures and on-the-run Treasury securities; Level 2 includes securities valued at evaluated prices derived from observable inputs; and Level 3 includes positions valued using significant unobservable inputs, including single broker quotations, the private placement matrix and distressed recovery models. Transfers between levels are reported to the Committee.

### 4.2 Privately Placed Notes

Privately placed notes are valued by a matrix method. The Firm determines a spread over the interpolated U.S. Treasury curve equal to the spread of comparable public securities of the same issuer or, where none exist, of comparable issuers with a similar internal rating, sector and maturity, plus an illiquidity premium, plus adjustments for structural features such as financial covenants, make-whole provisions, security and subordination. The note's scheduled cash flows are discounted at the resulting yield. The illiquidity premium is set by the Committee quarterly and currently ranges from 15 to 40 basis points depending on issue size and structure.

### 4.3 Distressed and Defaulted Securities

Securities of an issuer that is in default, or that is assigned the lowest internal rating categories, are valued using a recovery analysis based on the estimated enterprise value available to each class of creditors or on recovery rates observed in comparable restructurings. The recovery value is compared with any available dealer quotations, and differences are explained in the valuation memorandum.

### 4.4 Documentation and Approval

Each Level 3 valuation is supported by a valuation memorandum that records the inputs, their sources and the rationale for each judgment. Level 3 valuations are approved by the Committee at its monthly meeting. For the Commingled Fund's financial statements, Portfolio Operations prepares the quantitative disclosure of significant unobservable inputs.

## SECTION 5. PRICE CHALLENGES AND OVERRIDES

### 5.1 Tolerance Checks

Each business day, Portfolio Operations compares the primary and secondary evaluations for every security. A difference of more than 1.0 point (1% of par) for an investment grade security, or more than 2.0 points for a below investment grade or structured security, is investigated on the same business day. Portfolio Operations also investigates any day-over-day price change of more than 2.0 points for an investment grade security, or 5.0 points for any other security, that is not explained by a market event.

### 5.2 Price Challenges

When Portfolio Operations concludes that a vendor price does not reflect fair value, based on evidence such as recent reported trades, executable dealer quotations or new issue pricing, it submits a formal challenge through the vendor's challenge process within one business day. Each challenge is recorded in the challenge log. If the vendor does not respond within three business days, or declines to change its price, the matter is escalated to the Chief Financial Officer.

### 5.3 Overrides

A price override, meaning the use of a price other than the price indicated by the hierarchy in Section 3.1, must be approved in writing by the Chief Financial Officer before it is applied. Portfolio Operations prepares each override request with the supporting evidence. Overrides are reported to the Committee at its next meeting and lapse at that meeting unless the Committee re-approves them.

### 5.4 Role of Portfolio Managers and Traders

Portfolio managers and traders may provide market information to Portfolio Operations and may ask it to challenge a price, but they may not communicate with pricing vendors about the valuation of positions they manage, determine a price, or approve an override.

## SECTION 6. STALE PRICE MONITORING

### 6.1 Stale Price Threshold

Portfolio Operations runs a stale price report each business day. A security whose price from its assigned source has not changed for five consecutive business days is flagged as stale. For each flagged security, Portfolio Operations obtains dealer quotations or other market evidence to confirm or replace the price and records the result.

### 6.2 Escalation

A security whose price remains unchanged for 20 consecutive business days is referred to the Committee, which determines whether the price should be replaced by a fair value determination. The Committee receives a monthly summary of all stale price flags and their resolution.

## SECTION 7. BACK-TESTING

### 7.1 Procedure

Each month, Portfolio Operations compares the valuation of each security sold during the month, as of the business day before the sale, with the actual sale price. Differences are summarized by sector, pricing source and fair value level. Any difference greater than 2.0 points for an investment grade security, or 3.0 points for any other security, is investigated and explained.

### 7.2 Reporting

Back-testing results are reported to the Committee quarterly. A persistent bias in the prices supplied by a source is referred for review of that source and is considered in the annual pricing vendor due diligence under Section 8.3.

## SECTION 8. INDEPENDENT REVIEW

### 8.1 Independent Valuation Firm

Selwick Harrow Valuation Advisors LLC performs an independent review of Level 3 positions each quarter. The review covers every Level 3 position held by the Commingled Fund and every Level 3 position in a separate account with a market value above $10 million. Selwick Harrow provides a range of values for each position reviewed; where the Firm's value falls outside the range, the Committee documents its conclusion and the reasons for it.

### 8.2 Annual Audit

The Commingled Fund's independent auditor, Marrick Tolland LLP, audits the Fund's valuations as part of its annual audit. Portfolio Operations provides the auditor with the valuation memoranda, challenge logs and Committee minutes it requests.

### 8.3 Pricing Vendor Due Diligence

The Committee conducts due diligence on each pricing service at least annually, including a review of the vendor's methodology documentation, coverage, responsiveness to challenges, back-testing results and SOC 1 report.

### 8.4 Policy Review

The Committee and the Chief Compliance Officer review this Policy at least annually. Changes are approved by the Management Committee.
