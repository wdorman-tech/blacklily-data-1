## Dividend Growth Equity

The strategy holds 35 to 45 U.S. companies that have raised their dividends in at least seven of the past ten years, with no position above 5% of an account at purchase. Minimum account: $500,000. Composite inception: January 1, 2006.

For the ten years ended December 31, 2025, the composite returned 11.84% annualized gross of fees and 10.72% annualized net of fees.

What to weigh: stocks can lose value quickly and stay down for long periods. The composite's worst calendar year was 2008, when it returned -31.62% gross and -32.30% net of fees. A dividend focus can trail the broader market when lower-yielding growth companies lead, can be concentrated in a few sectors, and offers no protection against a company cutting or suspending its dividend.

## Intermediate Tax-Exempt

The strategy holds investment-grade municipal bonds with an average maturity of four to seven years, laddered across issuers and maturities, and favors Virginia issuers for Virginia residents. Minimum account: $1,000,000. Composite inception: April 1, 2009.

Since its April 1, 2009 inception through December 31, 2025, the Intermediate Tax-Exempt Composite returned 3.28% annualized gross of fees and 2.76% annualized net of fees.

What to weigh: bond prices fall when interest rates rise, and the composite returned -5.84% gross and -6.31% net of fees in 2022. Issuers can be downgraded or fail to pay, some bonds can be called before maturity, and municipal bonds can be hard to sell quickly at a fair price. Interest that is exempt from federal income tax may still be subject to state income tax or the alternative minimum tax, and tax laws can change.

## Core Balanced

The strategy holds 55% to 70% in stocks and 30% to 45% in bonds, drawing on the same research as our other two strategies, and rebalances when either range is breached. Minimum account: $250,000. Composite inception: July 1, 2018.

Over the one year ended December 31, 2025, the Core Balanced Composite returned 11.26% gross and 10.31% net of fees; over five years, 8.14% gross and 7.22% net annualized; and since its July 1, 2018 inception, 8.02% gross and 7.10% net annualized. Because the composite has existed for less than ten years, the since-inception period is shown in place of a ten-year period.

What to weigh: stocks and bonds can fall at the same time. In 2022 the composite returned -12.48% gross and -13.22% net of fees. A balanced account will generally trail an all-stock account in strong equity markets.

## How These Figures Are Calculated

- Accounts enter a composite at the start of their first full month under management and leave after their last full month; closed accounts remain in each composite's history.
- Returns are time-weighted, stated in U.S. dollars, include the reinvestment of dividends and interest, and are after transaction costs.
- Gross returns do not reflect the deduction of advisory fees. Net returns reflect the deduction, applied monthly, of the highest annual fee in each strategy's schedule: 1.00% for Dividend Growth Equity, 0.50% for Intermediate Tax-Exempt and 0.85% for Core Balanced. Many clients pay less. Net returns do not reflect custodial fees that clients pay directly to their custodian. Our fee schedules appear in Part 2A of our Form ADV.
- Returns do not reflect taxes, and individual account results differ from composite results.
- Past performance does not guarantee future results. All investments can lose value, including the loss of principal.
- Calendar-year returns since inception and a list of composite descriptions are available on request.
