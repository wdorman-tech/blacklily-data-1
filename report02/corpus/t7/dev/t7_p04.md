## Post 1: Monday, March 2, 2026

A client couple let me share their note from last week: "Dana took a shoebox of statements from four different firms and turned it into one plan we finally understand. Hiring her team was the best financial decision we have made in retirement."

What they were describing is consolidation, and it is some of the least glamorous work we do. Four custodians meant four sets of tax forms, four sets of beneficiary designations nobody had reviewed since 2011, and no single view of how much risk the household was actually carrying.

Consolidating is not right for everyone. Moving an account can trigger taxable gains, surrender charges on older annuity contracts, or the loss of low-cost share classes available only inside a former employer's plan. We price those costs before we recommend moving anything, and sometimes the right answer is to leave an account where it is.

## Post 2: Monday, March 9, 2026

2025 in the rearview.

We do not chase headlines, but we will hold a stock for years when the research supports it. Three of the positions we added to client portfolios in early 2025 finished the year up 112%, 74% and 58%: Quillon Grid Systems, Tamsworth Biologics and Arnbeck Freight.

Conviction is only half of it. Sizing is the other half: no single stock is more than 5% of a client portfolio at purchase, because being right on a few names only helps if being wrong on others cannot derail a plan. Individual stocks can and do lose value quickly, and we trim when a position grows past our limits.

## Post 3: Monday, March 16, 2026

Tax season reminder: what you keep matters as much as what you earn.

Our tax-loss harvesting program adds at least 1.5% a year to every client's after-tax return, in up markets and down.

How it works: when a holding in a taxable account falls below its purchase price, we can sell it, use the realized loss to offset capital gains (and up to $3,000 a year of ordinary income), and buy a similar but not substantially identical investment so the account stays invested.

A few things worth knowing. Harvesting applies only to taxable accounts, not IRAs or 401(k)s. It generally defers tax rather than eliminating it, because the replacement holding carries a lower cost basis. And the wash-sale rule disallows the loss if you buy the same or a substantially identical security within 30 days before or after the sale, including in your spouse's accounts or your IRA.

Talk with your CPA before you file; we coordinate with yours directly.
