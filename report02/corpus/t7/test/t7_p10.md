### Post 1 (October 6): Why we rebalance on thresholds, not the calendar

Most of our client portfolios are rebalanced when an asset class drifts more than 5 percentage points from its target, rather than on a fixed date every quarter.

The benefit is that we trade when the mix has actually moved, which can mean fewer trades in quiet years and a portfolio that stays closer to the risk level you signed up for. It has costs too. In volatile years it can mean more trades and more realized capital gains in taxable accounts. It always means selling some of what has been working. And in a long, one-directional rally, a portfolio that is never rebalanced can end up ahead.

We think the trade-off is worth it for most of the families we work with, but it is a trade-off, and we set the bands account by account.

### Post 2 (October 13): Three ideas that worked

Every January our research team names the handful of companies it has the most conviction in for the individual-stock sleeve of client portfolios.

### Post 4 (October 27): Retiring into a down market

The risk that keeps pre-retirees up at night is sequence risk: a bear market in the first few years of retirement, when every withdrawal locks in a loss.

Our answer is a spending reserve. For retired clients, we hold roughly three years of planned withdrawals in cash, Treasury bills and short-term Treasury notes, and we refill the reserve from the growth portfolio after years when markets have risen.

With three years of spending set aside, a market decline will never force a Fairlop Wendt client to sell stocks at a loss to pay their bills.

The reserve has a cost. Money held in cash and short-term Treasuries will usually earn less than a diversified stock portfolio over long periods, so a larger reserve means a lower expected long-term return.

Even so, when we ran our reserve rules through every rolling 25-year retirement since 1928, a client with a three-year reserve finished with more money than one without it in 64 of those 74 periods.

If you are within five years of retirement, we are glad to walk through how a reserve would fit your own plan.
