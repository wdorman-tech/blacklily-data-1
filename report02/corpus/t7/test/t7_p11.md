## Episode summary

Chief Investment Officer Hal Cotesworth and Director of Client Planning Ines Farrant look back at the twelve months ended June 30, 2026 for the Tollemarsh Dividend Growth strategy, the separately managed account approach that most of our clients hold as their core U.S. equity allocation. They talk through what drove results, where the discipline cost us, and how the investment committee is positioned for the second half of the year. Independent economist Dr. Rosalind Ebbets, who writes the Cold Ledge Macro newsletter, joins later in the episode to discuss tariffs, wages and the rate outlook for households that live on portfolio income.

## How the strategy works (from the 02:45 and 13:10 segments)

- The Dividend Growth strategy holds 35 to 45 U.S. companies that have raised their dividend in at least eight of the past ten years and whose payout ratio is below 75% at purchase.
- No position exceeds 5% of an account at the time it is bought, and no sector exceeds 25%.
- A holding is reviewed for sale when the company cuts its dividend, when its payout ratio stays above 75% for two consecutive quarters, or when its price exceeds the investment committee's estimate of fair value by more than 20%.
- Annual turnover has typically run between 15% and 25%. Lower turnover generally means fewer realized gains in a given year, and so less tax drag in taxable accounts, but it also means the portfolio can be slow to add companies whose earnings are growing quickly but that pay little or no dividend.
