## Post 2 (Tuesday, August 11, 2026)

A lot of clients have asked what two more rate cuts would mean for them. My honest answer: it depends on where the cash is sitting. Money market yields tend to follow the Fed within weeks. CDs and Treasuries lock in today's rate for their term. Bond prices generally rise when rates fall, but by less than many people expect at shorter maturities.

We are not trying to guess the Fed's next move. We are making sure each client's cash reserve covers the next 12 to 24 months of planned spending, and that the rest is invested for the plan rather than the headline.

## Post 4 (Tuesday, August 25, 2026)

For clients who need their portfolio to produce income, Balanced Income is our workhorse. Over the three years ended June 30, 2026, our Balanced Income composite, launched in 2012, returned 7.9% a year net of fees and 8.6% a year gross of fees.

The composite holds roughly 55% investment-grade bonds and 45% dividend-paying stocks. It is built to generate income, and it will lose value when stocks and bonds fall together, as they did in 2022. It also gives up some growth compared with an all-equity portfolio, which matters for clients with long horizons.

## Post 5 (Tuesday, September 1, 2026)

The question I hear most from clients in their 60s: "How do I stay invested without losing sleep?"

Our Buffered Equity sleeve lets clients keep participating in stock market gains while a 15% buffer absorbs the first leg of any decline, so they can stay invested through rough patches without the sleepless nights.

For most clients who use it, the sleeve sits alongside their core stock and bond holdings, and we size it from the financial plan, not from a market call. If you are wondering whether it belongs in your mix, let's talk.

## Post 6 (Tuesday, September 8, 2026)

Why start early? A hypothetical illustration. A 35-year-old who invests $1,000 at the end of every month for 30 years and earns an assumed 6% a year, compounded monthly, would have about $1,004,515 at 65, before taxes and fees. At an assumed 4%, the same savings would grow to about $694,049.

The 6% and 4% rates are assumptions chosen to show how compounding works. They are not projections, they are not the returns of any Merridew Greaves strategy or client account, and no actual account achieved these results. Real returns vary from year to year and can be negative, and the illustration ignores inflation, taxes, fees and the chance that contributions stop. With steady contributions, a portfolio that averages 6% can still end with far less if its worst years come near the end.
