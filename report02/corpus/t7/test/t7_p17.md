## Key Takeaways

- Interval funds give individual investors a simple way into private credit: higher yields than most public bond funds, steady quarterly distributions and far less of the day-to-day price movement you see in stocks.
- Withdrawal order matters. Drawing on taxable accounts first is a common starting point, but partial Roth conversions in lower-income years can reduce lifetime taxes for some households. Conversions raise taxable income in the year they are made and can increase Medicare premiums two years later, so the math has to be run household by household.
- Under current law, required minimum distributions generally begin at age 73 for people born from 1951 through 1959 and at age 75 for people born in 1960 or later. Missing one triggers an excise tax on the shortfall.
- A Treasury ladder is straightforward to build yourself. The harder decisions are how many years of spending it should cover and what the rest of the portfolio does meanwhile.
