## The Claiming Trade-Off in Numbers

*Hypothetical illustration.* For a worker whose full retirement age is 67 and whose primary insurance amount is $2,800 a month, claiming at 62 pays about $1,960 a month, claiming at 67 pays $2,800, and claiming at 70 pays $3,472, before cost-of-living adjustments.

These figures apply the early-claiming reduction (30% at age 62 for a full retirement age of 67) and the delayed retirement credit (8% for each year claiming is deferred past full retirement age, up to age 70) that the Social Security Administration publishes. They assume no earnings after claiming and ignore cost-of-living adjustments, income taxes and Medicare premiums. This illustrates the benefit formula; it is not investment performance and not a projection of your benefit, which depends on your own earnings record and the law when you claim. Waiting pays more each month but for fewer months: for this worker, the total collected by claiming at 70 does not overtake the total collected by claiming at 62 until about age 80. Health, other income, and a spouse's or survivor's benefit all bear on the decision.
