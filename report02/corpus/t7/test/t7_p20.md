## Tactical Duration: A Strategy We Are Preparing to Offer

Tactical Duration adjusts a portfolio's interest-rate sensitivity between two and eight years in response to the slope of the yield curve, inflation surprises and the ratio of municipal to Treasury yields. Applied to historical market data from 2006 through 2025, the Tactical Duration model would have returned 4.7% a year against 3.2% for the Index, beating it in 15 of 20 years. We expect to open Tactical Duration to separately managed accounts in the fourth quarter of 2026, with a minimum account size of $1 million.

## How We Calculate These Figures

The Intermediate Tax-Exempt Composite includes all fee-paying, discretionary accounts of $500,000 or more invested in the strategy for at least one full month. Composite inception is October 1, 2004. Returns are time-weighted, calculated monthly, asset-weighted across accounts, and include accrued interest and the reinvestment of income. Gross returns are after transaction costs but before advisory fees. Net returns reflect the actual advisory fees paid by each account, which ranged from 0.30% to 0.50% per year over the periods shown. As of June 30, 2026 the composite held $1.12 billion across 486 accounts. A description of every composite Marrowby Glen manages is available on request.

The Aysgarth 1-15 Year Municipal Bond Index is an unmanaged index of investment-grade, tax-exempt municipal bonds with maturities of one to fifteen years. It reflects no fees or transaction costs, holds bonds from all states, and has a duration that differs from the composite's. One cannot invest directly in an index.

## Risks

Municipal bonds are subject to interest-rate risk (prices fall when rates rise), credit risk, call and reinvestment risk, and liquidity risk; bonds sold before maturity may be worth less than their purchase price. A concentration in Virginia issuers increases exposure to economic and fiscal conditions in a single state. Some income may be subject to the federal alternative minimum tax or to state and local taxes, and a bond's tax-exempt status can be challenged after purchase.
