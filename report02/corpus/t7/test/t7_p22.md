Dear Friend,

For most of a working life, the question is how much to save. In retirement it becomes a harder one: how to turn balances spread across a 401(k), an IRA, a brokerage account and perhaps a pension into a monthly income that covers the bills and keeps taxes in check.

We invite you and a guest to a two-hour workshop on that question, designed for people within about ten years of retirement and those who have recently retired. Both sessions cover the same material.

## What We Will Cover

**1. When to claim Social Security.** Claiming at 62, at full retirement age or at 70 changes the size of every check for the rest of your life, and for married couples it changes the survivor benefit as well. We will explain delayed retirement credits and the tradeoff they involve: waiting means drawing more from savings in the meantime, and whether it pays off depends on how long you and your spouse live.

**2. Which account to draw from first.** The order in which you tap taxable, tax-deferred and Roth accounts affects your bracket, the taxation of your Social Security benefits and, two years later, your Medicare Part B and Part D premiums.

**3. The Four-Bucket income approach.** Tobias Carperby will explain how we divide a retirement portfolio into four pools: one to two years of spending in cash, three to five years in short- and intermediate-term bonds, a balanced middle bucket and a long-term growth bucket, with the nearer buckets refilled from the farther ones on a set schedule. No withdrawal approach removes market risk. A bucket can run down faster than planned if returns are poor early in retirement, and refilling the nearer buckets can mean selling investments at a loss or realizing taxable gains. Run against actual market returns from January 2000 through December 2025, our Four-Bucket Income Model would have produced an average annual return of 8.7% with no calendar year worse than -4.1%, including 2008.

**4. Roth conversions.** A Roth conversion can reduce future required minimum distributions and leave heirs assets they can generally withdraw tax-free, but it pulls income tax into the year of conversion, can raise Medicare premiums two years later, and is not right for everyone. We will show how to size one to stay within a chosen bracket.

**5. Medicare timing.** Enrollment windows and the penalties for missing them.
