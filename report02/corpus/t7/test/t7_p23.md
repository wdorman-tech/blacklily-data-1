## 1. Composite performance

For the periods ended June 30, 2026, the Intermediate Core Fixed Income Composite returned, net of fees, 5.34% for one year, 0.86% annualized for five years and 1.92% annualized for ten years; gross of fees, the same periods returned 5.68%, 1.18% and 2.25%. Over the same periods the Index returned 5.02%, 0.41% and 1.63%.

The five-year figure includes calendar 2022, when the composite lost 11.4% net of fees as yields rose sharply. Graham addressed this in the meeting, and it bears repeating in writing: an intermediate-duration portfolio has earned more income than a short-duration portfolio over most periods, but it carries more interest rate risk and will lose value when yields rise quickly. The strategy also holds investment-grade corporate and securitized bonds, which add credit, liquidity and prepayment risk that a Treasury-only portfolio would not carry.

The composite includes all fee-paying, discretionary, U.S. dollar intermediate core fixed income accounts without material restrictions; at June 30, 2026 it held 63 accounts with combined assets of $4.18 billion. Net returns deduct a model fee of 0.32% per year, the highest fee in our standard fixed income schedule, applied monthly. The Index is a market-value-weighted index of U.S. dollar investment-grade government, corporate and securitized bonds maturing in one to ten years; it does not reflect fees or expenses and cannot be invested in directly.

## 2. The 70/30 blend (hypothetical)

At the committee's request, we calculated hypothetical performance for a blend of 70% Intermediate Core and 30% Short Duration, rebalanced monthly, using each composite's actual net-of-fee monthly returns. On that basis, for the periods ended June 30, 2026, the blend returned 5.03% for one year, 1.20% annualized for five years and 1.84% annualized for ten years. Its worst calendar year was 2022, at -9.2%.

Please read these figures with their limits in mind. No account was managed as a 70/30 blend, so the figures do not reflect actual trading, the cost of moving assets between the strategies at each rebalance, cash flows into or out of the foundation's portfolio, or decisions a committee would have made along the way. The 70/30 weights were chosen this year, with knowledge of how both strategies performed, which introduces hindsight. Net figures reflect each composite's model fee (0.32% for Intermediate Core, 0.25% for Short Duration), not the fees proposed below. The foundation's actual results would have differed, possibly materially, and could have been lower. We prepared this analysis for the committee alone, in light of the foundation's stated objectives, 4.5% spending policy and liquidity needs, and ask that it not be circulated beyond the committee and its staff.

## 3. Proposed fees

For a mandate of approximately $85 million, we propose 0.28% per year on Intermediate Core assets and 0.20% per year on Short Duration assets, billed quarterly in arrears on average daily market value. There is no performance-based fee. Our fee schedule, and the conflicts of interest that arise from charging asset-based fees, are described in Item 5 of the attached Form ADV Part 2A.
