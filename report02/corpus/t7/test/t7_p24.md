## Investment Philosophy

We believe companies that earn high returns on invested capital, fund growth from internal cash flow and carry little debt can compound shareholder value over long periods, and that the market often underestimates how long those returns persist. We would rather own 30 such businesses we know well than 300 we know slightly. The strategy has outperformed the Index over every trailing period we have reported, in rising and falling markets alike.

## Investment Process

1. **Universe.** Roughly 1,000 U.S.-listed companies with market capitalizations above $5 billion.
2. **Quality screen.** Return on invested capital above 15% in at least five of the last seven fiscal years, net debt below 2.0 times EBITDA, and positive free cash flow in each of the last five years. About 220 companies typically pass.
3. **Fundamental research.** Analysts build five-year models, meet management and assess competitive position, capital allocation and accounting quality.
4. **Portfolio construction.** Positions of 2% to 6% at cost, a 30% cap on any one sector, and no more than 10% in companies below $10 billion in market capitalization.
5. **Sell discipline.** We sell when the thesis breaks, when valuation leaves little room for further return, or when a better-qualified candidate needs the capital.

## Key Risks

- **Concentration.** With 25 to 35 holdings, a setback at a single company can have a meaningful effect on results.
- **Style.** Quality growth can lag for extended periods when lower-quality or more cyclical stocks lead, as they did in the twelve months ended June 30, 2026, when the composite trailed the Index by 2.90 percentage points net of fees.
- **Sector.** The strategy has typically held more technology, health care and industrial companies than the Index and fewer financial, energy and utility companies, so sector moves can drive relative results.
- **Valuation.** High-quality companies often trade at premium valuations, and those premiums can contract even when business results are sound.
- **Market.** The strategy is fully invested in equities, and clients can lose money, including a substantial portion of principal, in a market decline.
