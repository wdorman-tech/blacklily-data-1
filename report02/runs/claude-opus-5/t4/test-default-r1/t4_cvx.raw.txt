**Chevron reported second quarter 2026 earnings of $12.1 billion on record U.S. upstream production and record U.S. refinery crude throughput, while cutting total debt by a record $8.4 billion in the quarter.**

## Results
- Reported earnings were $12.1 billion, or $6.11 per diluted share, versus $2,490 million and $1.45 per diluted share in 2Q25. Return on capital employed was 21.4 percent, against 6.2 percent a year ago.
- Adjusted earnings (non-GAAP) were $12.0 billion, or $6.06 per diluted share, compared with $3,053 million and $1.77 per diluted share in 2Q25. The quarter included an asset sale gain of $230 million and pension settlement costs of $86 million; foreign currency effects decreased earnings by $49 million.
- Sales and other operating revenues were $67,199 million versus $44,375 million in 2Q25. Depreciation, depletion and amortization was $6,082 million versus $4,344 million.
- Cash flow from operations was $22.6 billion; cash flow from operations excluding working capital (non-GAAP) was $19.7 billion. Free cash flow (non-GAAP) was $18.1 billion and adjusted free cash flow (non-GAAP) was $15.4 billion. Capex was $4.5 billion, with affiliate capex of $0.3 billion.
- Management says results included $1.4 billion in favorable timing effects, defined in the release as mark-to-market of financial derivatives before physical delivery plus LIFO inventory accounting.

## Segments and drivers
- Upstream earned $8,182 million ($3,541 million U.S.; $4,641 million international). Net oil-equivalent production was 4,070 MBOED, 20 percent above 2Q25, attributed to legacy Hess assets and growth in the Permian Basin and Gulf of America. U.S. liquids realization was $70.80/bbl; U.S. natural gas realization was $0.91/mcf versus $1.75 a year ago. Average Brent was $104/bbl versus $68.
- International production was reduced by curtailments in the Partitioned Zone due to the Middle East conflict.
- Downstream earned $4,868 million ($2,411 million U.S.; $2,457 million international) on higher refined product margins and higher Chevron Phillips Chemical earnings. U.S. crude unit inputs were a record 1,070 MBD at over 97 percent utilization.
- International refinery crude inputs fell 10 percent and refined product sales fell 13 percent versus 2Q25 on Middle East supply disruptions and weaker gasoline and diesel demand. U.S. refined product sales fell 4 percent on lower gasoline demand.

## Outlook
- The release provides no earnings, production or capex guidance. It notes Chevron publishes a separate quarterly "Sensitivities and Forward Guidance" document on its website.
- The only forward target stated: structural cost reductions of $3–4 billion versus 2024 levels by the end of 2026; $3 billion annual run-rate achieved, six months early. Hess-related synergies of $1.5 billion annual run-rate were achieved.

## Capital and balance sheet
- Total debt was $37,075 million at June 30, 2026 versus $40,758 million at December 31, 2025; net debt (non-GAAP) was $28,545 million. Net debt-to-CFFO (non-GAAP) was 0.6x.
- The quarter included $3,504 million of dividends and $3,117 million of share repurchases. A quarterly dividend of $1.78 per share was declared, payable September 10, 2026.

## Watch items
- How much of the earnings step-up is durable versus the stated $1.4 billion of favorable timing effects and the $2,945 million working capital inflow, both of which the release itself isolates.
- The U.S. natural gas realization of $0.91/mcf, down from $1.75 a year ago, alongside DD&A of $6,082 million — both cited as offsets to U.S. upstream earnings.
- Execution and terms of announced portfolio moves: the completed Hong Kong downstream sale, the Asia-Pacific downstream package expected to close in 2027, and the 2.67 GW, 20-year Microsoft power agreement, for which the release discloses no capital cost or earnings contribution.
