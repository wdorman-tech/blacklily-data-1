**What we did in the ladder.** Three things are worth reporting.

First, a rung matured in September and we reinvested the proceeds into a new three-year Treasury rung at a yield to maturity of 3.72%.

Yield to maturity reflects the yield at the time of purchase, assumes the security is held to maturity, and is not a forecast of the return you will receive.

Second, adding that longer rung lifted the ladder's duration to 1.7 years, up from 1.5 years at June 30. Duration is simply a measure of how much the value of the bonds moves when interest rates move. At under two years, the ladder stays short, which suits an account whose job is to fund your spending over the next two years.

Third, we cut agency bonds to 12% of the ladder, down from 20%. Many of those agency bonds can be called away early by the issuer, and the extra yield they were paying no longer seemed worth accepting that uncertainty. We put the proceeds into Treasury bills.
