**IBM reports second-quarter 2026 results with a focus on AI-driven growth and updated full-year expectations.**

## Results
*   Reported revenue for the second quarter of 2026 was $17.2 billion.
*   Reported net income for the second quarter of 2026 was $2.165 billion.
*   Reported gross profit for the second quarter of 2026 was $9.9 billion, resulting in a gross profit margin of 57.7%.
*   Operating (non-GAAP) revenue for the second quarter of 2026 was $10.2 billion, with an operating (non-GAAP) pre-tax income margin of 19.2%.

## Segments and drivers
*   Software revenue reached $7.8 billion in the second quarter, with Hybrid Cloud (Red Hat) up 11 percent and Data up 19 percent.
*   Consulting revenue was $5.3 billion for the second quarter, with Strategy and Technology and Intelligent Operations both up 1 percent at constant currency.
*   Infrastructure revenue was $3.8 billion for the second quarter, while Distributed Infrastructure grew by 37 percent.
*   Financing revenue for the second quarter was $0.2 billion, up 12 percent.

## Outlook
*   The company expects constant currency revenue growth in the range of four-to-five percent for the full year.
*   The company expects free cash flow to increase by about $1 billion year-over-year for the full year.
*   The company expects improved pre-tax income margin expansion for the full year.

## Capital and balance sheet
*   The company generated $2.5 billion in free cash flow for the second quarter, which was down $0.3 billion year-to-year.
*   The company ended the second quarter with $8.2 billion of cash, restricted cash, and marketable securities.
*   Total debt, including IBM Financing debt of $13.0 billion, was $62.0 billion as of the end of the second quarter.

## Watch items
*   The company reported a 7 percent decrease in Infrastructure revenue for the second quarter; the committee should monitor the impact of the "Power and Storage" order backlog of nearly $500 million on future growth.
*   The company invested $10.5 billion in acquisitions this year; the committee should monitor the integration of these assets and the associated "workforce rebalancing charges" noted in the non-GAAP reconciliations.
