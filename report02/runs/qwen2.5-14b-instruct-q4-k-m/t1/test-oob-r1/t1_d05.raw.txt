### 9. Service Providers

#### 9.1 Administrator
- **Wendlebury Fund Services, LLC** acts as the Fund's administrator, responsible for calculating the Net Asset Value (NAV), maintaining the register of Limited Partners, processing subscriptions and redemptions, and conducting anti-money laundering screening. The administrator relies on the Investment Manager for pricing of positions lacking readily available quotations.

#### 9.2 Prime Brokers, Clearing and Custody
- **Orrinshaw Securities LLC** and **Kestermoor Prime Services LLC** serve as prime brokers, providing custody, margin financing, securities borrowing, and clearing services. The Fund clears exchange-traded futures through **Pendlebrook Clearing LLC**, a futures commission merchant registered with the CFTC, which does not act as a prime broker to the Fund. Over-the-counter derivatives are entered into with dealer counterparties under ISDA master agreements.

#### 9.3 Independent Auditor
- **Marrable Oakshott LLP** acts as the Fund's independent auditor, having audited the Fund's financial statements for the period from April 1, 2025, through December 31, 2025, and will continue to audit the Fund's GAAP financial statements for subsequent fiscal years.

#### 9.4 Legal Counsel
- **Hollinrake Vaux LLP** acts as counsel to the Fund, the General Partner, and the Investment Manager in connection with the offering of Interests. The firm does not represent the Limited Partners, and no independent counsel has been engaged to represent them.

### 10. Side Letters
- The General Partner may enter into side letters with Limited Partners, granting terms different from or in addition to those described in the Memorandum, such as reduced Management Fees or Incentive Allocations, shorter notice periods, waivers of lock-ups, enhanced reporting, and capacity rights. The General Partner is not required to disclose side letter terms to other Limited Partners or offer them the same terms. The Fund does not offer a "most favored nation" election, meaning no Limited Partner is entitled to elect the benefit of terms granted to another Limited Partner.

### 11. Certain Risk Factors
- **Limited Operating History**: The Fund commenced operations on April 1, 2025, and the prior performance of Ms. Penhallow or of any portfolio manager is not indicative of future results.
- **Multi-Manager Platform Risk**: The Fund depends on the Investment Manager's ability to recruit and retain capable portfolio managers, whose rising compensation the Fund bears as Pass-Through Expenses. Portfolio managers may leave on short notice, and crowding among multi-manager firms in similar trades may cause correlated losses across Sub-Portfolios in periods of market stress.
- **Pass-Through Expenses**: Pass-Through Expenses are uncapped and depend on the performance of individual Sub-Portfolios, potentially causing the Fund to bear substantial compensation expense in unprofitable periods, and Pass-Through Expenses may exceed the Management Fee.
- **Leverage, Short Selling, and Derivatives**: The Fund uses substantial leverage through margin borrowing, repurchase agreements, and derivatives, which magnifies gains and losses. An increase in margin or haircut requirements could force the Fund to liquidate positions at disadvantageous prices. Short sales expose the Fund to theoretically unlimited loss, and fixed income relative value trades can incur large losses when spreads widen rather than converge.
- **Limited Liquidity of Interests**: Interests are illiquid, and no market for them is expected to develop. Redemptions are restricted by the lock-up, early redemption charge, notice, and gate provisions, and by the General Partner's suspension rights. Founders Class Interests carry the longest lock-up and notice period of any Class.
- **Counterparty, Valuation, and Operational Risk**: Assets held by the Prime Brokers, the futures commission merchant, and derivatives counterparties are subject to their credit risk, and rehypothecated assets may not be recoverable in full on an insolvency. Valuations of positions lacking quotations may differ from amounts ultimately realized, and systems failures or cyber intrusions could cause losses.

### 12. Conflicts of Interest
- **Allocation Between the Fund and the Parallel Fund**: The Investment Manager allocates investment opportunities between the Fund and the Parallel Fund pro rata to their net assets, except where legal, tax, or regulatory considerations make a different allocation appropriate, and may effect cross trades between them at independent market prices.
- **Differing Class Economics**: Because the Classes bear different Management Fee and Incentive Allocation rates, the Investment Manager and the General Partner earn more from Class A capital than from an equal amount of Class B or Founders Class capital, although all Classes participate pro rata in the same portfolio. Differences in lock-up and notice terms mean that Limited Partners of one Class may redeem earlier than others. Principals hold Founders Class Interests free of the Management Fee and the Incentive Allocation, which may affect the Investment Manager's liquidity decisions.
- **Pass-Through Expenses and Principal Transactions**: The Investment Manager determines the compensation of investment personnel that the Fund bears as Pass-Through Expenses, and therefore has an incentive to hire and retain personnel at the Fund's cost. The Investment Manager will engage in principal transactions with the Fund only in compliance with Section 206(3) of the Advisers Act, with any required consent obtained from an independent representative.

### 13. Certain Tax Considerations
- **Partnership Classification**: The Fund expects to be classified as a partnership and not as a publicly traded partnership taxable as a corporation, relying on the private placement safe harbor of Treasury Regulations Section 1.7704-1(h). Each Limited Partner must include its distributive share of the Fund's income, gain, loss, and deduction in its own return whether or not the Fund makes distributions, and the Fund does not intend to make distributions.
- **Character of Income and Deductions**: The Fund's trading will produce predominantly short-term capital gain and ordinary income. The Incentive Allocation may be subject to Section 1061 of the Internal Revenue Code of 1986, which treats certain long-term capital gain allocated in respect of an applicable partnership interest as short-term capital gain. If the Fund is not engaged in a trade or business, a non-corporate Limited Partner's share of the Management Fee may be a miscellaneous itemized deduction that is currently not deductible.
- **Tax-Exempt Investors and Partnership Audits**: Because the Fund uses leverage, a tax-exempt Limited Partner will likely realize unrelated business taxable income. Under the partnership audit regime of the Bipartisan Budget Act of 2015, an adjustment is generally assessed against the Fund unless it elects to push the adjustment out to the reviewed-year partners; the General Partner is the partnership representative.

### 14. ERISA Considerations
- **Plan Asset Limitation**: The General Partner intends to limit investment in each Class by benefit plan investors to less than 25% of the value of that Class (disregarding Interests held by the General Partner, the Investment Manager, and their affiliates), so that the Fund's assets are not plan assets under 29 C.F.R. Section 2510.3-101, as modified by Section 3(42) of ERISA, and may refuse subscriptions or compel redemptions for that purpose. Fiduciaries of plans subject to ERISA or Section 4975 of the Code should consider prudence, diversification, the illiquidity of Interests, and the prohibited transaction rules.

### 15. Valuation, Reports, and Amendments
- **Net Asset Value and Reports**: The Administrator determines the NAV of the Fund and of each Capital Account as of the last Business Day of each month under valuation policies adopted by the General Partner. Limited Partners receive monthly Capital Account statements, a quarterly letter, and audited annual financial statements within 120 days after each fiscal year end.
- **Amendments**: The General Partner may amend the Partnership Agreement without the consent of the Limited Partners, except that an amendment that adversely changes the Management Fee rate, Incentive Allocation rate, lock-up period, or redemption rights of a Class requires either the consent of a majority in interest of that Class or written notice at least 30 days before it takes effect, together with the right to redeem before it takes effect free of any lock-up, early redemption charge, and the Investor-Level Gate.
