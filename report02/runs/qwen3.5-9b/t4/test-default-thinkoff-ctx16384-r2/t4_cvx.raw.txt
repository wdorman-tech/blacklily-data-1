**Chevron Reports Record Second Quarter 2026 Earnings Driven by U.S. Production and Hess Synergies**

## Results
*   Reported earnings totaled $12.1 billion, or $6.11 per share diluted, for the second quarter of 2026.
*   Adjusted earnings were $12.0 billion, or $6.06 per share diluted, excluding an asset sale gain of $230 million and pension settlement costs of $86 million.
*   Foreign currency effects decreased earnings by $49 million during the quarter.
*   Cash flow from operations reached $22.6 billion, while free cash flow was $18.1 billion for the three months ended June 30, 2026.

## Segments and drivers
*   Upstream earnings of $8.18 billion were bolstered by record U.S. production and higher liquids realizations, though partially offset by increased depreciation, depletion, and amortization.
*   Downstream earnings of $4.87 billion reflected higher margins on refined product sales in the United States, while international downstream results included an asset sale gain and favorable foreign currency effects.
*   All Other segments recorded net charges of $978 million, driven by higher employee benefit costs and interest expense, which were offset by the absence of a prior-year unfavorable fair value adjustment on Hess shares.

## Outlook
*   The company achieved its structural cost reduction target six months early, capturing $3 billion in annual run-rate savings.
*   Chevron delivered $1.5 billion of annual run-rate synergies related to the Hess Corporation acquisition within one year of closing, exceeding the initial target by 50 percent.
*   The firm signed a 20-year power purchase agreement with Microsoft to provide 2.67 gigawatts of behind-the-meter power for a data center in West Texas.

## Capital and balance sheet
*   Total debt was reduced by a record $8.4 billion during the quarter, further strengthening the company's balance sheet.
*   The net debt-to-CFFO ratio stood at 0.6x for the trailing four quarters as of June 30, 2026.
*   Capital expenditures for the quarter were $4.5 billion, with higher spend on legacy Hess assets partially offset by lower spend in the Permian Basin.

## Watch items
*   Monitor the impact of supply disruptions from the Middle East conflict on international downstream refinery crude unit inputs, which decreased 10 percent from the year-ago period due to these events.
*   Track the progress of the planned sale of the company's 50 percent interest in the Singapore Refining Company and other downstream assets in Singapore, Vietnam, Australia, Indonesia, the Philippines, and Malaysia, expected to close in 2027.
*   Observe production curtailments in the Partitioned Zone between Saudi Arabia and Kuwait due to the ongoing Middle East conflict, which affected international upstream net oil-equivalent production growth.
