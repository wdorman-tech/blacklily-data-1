**JPMorgan Chase Reports Record Second-Quarter 2026 Net Income of $21.2 Billion Driven by Strong Markets and Investment Banking Performance**

## Results
*   The Firm reported second-quarter 2026 net income of $21.2 billion, or $7.70 per share, representing a 41% increase compared to the prior-year quarter.
*   Net income excluding significant items was $16.9 billion, or $6.14 per share, reflecting a 13% increase year over year.
*   Reported revenue reached $57.3 billion, while managed revenue totaled $58.0 billion, with expenses at $27.3 billion resulting in a reported overhead ratio of 48%.
*   Credit costs were $2.5 billion, comprising $2.4 billion of net charge-offs and a $149 million net reserve build.

## Segments and drivers
*   Commercial & Investment Banking (CIB) revenue grew 27% year over year to $24.9 billion, propelled by a 30% increase in Investment Banking fees and a 35% rise in Markets revenue.
*   Consumer & Community Banking (CCB) net income increased 3% to $5.3 billion, supported by an 8% revenue growth where Card Services net charge-off rates stood at 3.34%.
*   Asset & Wealth Management (AWM) generated $6.9 billion in revenue, an 18% year-over-year increase, driven by Assets Under Management reaching $5.1 trillion and first-time investors hitting a record of nearly 44,000.

## Outlook
*   Chairman Jamie Dimon noted that while the U.S. economy has shown resiliency supported by AI-driven capital investment and fiscal stimulus, several risks are shifting below the surface including geopolitical tensions, sticky inflation, large global fiscal deficits, and elevated asset prices.
*   Management stated it cannot predict how these forces will ultimately play out but emphasized careful monitoring of risks to prepare for a wide range of scenarios.
*   The release contains no specific numerical guidance or outlook for future quarters; the firm explicitly states that actual results may differ materially from forward-looking statements due to significant risks and uncertainties.

## Capital and balance sheet
*   CET1 Capital Ratios were 14.1% under the Standardized approach and 14.2% under the Advanced approach, with Total Loss-Absorbing Capacity at $590 billion.
*   Book value per share increased 9% year over year to $133.01, while tangible book value per share rose 10% to $113.35.
*   Capital distributions included a common dividend of $4.0 billion and $6.2 billion of common stock net repurchases, resulting in a net payout of 73% for the last twelve months.

## Watch items
*   Monitor the impact of "significant items" on reported earnings, specifically the $4.6 billion net gain related to Visa shares and $1.0 billion of gains on certain equity investments which collectively reduced adjusted net income by $4.2 billion after tax.
*   Track the shift in risk factors mentioned by leadership, particularly how geopolitical tensions, sticky inflation, and elevated asset prices could cause meaningful disruptions given the firm's exposure to large global fiscal deficits.
