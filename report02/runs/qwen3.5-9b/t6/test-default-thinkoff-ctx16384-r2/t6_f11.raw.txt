Dear Dr. Ferreira and Dr. Lindqvist,

Please accept this letter as your quarterly update for the International Developed Quality Equity strategy covering the period from April 1 through June 30, 2026.

During the second quarter of 2026, the portfolio returned -1.9% net of fees. For comparison, the gross return before advisory fees was -1.7%. This performance outperformed the developed markets ex-US equity index (net dividends), which declined by -3.2% over the same period. On a year-to-date basis through June 30, 2026, the account has returned 4.6% net of fees. Since the strategy's inception on May 3, 2021, the annualized return net of fees stands at 6.3%.

Our portfolio construction focused on navigating a challenging global environment while maintaining our long-term objectives. We took advantage of market volatility in April by halving our positions in Nordic banks during that rally. Consequently, financials now represent 14.5% of the portfolio, down from 19.0% at the end of March. We also added to our Japan exposure following a sell-off in May; Japan is now the largest country weight within the portfolio at 23.5%. Cash holdings stood at 4.4% at quarter-end and are scheduled for redeployment throughout July. The number of holdings remains unchanged at 38 names.

We are pleased to inform you that we have adjusted our advisory fee structure effective October 1, 2026. This change applies a new rate of 0.80% per year on the first $2,000,000 of household assets under management, reducing the previous rate of 0.90% per year. Assets above $2,000,000 will continue to be charged at 0.65% per year. This reduction is part of a firm-wide repricing of our international equity schedule approved by our partners in June. As this is a benefit to you, no notice period was required and no action is needed on your part; the new rate will first appear on your Q4 2026 invoice, billed quarterly in advance based on the September 30, 2026 value.

International investing involves risks not present in domestic markets, including currency fluctuation, political and economic instability, and differences in accounting and reporting standards, and these risks may be heightened in periods of market stress.
