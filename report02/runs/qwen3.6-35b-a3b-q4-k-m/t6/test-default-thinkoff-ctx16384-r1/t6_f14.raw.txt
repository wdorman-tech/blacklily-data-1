Dear Mrs. Delacourt and Mr. Ambrose,

I hope this letter finds you well as we close the first quarter of 2026. It has been a pleasure serving the Delacourt-Ambrose Charitable Remainder Unitrust since its inception in 2017, and I am writing to share our progress for the period ending March 31, 2026.

During Q1, the trust’s income-oriented balanced strategy, which maintains a target allocation of 55% global equity and 45% fixed income, delivered a net return of 2.2%. For your reference, the gross-of-fees return was 2.4%. This performance compares favorably to our benchmark index, which returned 1.8% over the same period. The year-to-date return through March 31 is also 2.2%, and the annualized return since inception remains strong at 6.8%.

We have taken specific steps to ensure liquidity for your charitable goals. On March 31, we processed the first unitrust payment of $31,250. This amount represents a 5% distribution based on the January 2 valuation of $2,500,000, structured as four equal quarterly installments. To support future obligations, we have increased our holdings in cash and Treasury bills to 6.5% of trust assets. This position pre-funds the payments due in June and September, allowing us to avoid selling risk assets during this period.

In terms of portfolio management, we adjusted our fixed income allocation by trimming high-yield bonds from 7.5% down to 3.5%. We made this decision as credit spreads have tightened to their narrowest levels since 2021, suggesting limited additional upside for that segment.

I am also pleased to inform you of a fee adjustment benefiting the trust. Effective June 1, 2026, the advisory fee on the first $3,000,000 of trust assets will decrease from 1.00% per year to 0.90% per year. This change aligns your account with our standard individual schedule and will be prorated on your Q2 invoice. Assets above this threshold remain unchanged at 0.70% per year, though that level has not yet been reached.

Unitrust payments are set by the trust instrument as a fixed percentage of the trust's net assets as revalued each year, will rise or fall with the value of the trust, and are not a measure of investment performance.

Thank you for your continued trust in our guidance. Please do not hesitate to reach out if you have any questions regarding this report or the upcoming fee change.

Warm regards,

Marguerite Akintola-Reyes, CFP, CTFA
Senior Wealth Advisor
