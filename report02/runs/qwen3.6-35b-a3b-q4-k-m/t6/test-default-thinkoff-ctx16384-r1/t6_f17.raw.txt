I hope this quarter found you well. I am writing to share the performance update for your Real Assets sleeve within joint taxable account x6190 for the period ending September 30, 2026.

During Q3 2026, your sleeve returned 2.9% net of fees. For context, the gross-of-fees return was 3.1%. This compares to a 3.4% return for our real assets policy blend index, which is weighted 40% global listed infrastructure, 30% global REITs, 20% US TIPS, and 10% broad commodities. Year-to-date through September 30, 2026, the sleeve returned 6.1% net of fees. The trailing twelve-month return through September 30, 2026, stands at 8.7% net of fees.

We made several adjustments to the portfolio during this period. We increased our allocation to listed infrastructure from 38% to 44%, focusing primarily on towers and natural gas pipelines. We also initiated a new position in 2034 Treasury Inflation-Protected Securities (TIPS), purchased at a real yield of 1.95%. Additionally, following the recent rally in gold prices, we reduced our commodities allocation to 5% of the sleeve. This brings us back within our policy range of 4% to 12%.

I also want to inform you of a fee adjustment specific to this Real Assets sleeve. Effective December 1, 2026, the advisory fee for this sleeve will increase from 0.60% per year to 0.75% per year. This change reflects a shift in how we manage TIPS and commodity exposure. We are moving from using exchange-traded funds (ETFs) to directly held TIPS and exchange-traded futures managed by our firm. While the advisory fee increases, the weighted fund expenses within the sleeve will decrease significantly from 0.14% to 0.03% as we sell the ETFs. This reduction in underlying costs offsets much of the increase in our advisory fee. Please note that fees for your core equity and bond holdings remain unchanged. This letter serves as the required 30 days’ written notice under Section 7 of our advisory agreement.

Real asset investments, including commodities, real estate securities and inflation-linked bonds, can be affected by changes in interest rates, inflation, supply and demand, and regulation, and may not keep pace with inflation in every period.
