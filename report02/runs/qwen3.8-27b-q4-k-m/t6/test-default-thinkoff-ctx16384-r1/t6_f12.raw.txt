Dear Everett and Lucia,

I hope this letter finds you both well. I am writing to share the results for your Liquidity Reserve account for the third quarter of 2026. This account is designed to hold your cash reserves safely while providing steady income, so I want to keep this update focused on yield and the safety of your principal.

For the quarter ending September 30, 2026, your account returned 1.3% net of fees. For context, the gross return before fees was 1.4%. This performance compares favorably to the 1-3 year US Treasury index, which returned 1.2% over the same period. Year to date, your net return stands at 3.6%, and the trailing twelve-month net return is 4.7%.

During the quarter, we made a few adjustments to optimize your ladder. In September, a maturing security was rolled into a new three-year Treasury rung with a yield to maturity of 3.72%. This move increased the ladder’s duration to 1.7 years, up from 1.5 years at the end of June. Additionally, we reduced our allocation to agency securities from 20% to 12% of the ladder. We made this change because the spreads on callable agency securities were too tight to justify the associated call risk. We moved the proceeds from these sales into Treasury bills to maintain liquidity and safety.

I am also pleased to inform you of a fee reduction. Effective November 1, 2026, the advisory fee for all assets in your Liquidity Reserve account will decrease from 0.40% per year to 0.30% per year. This change applies to every rung in the ladder as well as any cash held in the account. The new rate will be prorated on your fourth-quarter invoice. Please note that this change is specific to the Liquidity Reserve strategy; the fee for your equity account remains unchanged.

As always, our goal is to keep your funds accessible and secure for your upcoming spending needs. If you have any questions about these changes or your current yields, please do not hesitate to reach out.

Yield to maturity reflects the yield at the time of purchase, assumes the security is held to maturity, and is not a forecast of the return you will receive.
